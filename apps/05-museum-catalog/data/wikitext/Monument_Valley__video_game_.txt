'''''Monument Valley''''' is a 2014 [[Puzzle video game|puzzle]] and [[indie game]] by [[Ustwo|Ustwo Games]]. The player leads the princess Ida through mazes of [[optical illusion]]s and [[impossible object]]s while manipulating the world around her to reach various platforms. ''Monument Valley'' was developed over ten months beginning in early 2013 based on concept drawings by company artist Ken Wong. Its visual style was inspired by [[Japanese prints]], [[Minimalism (visual arts)|minimalist sculpture]], and [[indie games]] ''[[Windosill]]'', ''[[Fez (video game)|Fez]]'', and ''[[Superbrothers: Sword & Sworcery EP|Sword & Sworcery]]'', and was compared by critics to [[M. C. Escher]] drawings and ''[[Echochrome]]''.

After a closed [[video game beta|beta]] test, ''Monument Valley'' was released for [[iOS]] on April 3, 2014, and was later [[Porting#Porting in gaming|ported]] to [[Android (operating system)|Android]] and [[Windows Phone]]. The game received generally favorable reviews. Critics praised its art and sound design, but noted its lack of difficulty and short length. The game is cited as one of the [[List of mobile games listed among the best|greatest mobile video games ever made]]. It won a 2014 [[Apple Design Award]], was named Apple's best iPad game of 2014, and sold over two million copies by January 2015; by May 2016, sales of the game exceeded 26 million.

Two expansions titled ''Forgotten Shores'' and ''Ida's (RED) Dream'' were released in 2014 and 2015, respectively. A sequel, ''[[Monument Valley 2]]'', was released for both iOS and Android in 2017.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Sulleyman |first=Aatif |date=June 5, 2017 |title=Monument Valley 2 has just been revealed |work=The Independent |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/life-style/gadgets-and-tech/news/monument-valley-2-wwdc-2017-iphone-android-games-announcement-latest-a7774201.html |access-date=June 5, 2017 |archive-date=December 21, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171221215054/http://www.independent.co.uk/life-style/gadgets-and-tech/news/monument-valley-2-wwdc-2017-iphone-android-games-announcement-latest-a7774201.html |url-status=live }}</ref> ''Monument Valley+'', an [[Apple Arcade]] exclusive, was released April 2, 2021.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://m.igdb.com/games/monument-valley-plus |title=Monument Valley+ at the Internet Game Database (IGDB |access-date=November 28, 2022 |archive-date=November 28, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221128193522/https://m.igdb.com/games/monument-valley-plus |url-status=live }}</ref> A PC version under the title ''Monument Valley: Panoramic Edition'' was released for [[Microsoft Windows]] in July 2022 and the game was later also released for [[Nintendo Switch]], [[PlayStation 4]], [[PlayStation 5]], [[Xbox One]] and [[Xbox Series X and Series S]] in April 2025. A third game, ''[[Monument Valley 3]]'', was released in December 2024.

== Gameplay ==
[[File:Monument Valley screenshot.jpeg|thumb|left|150px|A screenshot of part of a level in ''Monument Valley'']]

In ''Monument Valley'', [[player character]] Princess<ref name="Creative Review" /> Ida journeys through mazes of [[optical illusions]] and [[impossible objects]],<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> which are referred to as "[[sacred geometry]]" in-game, as she journeys to be forgiven for something.<ref name="Indiegames.com: trailer" /> The game is presented in [[isometric view]],<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> and the player interacts with the environment to find hidden passages as Ida progresses to the map's exit.<ref name="Edge: revealed" /> Each of the ten<ref name="Edge review" /> [[Level (video gaming)|levels]] has a different central mechanic.<ref name="Edge: revealed" /> Interactions include moving platforms and pillars and creating bridges.<ref name="Polygon: intro" /> The player is indirectly cued through the game by design elements like color, and directly cued by crow people, who block Ida's path. Critics compared the game's visual style to a vibrant [[M. C. Escher]] drawing<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> and ''[[Echochrome]]''.<ref name="Edge: revealed" /> The game includes a camera mode where the player can roam the level to compose screenshots. It includes filters similar to those of [[Instagram]].<ref name="Polygon: everyone can" />

== Development ==
''Monument Valley'' was developed by Ustwo, a digital design firm founded in 2004 that has produced iPhone apps since 2007. Their ''[[Whale Trail]]'' game received millions of downloads, and their other apps include design app Granimator and photo sharing app Rando.<ref name="Creative Review" /> ''Monument Valley'' was conceived as a touch game for [[Tablet computer|tablets]]. Its development began in the beginning of 2013,<ref name="Indiegames.com: trailer" /> and lasted 10 months. It was developed under the working title, Tower of Illusion.<ref>{{Cite news |date=June 15, 2017 |title=Monument Valley was Almost Called "The Garden of Geometric Delights" |publisher=Grab It Magazine |url=http://www.grabitmagazine.com/blog/post/monument-valley-alternate-name/ |access-date=June 15, 2017 |archive-date=June 17, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170617130940/http://www.grabitmagazine.com/blog/post/monument-valley-alternate-name/ |url-status=live }}</ref> It began with a piece of concept art drawn in the style of [[M. C. Escher]], and the final design did not deviate far from this original.<ref name="Polygon: everyone can" /> Ustwo management did not give the development team a timeline or budget, and instead told them to focus on "making a high-quality product". Games development is not a large portion of Ustwo's revenue, so the company focuses its games development on producing "great products" that reflect well on the company, rather than highly profitable apps.<ref name="Gamasutra: postmortem" />

[[File:Ken_wong_-_game_developers_conference_cropped.jpg|thumb|right|upright|Ken Wong, the lead designer for Ustwo, at the 2015 [[Game Developers Conference]]]]
Of the art style, game designer and artist Ken Wong said he aspired to make each frame of the gameplay worthy of public display.<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> The project began as Wong's concept drawings before it became a game project.<ref name="Edge: revealed" /> The visual style was inspired by [[Japanese prints]], [[Minimalism (visual arts)|minimalist sculpture]],<ref name="Creative Review" /> and [[indie games]] ''[[Windosill]]'', ''[[Fez (video game)|Fez]]'', and ''[[Superbrothers: Sword & Sworcery EP]]''.<ref name="Indiegames.com: trailer" /> Wong added that the gameplay was designed to let the player find the object of the game through exploration, without direct guidance. The game uses colors to signify where the player can interact, similar to ''[[Mirror's Edge]]''.<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> Wong compared the gameplay experience to a cross between the wonder of a toy shop and the world of ''[[The Lion, the Witch, and the Wardrobe]]'',<ref name="Creative Review" /> and its story to a symbolic "song" rather than a narrative book.<ref name="Polygon: intro" /> The game was designed to be completed by most players, a style uncommon for games designed for popular audiences.<ref name="Polygon: everyone can" /> It was intended to be a "premium experience" rather than a difficult challenge.<ref name="Gamasutra: postmortem" />

The game's soundtrack features music by Stafford Bawler, [[Obfusc (musician)|Obfusc]], and Grigori. A limited edition [[vinyl record|vinyl]] two-volume recording was released by Ustwo and [[iam8bit]] in 2016, which includes the music from the main game and its two expansions.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Crecente |first=Brian |date=January 26, 2016 |title=Monument Valley Music Gets a Surprise Vinyl, Pre-orders Open Tomorrow |url=http://www.polygon.com/2016/1/26/10832622/monument-valley-soundtrack-vinyl |access-date=January 26, 2016 |website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] |archive-date=January 26, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160126190808/http://www.polygon.com/2016/1/26/10832622/monument-valley-soundtrack-vinyl |url-status=live }}</ref>

The game was in [[video game beta|beta]] as of December 2013,<ref name="Creative Review" /><ref name="Polygon: intro" /> where it had over 1,000 testers and the average finish time was 90 minutes.<ref name="Polygon: everyone can" /> It was planned as an iPad exclusive.<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> The game was initially released for [[iOS]] on April 3, 2014,<ref name="Polygon: release" /> and the company recouped their cost of development within the first two weeks of release. The game received "generally favorable" reviews, according to video game review score aggregator [[Metacritic]].<ref name="Metacritic" /> It immediately went to the top of the App Store paid apps chart, where it stayed for at least a month based on strong reviews and [[word of mouth]]. It was chosen as an App Store Editor's Choice<ref name="Edge: next" /> and later both received a 2014 [[Apple Design Award]]<ref name="Polygon: Apple Design Award" /> and was named Apple's best iPad game of 2014.<ref name="Guardian: 2014" /> ''[[Pocket Gamer]]'' gave the game their gold award, and their Harry Slater wrote that there was "nothing else like ''Monument Valley'' on the App Store".<ref name="Pocket Gamer review" />

Multiple reviewers cited the art<ref name="GI review" /><ref name="Polygon review" /><ref name="Pocket Gamer review" /><ref name="TouchArcade review" /> and sound design as exceptional.<ref name="Edge review" /><ref name="Polygon review" /><ref name="TouchArcade review" /> ''[[Edge (magazine)|Edge]]'' added that the sound design's addition of "deep rumbles" and environmental "clicks" gave the game the feeling of ''[[Tomb Raider]]''{{'s}} moving "ancient mechanisms".<ref name="Edge review" /> ''TouchArcade''{{'s}} Shaun Musgrave called the visuals "almost impossibly gorgeous",<ref name="TouchArcade review" /> and ''[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]]'' wrote that the game "might be the most beautiful iPad game of 2014".<ref name="Wired: gorgeous" /> ''[[Creative Review]]'' called the game's puzzles "clever" and appreciated the developers' attention to detail.<ref name="Creative Review" />

While ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]''{{'s}} Danielle Riendeau praised the puzzle design,<ref name="Polygon review" /> other critics noted its lack of difficulty<ref name="Edge review" /><ref name="GI review" /> and short length.<ref name="GI review" /><ref name="Polygon review" /><ref name="Pocket Gamer review" /><ref name="TouchArcade review" /> Riendeau wrote that the puzzles solutions always felt intuitive and never felt frustrating. She found "everything" about the game to be "soothing, almost hypnotic".<ref name="Polygon review" /> ''Edge'' thought that the game did not present a "genuine" challenge, and that the puzzles did not "find fresh ways to confound and delight" until the final levels.<ref name="Edge review" /> The magazine compared the game to "assembling flat-pack furniture": straightforward in its process, but rewarding in its transformation.<ref name="Edge review" /> Harry Slater of ''Pocket Gamer'' wrote that the game was "almost breathtakingly unique".<ref name="Pocket Gamer review" /> Riendeau said she finished the game in under three hours,<ref name="Polygon review" /> and ''TouchArcade''{{'s}} Shaun Musgrave, who could not "even begin to imagine anyone getting stuck on ... the puzzles", put the game's length at "an hour and change".<ref name="TouchArcade review" /> Jeff Marchiafava of ''[[Game Informer]]'' wanted more content. He was also unsatisfied with the game's narrative, which he found "obtuse" and "vague" to a fault.<ref name="GI review" /> Musgrave of ''TouchArcade'' thought that while games that focus on experiences can be shorter, ''Monument Valley'' did not last long enough "for the mechanics to reach their full potential", though the time was "just about right" for the story.<ref name="TouchArcade review" />

== Legacy ==

== Film adaptation ==
In August 2018, [[Paramount Animation]] and [[Weed Road]], in partnership with Ustwo, announced plans to develop a live action/CGI hybrid film based on ''Monument Valley'', with [[Patrick Osborne (animator)|Patrick Osborne]] slated to direct. It was anticipated that the film would feature live actors exploring the computer generated settings based on the game. Osborne stated "I'm privileged to be handed the reins to Ida's mysterious kingdom, to play in her world of impossible architecture where seeing things differently is everything".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |date=August 22, 2018 |title=Paramount, Weed Road Tap Patrick Osborne For 'Monument Valley' Mobile Game Movie |url=https://deadline.com/2018/08/monument-valley-ustwo-games-movie-patrick-osborne-paramount-pictures-akiva-goldsman-1202446137/ |access-date=August 22, 2018 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |archive-date=August 22, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180822003513/https://deadline.com/2018/08/monument-valley-ustwo-games-movie-patrick-osborne-paramount-pictures-akiva-goldsman-1202446137/ |url-status=live }}</ref>
