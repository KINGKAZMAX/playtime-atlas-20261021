'''''2048''''' is a single-player [[sliding puzzle|sliding tile puzzle]] video game written by Italian [[web developer]] Gabriele Cirulli and published on [[GitHub]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://github.com/gabrielecirulli/2048 | title=2048 | website=[[GitHub]] }}</ref> The objective of the game is to slide numbered tiles on a grid to combine them to create a tile with the number 2048; however, one can continue to play the game after reaching the goal, creating tiles with larger numbers. It was originally written in [[JavaScript]] and [[CSS]] over a weekend, and released on 9 March 2014 as [[free and open-source software]] subject to the [[MIT License]]. Versions for [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] followed in May 2014.

''2048'' was intended to be an improved version of two other games, both of which were clones of the iOS game ''[[Threes]]'' released a month earlier. Cirulli himself described ''2048'' as being "conceptually similar" to ''Threes''.<ref name=Softonic/> The release of ''2048'' resulted in the rapid appearance of many similar games, akin to the flood of ''[[Flappy Bird]]'' variations from 2013. The game received generally positive reviews from critics, with it being described as "viral" and "addictive".

== Gameplay ==
[[File:2048 win.png|thumb|upright|A completed game. The 2048 tile is in the bottom-right corner.]]
''2048'' is played on a plain 4×4 grid, with numbered tiles that slide when a player moves them using the four [[arrow keys]].<ref name=spence /> The game begins with two tiles already in the grid, having a value of either 2 or 4, and another such tile appears in a random empty space after each turn.<ref name=times/> Tiles with a value of 2 appear 90% of the time, and tiles with a value of 4 appear 10% of the time.<ref name=gamecode /> Tiles slide as far as possible in the chosen direction until they are stopped by either another tile or the edge of the grid. If two tiles of the same number collide while moving, they will merge into a tile with the total value of the two tiles that collided.<ref name=anonymous /><ref name=eat /> The resulting tile cannot merge with another tile again in the same move. Higher-scoring tiles emit a soft glow;<ref name=times/> the largest possible tile is 131,072.<ref>{{Cite journal|last=Neller|first=Todd W.|title=Pedagogical Possibilities for the 2048 Puzzle Game|pages=38–46 [39]|url=https://cupola.gettysburg.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1025&context=csfac|journal=Journal of Computing Sciences in Colleges|volume=30|issue=3|date=2015|access-date=28 January 2021|archive-date=9 July 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200709094718/https://cupola.gettysburg.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1025&context=csfac|url-status=live}}</ref>

If a move causes three consecutive tiles of the same value to slide together, only the two tiles farthest along the direction of motion will combine. If all four spaces in a row or column are filled with tiles of the same value, a move parallel to that row/column will combine the first two and last two.<ref>{{cite news |title=This hyper-addictive math game is driving the Internet insane |url=https://www.dailydot.com/debug/2048-math-game/ |first=Rob |last=Price |date=March 2, 2020 |work=[[Daily Dot]] |access-date=January 25, 2021 |archive-date=16 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200716130246/https://www.dailydot.com/debug/2048-math-game/ |url-status=live }}</ref> A scoreboard on the upper-right keeps track of the user's score. The user's score starts at zero, and is increased whenever two tiles combine, by the value of the new tile.<ref name=times/>

The game is won when a tile with a value of 2048 appears on the board. Players can continue beyond that to reach higher scores.<ref name=CNBC>{{cite news | title=19-Year-Old Makes Viral Game Hit in a Weekend | url=https://www.cnbc.com/2014/03/26/19-year-old-makes-viral-game-hit-in-a-weekend.html | first=Arjun | last=Kharpal | date=26 March 2014 | publisher=[[CNBC]] | access-date=8 April 2014 | archive-date=13 September 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200913121341/https://www.cnbc.com/2014/03/26/19-year-old-makes-viral-game-hit-in-a-weekend.html | url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=wapo>{{cite news |title=Everything you ever wanted to know about 2048, the Internet's latest impossible hit game |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/arts-and-entertainment/wp/2014/04/23/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-2048-the-internets-latest-impossible-hit-game/ |first=Caitlin |last=Dewey |date=April 23, 2014 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |access-date=January 25, 2021 |archive-date=26 April 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160426154543/https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/arts-and-entertainment/wp/2014/04/23/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-2048-the-internets-latest-impossible-hit-game/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=2048 starts easy; gets hard. Here's how to make it easy again |url=https://www.cnet.com/news/2048-starts-easy-gets-hard-heres-how-to-make-it-easy-again/ |first=Nick |last=Statt |date=March 22, 2014 |publisher=CNet |access-date=January 25, 2021 |archive-date=27 January 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160127014126/https://www.cnet.com/news/2048-starts-easy-gets-hard-heres-how-to-make-it-easy-again/ |url-status=live }}</ref> When the player has no legal moves (there are no empty spaces and no adjacent tiles with the same value), the game ends.<ref name=Softonic /><ref>{{cite news |title=How to play '2048,' the addicting smartphone game Travis Kalanick is obsessed with |url=https://www.businessinsider.com/how-to-play-2048-smartphone-game-travis-kalanick-2018-1 |first=Avery |last=Hartmans |date=January 18, 2018 |work=[[Business Insider]] |access-date=January 25, 2021 |archive-date=30 January 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210130021357/https://www.businessinsider.com/how-to-play-2048-smartphone-game-travis-kalanick-2018-1 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Strategy===
Strategies in ''2048'' include keeping the largest tile in a specific corner with other large tiles filling the row (either vertically or horizontally). If the row remains filled, the player can then move in three different directions while still keeping the largest tile in the preferred corner. As a general rule, no small tiles should be separated from other small tiles by a large tile.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Johnston |first1=Stephen |title=2048 Game Strategy - How to Always Win at 2048 |url=https://www.gameskinny.com/lnagr/2048-game-strategy-how-to-always-win-at-2048 |website=www.gameskinny.com |access-date=8 February 2022 |date=7 December 2021 |archive-date=8 February 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220208170550/https://www.gameskinny.com/lnagr/2048-game-strategy-how-to-always-win-at-2048 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Six tips and tricks to help you achieve your highest score in 2048! |url=https://www.imore.com/2048-tips-and-tricks |website=iMore |access-date=8 February 2022 |date=13 July 2018 |archive-date=8 February 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220208170549/https://www.imore.com/2048-tips-and-tricks |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Reception ==
The game has been described by ''The Wall Street Journal'' as "almost like ''[[Candy Crush]]'' for math geeks",<ref name="anonymous">{{cite news|title=Want to Stay Anonymous? Don't Make a Hit Computer Game|url=https://blogs.wsj.com/digits/2014/03/18/want-to-stay-anonymous-dont-make-a-hit-computer-game/|first=Ed|last=Ballard|newspaper=[[The Wall Street Journal]]|date=18 March 2014|access-date=4 August 2017|archive-date=19 March 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140319233352/https://blogs.wsj.com/digits/2014/03/18/want-to-stay-anonymous-dont-make-a-hit-computer-game/|url-status=live}}</ref> and ''Business Insider'' called it "''Threes'' on steroids".<ref name="insider" /> Caitlin Dewey of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' called it "a nerdy, minimalist, frustrating game",<ref name="wapo"/> while ''[[The Independent]]'' called it "addictive".<ref name=Independent/> The phenomenon of ''2048'' has been compared to that of ''Flappy Bird'' by several commentators. James Vincent of ''The Independent'' labeled ''2048'' as "a clone of a clone".<ref name=Independent/> In April 2014, ''[[Pocket Gamer]]'' reported that 15 new clones of ''Threes'' were released daily in the App Store.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Brown |first1=Mark |title=There are 15 new Threes! / 2048 clones released on the App Store every day |url=https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/058810/there-are-15-new-threes-2048-clones-released-on-the-app-store-every-day/ |website=Pocket Gamer |date=April 7, 2014 |access-date=23 January 2021 |archive-date=27 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200127143046/https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/058810/there-are-15-new-threes-2048-clones-released-on-the-app-store-every-day/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Use in artificial intelligence research ==
The mathematical nature of ''2048'' has made the game of interest to researchers of [[artificial intelligence]].  As of 2022, AI achieved<ref name='macroxue-2048-ai'>{{cite web |url=https://github.com/macroxue/2048-ai |title=2048-ai |last=Xue |first=Hanhong |website=[[GitHub]] |access-date=August 30, 2022 |archive-date=31 August 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220831024028/https://github.com/macroxue/2048-ai |url-status=live }} Additional results are on [https://github.com/macroxue/2048-ai/tree/master/analysis 2048 analysis] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220831024032/https://github.com/macroxue/2048-ai/tree/master/analysis |date=31 August 2022 }} page.</ref> over 95% (likely over 98%, but the measurement has noise) probability of making a 16384 tile, over 75% (likely over 80%) probability of making a 32768 tile, and over 3% probability of making a 65536 tile (improving over the results in previous papers<ref name=1606.07374>{{cite journal |title=Multi-Stage Temporal Difference Learning for 2048-like Games |arxiv=1606.07374 |last1=Yeh |first1=Kun-Hao |last2=Wu |first2=I-Chen |last3=Hsueh |first3=Chu-Hsuan |last4=Chang |first4=Chia-Chuan |last5=Liang |first5=Chao-Chin |last6=Chiang |first6=Han| date=July 19, 2016 |journal=IEEE Transactions on Computational Intelligence and AI in Games |volume=9 |issue=4 | pages=369–380|doi=10.1109/TCIAIG.2016.2593710 |s2cid=6630665 }}</ref><ref name="2111.11090">{{cite journal |last1=Guei |first1=Hung |last2=Chen |first2=Lung-Pin |last3=Wu |first3=I-Chen |year=2022 |title=Optimistic Temporal Difference Learning for 2048 |journal=IEEE Transactions on Games |volume=14 |issue=3 |pages=478–487 |arxiv=2111.11090 |doi=10.1109/TG.2021.3109887 |s2cid=239727759}}  Corresponding code is in [https://github.com/moporgic/TDL2048] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220901065507/https://github.com/moporgic/TDL2048|date=1 September 2022}}.</ref>). Due to randomness and lack of spare room, the optimal probability of making a 65536 tile is expected to be low; this is supported by optimal solutions for constrained boards.<ref name='macroxue-2048-ai'/>

As of 2025, AI using a combination of [[expectiminimax]] and pre-built tablebases over 1200 simulated games observed a 99.9% probability of reaching a 16384 tile, an 86.1% probability of reaching a 32768 tile, and an 8.4% probability of reaching a 65536 tile, with a median score per game of approximately 820,000.<ref>{{Citation |last=game-difficulty |title=game-difficulty/2048EndgameTablebase |date=2025-09-17 |url=https://github.com/game-difficulty/2048EndgameTablebase |access-date=2025-09-21}}</ref>

''2048'' AI strategy uses [[expectiminimax]] search up to a certain (variable) depth, plus [[transposition table]]s to avoid duplication.  Analogously to [[endgame tablebase]]s, tables are used to estimate success (for building a large enough tile without destroying the configuration) in appropriate positions with many large tiles.  A position evaluation function can favor empty squares, having a large number of merge possibilities, placement of larger tiles at the edge, and monotonicity for tile sizes, especially for larger tiles.<ref>Robert Xiao (aka nneonneo) et al. (2014) {{cite web |url=https://stackoverflow.com/questions/22342854/what-is-the-optimal-algorithm-for-the-game-2048 |title=What is the optimal algorithm for the game 2048? |publisher=Stack Exchange Network |access-date=August 30, 2022 |archive-date=2 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220902052339/https://stackoverflow.com/questions/22342854/what-is-the-optimal-algorithm-for-the-game-2048 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.businessinsider.com/artificial-intelligence-crushed-all-human-records-in-the-addictive-tile-game-2048--heres-how-2015-5 |title=Artificial Intelligence crushed all human records in the addictive tile game 2048 — here's how |last=Olson |first=Randy |publisher=Insider |date=May 23, 2015 |access-date=August 30, 2022 |archive-date=31 August 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220831024032/https://www.businessinsider.com/artificial-intelligence-crushed-all-human-records-in-the-addictive-tile-game-2048--heres-how-2015-5 |url-status=live }}</ref>  The parameters are optimized by a search for better parameter values; some papers<ref name=1606.07374/><ref name='2111.11090'/> used [[temporal difference learning|temporal difference reinforcement learning]].
