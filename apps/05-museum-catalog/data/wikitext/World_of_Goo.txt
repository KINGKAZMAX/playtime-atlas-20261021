'''''World of Goo''''' is a physics-based [[puzzle video game]] developed and published by [[independent video game development|independent game developer]] 2D Boy. The game was released on [[Windows]] and [[Wii]]  on October 13, 2008, with releases on [[Nintendo Switch]], [[OS X]], [[Linux]], and various [[mobile phone|mobile device]]s in subsequent years. ''World of Goo'' has the player use small balls of goo to create bridges and similar structures over chasms and obstacles to help other goo balls reach a goal point, with the challenge to use as few goo balls as possible to build this structure.

The game was nominated for numerous awards—the [[Seumas McNally Grand Prize]], Design Innovation Award, and Technical Excellence—at the [[Independent Games Festival]], and has gone on to win several other gaming awards, such as [[D.I.C.E. Award for Outstanding Achievement in Game Design|Outstanding Achievement in Game Design]] at the [[12th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards]]. The game was critically acclaimed and became one of the earliest examples of a commercially successful [[indie game]].

A sequel, ''[[World of Goo 2]]'', was released on August 2, 2024, in collaboration with [[Tomorrow Corporation]].

== Gameplay ==

=== Overview ===
[[File:WorldOfGooBuilding.jpg|thumb|left|Placing a goo ball to construct a bridge]]
The game is built around the idea of creating large structures using balls of [[wikt:goo|goo]].<ref name="EGP">{{cite web|url=http://www.joystiq.com/2007/03/05/tower-of-goo-evolves-into-world-of-goo-2d-boy-is-born/|title=Tower of Goo evolves into World of Goo, 2D Boy is born|author=Bardinelli, John|date=March 5, 2007|work=[[Joystiq]]|publisher=Weblogs, Inc|access-date=January 27, 2008|archive-date=August 26, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090826152504/http://www.joystiq.com/2007/03/05/tower-of-goo-evolves-into-world-of-goo-2d-boy-is-born|url-status=live}}</ref> The game is divided into five chapters, each containing several [[Level (video gaming)|levels]]. Each level has its own graphic and musical theme, giving a unique atmosphere. There is also a bonus meta-game called ''World of Goo Corporation'', where the objective is to build the highest tower using goo balls which the player collected through the course of the game. Players from all over the world can compete, as the height of the tower and number of goo balls used are being constantly uploaded to the 2D Boy server.

=== Objective ===
The main objective of the game is to get a requisite number of goo balls to a pipe designed to represent the exit. In order to do so, the player must use the goo balls to construct bridges, towers, and other structures to overcome gravity and various terrain difficulties such as chasms, hills, spikes, windmills, or cliffs. There are several types of goo balls in the game, each of which has unique properties. The player must exploit combinations of these goo balls in order to complete each level. Extra goos recovered in the pipe are pumped through to World of Goo Corporation, a sandbox area where the objective is to compete with other players worldwide by building the tallest tower possible.<ref name="IGN">{{cite web |url=http://pc.ign.com/articles/846/846912p1.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080124150102/http://pc.ign.com/articles/846/846912p1.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=January 24, 2008 |title=World of Goo Preview |website=[[IGN]] |date=January 22, 2008 |last= Shea |first= Cam}}</ref> Players can also try to achieve the "Obsessive Completion Distinction Flag" for each level by completing the level under more stringent criteria, such as collecting a larger number of Goo balls, finishing under a set amount of time or using as few moves as possible.

The WiiWare version includes multiplayer with up to four people on the same Wii. This facility is also available, albeit unsupported, in the Linux port.<ref name="multimouse">{{cite web|url=http://goofans.com/faq/world-goo/technical-questions/how-do-i-use-more-one-mouse|title=How do I use more than one mouse?|access-date=May 2, 2009|archive-date=July 1, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170701152215/http://goofans.com/faq/world-goo/technical-questions/how-do-i-use-more-one-mouse|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Levels ===
''World of Goo'' is split up into four chapters and an epilogue, each containing a number of levels. The chapters are set over the course of a year in the ''World of Goo''. Each chapter takes place over one season, beginning in the start of summer, and ending during the spring of next year.<ref group="note">The last winter level, Product Launcher, has "happy new year" as its tagline. This is similar to [[Northern Hemisphere]] seasons.</ref> The 4th chapter is seasonless, and is set in a virtual world. Levels and chapters in the game are interspersed with [[cut scene]]s.

An additional location reached from the main menu is the World Of Goo Corporation. Goo Balls collected above and beyond the required amount to pass a level are piped out of each of the played levels to here. The aim is to build the highest possible tower with only the accumulated extra Goo Balls. Towers built by other players are represented by clouds bearing the player's name, nationality, and height of the tower, including details on the total number of balls collected by the player and how many were used in constructing their tower. The altitude of each cloud represents the height of that player's tower. An online leaderboard charts the heights of the top 50 towers, as well as the top 10 players for each level of the game.

There are a total of 48 levels in the game, including World of Goo Corporation.

In an interview, the developers stated that the retail version released in Europe would receive an additional sixth chapter, set on the Moon.<ref name="WiiWare World">{{cite web |url=http://www.wiiware-world.com/news/2008/08/2d_boy_interview_world_of_goo |title=2D Boy Interview – World Of Goo |work=WiiWare World |first=Darrent |last=Calvert |date=August 26, 2008 |access-date=September 14, 2008 |archive-date=March 29, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090329075918/http://www.wiiware-world.com/news/2008/08/2d_boy_interview_world_of_goo |url-status=live }}</ref> Few details were disclosed, but reportedly this chapter would have featured a freeform sandbox mode, similar to that of the World of Goo Corporation. This addition was canceled for Wii when 2D Boy announced they were releasing the game on WiiWare in Europe. Eventually, the idea was put on hold for all platforms, as 2D Boy felt they were rushing to finish extra content to justify the price, stating that "if we release any additional content, we will make it available on all platforms, to all people, at the same time. No more of this 'region' nonsense".<ref>{{cite web |date=November 12, 2008 |title=World Of Goo(d News): Euro Release Imminent |url=http://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2008/11/12/world-of-good-news-euro-release-imminent/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110713155749/http://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2008/11/12/world-of-good-news-euro-release-imminent/ |archive-date=July 13, 2011 |access-date=July 10, 2011 |website=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref>

== Plot ==
The story of the game is primarily told through [[Cutscene|cutscenes]] and signs scattered throughout various levels, which are attributed to an [[unseen character]] known as the Sign Painter.

In the beginning of the game, it is revealed that pipes have been appearing throughout the world, disturbing many Goo Balls in their natural habitats. Filled with a childlike sense of curiosity and naivety, they build themselves towards the pipes. Upon reaching the pipe entrance, the Goo Balls are sucked by the pipe system into the "World of Goo Corporation" main building where they are processed into many products. The excess Goo Balls are left outside the Corporation headquarters where they together begin to build a giant tower. At the end of the first chapter, some Goo Balls escape from a Corporation building by attaching themselves to eyeballs which have the ability to fly. The chapter ends with the Goo Balls "seeing far away new lands".

The second chapter takes place on an island where World of Goo Corporation is searching for a new power source, due to wind power not being sufficient anymore. However, the location and appearance of the power source was forgotten, because in the past, it stopped producing electricity.<ref group="note">"Lately, its output has been less than satisfactory." – Fly Away Little Ones, Little Miss World of Goo, World of Goo</ref> A new Goo Ball is introduced, which is ground up by the Corporation into a [[Cosmetics|facial cream]]. Near the end of the chapter, the power plant, which looks like a giant woman, is discovered. It is revealed that for many years, its remarkably powerful "beauty juice" powered the world. Yet with age, her beauty and consequently her electric output began to die. However, once unrefined beauty goo is injected directly into her forehead by the player, her youthful beauty is restored. At the expense of her ability to move her face, the world is supplied with large amounts of power. With this newfound energy, a factory doing [[Outsourcing|outsourced]] work for World of Goo Corporation opens in the south.

During the third chapter it is said that the factory is developing a mysterious "Product Z." It eventually turns out that the mysterious Product Z is actually the third dimension (Product Z is the [[Cartesian coordinate system|Z axis]] in [[mathematics]]). After the launch of Product Z, the player is informed that they are "incompatible with the world" due to the game being made up of only two-dimensional graphics, and is instructed to contact tech support in the [[information superhighway]].

In the fourth chapter the player sets out to find the mysterious "MOM" program in the information superhighway. Shortly after the beginning the Goo Balls find the [[Graphics processing unit|object responsible for rendering all the graphics]]. After pumping many of their own kind into the object, the graphics rendering improves, creating a more realistic environment (and the Pixel Goo Balls). Near the end they encounter MOM, who turns out to be a [[spam bot]]. Hoping to convert the world back to 2D, the player tries to overload World of Goo Corporation by having MOM send every spam email ever sent to everyone at World of Goo Corporation. After the player ventures to the Recycle Bin and un-deletes all the spam email, World of Goo Corporation receives the mail and, unable to deal with so much spam, explodes, shutting down Product Z while creating a massive layer of [[smog]], [[dust]], [[smoke]] and [[debris]] that envelops the entire world.

In the final chapter, it is revealed that all Goo Balls, except "scientifically pure" ones, have been sucked away to the former site of World of Goo Corporation. The remaining Goo Balls decide to work their way up the world's tallest island to reach a site where the [[telescope]] is located. The final level of the game reveals that the Goo Balls are now completely [[extinct]], all the remaining having been sucked away to the shattered remains of World of Goo Corporation and added to the tower, and the gigantic telescope at the site has been rendered useless as it cannot see past the layer of smog. The Sign Painter reveals in his final sign that he has now become the Telescope Operator. Some balloon-like [[Puffer fish|fish]] in the sea connect to the telescope and lift it out of the ground, where it breaks through the layer of smog and sees the tower of goo that has been built at the former World of Goo Corporation Headquarters, which can also see past the smog. The telescope falls back to the ground before it could see what the Goo Balls were building towards. A final and last cutscene reveals their goal; the camera pans up into space to reveal that the Goo Balls that escaped at the end of Chapter 1 have managed to reach a far-off planet populated by Goo Balls.

== Development ==
[[File:Ron Carmel and Kyle Gabler.jpg|thumb|Ron Carmel and Kyle Gabler, founders of 2D Boy and creators of ''World of Goo'']]
''World of Goo'' was developed by 2D Boy, a team based in [[San Francisco]] consisting only of former [[Electronic Arts]] employees Kyle Gabler and Ron Carmel. The game's development was started in January 2005<ref>{{cite web| url=https://kylegabler.com/assets/EgpPrototypes/egp_bin_kgabler.zip |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230227223254/https://kylegabler.com/assets/EgpPrototypes/egp_bin_kgabler.zip |archive-date=February 27, 2023| title=Old Prototypes (zip), Tower of Goo metadata |publisher=Kyle Gabler}}</ref> as a graduate student project at the [[Entertainment Technology Center]] of [[Carnegie Mellon University]].<ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/creativity/2010/summer/world-of-goo.shtml |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210613021619/https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/creativity/2010/summer/world-of-goo.shtml |archive-date=June 13, 2021| title=World of Goo |publisher=Carnegie Mellon University}}</ref> Gabler created this initial prototype in four days and submitted it under the title ''Tower of Goo'' for the Experimental Gameplay Project at the university<ref>{{cite web| url=http://www.etc.cmu.edu/projects/experimentalgameplay/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050827035545/http://www.etc.cmu.edu/projects/experimentalgameplay/ |archive-date=August 27, 2005| title=Experimental Gameplay Project |publisher=Carnegie Mellon University}}</ref> as a response to the prompt "make something with springs".<ref>{{cite web| url=https://kylegabler.com/assets/EgpPrototypes/egp_bin_kgabler.zip |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230227223254/https://kylegabler.com/assets/EgpPrototypes/egp_bin_kgabler.zip |archive-date=February 27, 2023| title=Old Prototypes (zip), Tower of Goo, about.txt |publisher=Kyle Gabler}}</ref>

Carmel and Gabler estimated that development on ''World of Goo'' lasted two years and cost US$10,000 of their personal savings, which included equipment, food, and rent.<ref name="venturebeat">{{cite web |title=How the World of Goo became one of the indie video game hits of 2008 |url=https://venturebeat.com/2009/01/02/the-world-of-goo-became-one-of-the-indie-hits-of-2008/ |publisher=Venture Beat |access-date=January 10, 2009 |date=January 2, 2009 |archive-date=September 12, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190912091610/https://venturebeat.com/2009/01/02/the-world-of-goo-became-one-of-the-indie-hits-of-2008/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Actual development was usually carried out in [[coffeehouse]]s equipped with [[Hotspot (Wi-Fi)|Wi-Fi hotspots]].

''World of Goo'' was programmed using available [[open-source software|open-source]] technologies, including [[Simple DirectMedia Layer]], [[Open Dynamics Engine]] for [[Game physics|physics]] [[Physics engine|simulation]], [[TinyXML]] for configuration files, [[Subversion (software)|Subversion]], [[Mantis Bug Tracker]], and [[PopCap Games Framework]].<ref name="gamasutra">{{cite web |url=http://www.gamasutra.com/php-bin/news_index.php?story=16749 |title=Road To The IGF: World Of Goo's 'Suggested Emotional Journey' To Wii |date=December 31, 2007 |first=Patrick |last=Murphy |access-date=October 13, 2008 |archive-date=July 3, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170703013635/http://www.gamasutra.com/php-bin/news_index.php?story=16749 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.nintendic.com/news/2113 |title=World of Goo switches from Wii to WiiWare |date=April 1, 2008 |first=Andrew |last=Macarthy |work=Nintendic |access-date=April 4, 2008 |archive-date=April 5, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080405194544/http://www.nintendic.com/news/2113 |url-status=usurped }}</ref> Allan Blomquist, a friend of Gabler and Carmel, was responsible for [[porting]] and optimizing the game for [[WiiWare]].<ref name="eurogamer interview1">{{cite web |title=2D Boy's Ron Carmel and Kyle Gabler – Interview |date=January 15, 2009 |author=Walker, John |publisher=[[Eurogamer]] |access-date=May 7, 2011 |url=http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2d-boys-ron-carmel-and-kyle-gabler-interview |archive-date=May 13, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110513050521/http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2d-boys-ron-carmel-and-kyle-gabler-interview |url-status=live }}</ref>

On July 6, 2010, 2D Boy, [[Capybara Games]], and [[thatgamecompany]] started a program called the "[[Indie Fund]]", which aims to support game development, by helping independent developers become financially independent.

=== Release ===
''World of Goo'' was released both for [[Windows]] and [[WiiWare]] in North America on October 13, 2008.<ref>{{cite web |title=Two WiiWare Games and Two Virtual Console Games Added to Wii Shop Channel |url=https://www.nintendo.com/whatsnew/detail/fvAWxg18x4_UYTIcsfyyt8d9vwa6KuSo |publisher=Nintendo |date=October 13, 2008 |access-date=October 16, 2008 |archive-date=October 15, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081015032547/http://www.nintendo.com/whatsnew/detail/fvAWxg18x4_UYTIcsfyyt8d9vwa6KuSo |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[Mac OS X]] version was released on November 2. The [[Linux]] version was released on February 13, 2009.<ref>{{cite news |title=World of Goo for Linux Is Here |newspaper=Softpedia |url=http://news.softpedia.com/news/World-of-Goo-for-Linux-Is-Here-104513.shtml |last1=Pop-Silaghi |first1=Daniel |access-date=July 25, 2011 |archive-date=February 16, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090216155214/http://news.softpedia.com/news/World-of-Goo-for-Linux-Is-Here-104513.shtml |url-status=live }}</ref> After the initial release, 2D Boy announced a bonus [[Patch (computing)|patch]] called "Profanity Pack", which would "[replace] the normal voices in the game with naughty words." The patch would be made available to users who pre-ordered the game.<ref name="arstechnica">{{cite web |url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/news/2008/03/world-of-goo-preorders-give-you-taste-of-game-profanity-pack.ars |title=World of Goo preorders give you taste of game, "profanity pack" |publisher=[[arsTechnica]] |access-date=August 8, 2010 |date=August 8, 2010 |archive-date=June 29, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100629070418/http://arstechnica.com/gaming/news/2008/03/world-of-goo-preorders-give-you-taste-of-game-profanity-pack.ars |url-status=live }}</ref> The patch was never released.<ref name="goofans faq">{{cite web |url=http://goofans.com/faq/world-goo/getting-world-goo/wherewhat-profanity-pack |title=Frequently Asked Questions – Where/what is the profanity pack? |access-date=January 3, 2013 |work=goofans! |archive-date=February 25, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240225082312/http://goofans.com/faq/world-of-goo/getting-world-of-goo/wherewhat-profanity-pack |url-status=live }}</ref>

2D Boy decided against placing [[digital rights management]] (DRM) protection on the PC versions, stating that "DRM is a futile attempt to prevent [[software piracy|piracy]], and it's expensive. Every game for which there is demand will be [[Software cracking|cracked]] and find its way onto the scene, so why waste time and money trying to prevent the inevitable?"<ref name="destructoid">{{cite web |url=http://www.destructoid.com/destructoid-interview-ron-carmel-and-kyle-gabler-creators-of-world-of-goo-110671.phtml |title=Destructoid interview: Ron Carmel and Kyle Gabler, creators of World of Goo |author=Burch, Anthony |publisher=[[Destructoid]] |date=November 7, 2008 |access-date=May 7, 2011 |archive-date=October 13, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111013131354/http://www.destructoid.com/destructoid-interview-ron-carmel-and-kyle-gabler-creators-of-world-of-goo-110671.phtml |url-status=live }}</ref> 2D Boy later reported that the PC versions of ''World of Goo'' were extensively pirated, with a rough estimate that 90% of copies were pirated.<ref>{{Cite web |date=November 13, 2008 |title=2D Boy » Blog Archive » 90% |url=http://2dboy.com/2008/11/13/90/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081205032425/http://2dboy.com/2008/11/13/90/ |archive-date=2008-12-05 |access-date=2026-01-28 |website=2dboy.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=PC Game Piracy: Why Bother With DRM? |url=https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/pc-game-piracy-why-bother-with-drm- |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250117121524/https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/pc-game-piracy-why-bother-with-drm- |archive-date=January 17, 2025 |access-date=2026-01-28 |website=[[Game Developer (website)|Game Developer]] |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref>

Summit]], [[Nintendo]] announced that it would publish ''World of Goo'' in Japan during the second quarter of 2009;<ref>{{cite web |last=Harris |first= Craig |title=DICE 2009: Nintendo to Publish World of Goo |url=http://wii.ign.com/articles/956/956075p1.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090224071438/http://wii.ign.com/articles/956/956075p1.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 24, 2009 |website=[[IGN]] |date=February 20, 2009 |access-date=February 27, 2009}}</ref> the game was released on April 21, 2009, under the title {{nihongo|'''''Planet of Goo'''''|グーの惑星|Gū no Wakusei}}.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nintendo.co.jp/wii/wiiware/list.html |title=Wiiウェア タイトルリスト |trans-title=WiiWare Title List |publisher=Nintendo Co., Ltd. |language=ja |access-date=May 7, 2011 |archive-date=May 27, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190527183138/https://www.nintendo.co.jp/wii/wiiware/list.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[Tomorrow Corporation]], formed by Gabler in 2010 along with his former EA colleagues Allan Blomquist and Kyle Gray, would develop and publish ''World of Goo'' for the [[Nintendo Switch]] which was released in March 2017.<ref>{{Cite magazine | url = https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/01/24/world-of-goo-little-inferno-human-resource-machine-heading-to-switch.aspx | title = World Of Goo, Little Inferno, Human Resource Machine Heading To Switch | first = Jeff | last = Cork | date = January 24, 2017 | access-date = January 24, 2017 | magazine = [[Game Informer]] | archive-date = February 2, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170202053559/http://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/01/24/world-of-goo-little-inferno-human-resource-machine-heading-to-switch.aspx | url-status = live }}</ref> The Japanese version was later released in May 2017 by Flyhigh Works.{{citation needed|date=February 2019}}

According to 2D Boy, [[Epic Games]] reached out to them to release ''World of Goo'' as a free promotional title on the [[Epic Games Store]]. The developers decided to use this opportunity to create an updated version for Windows, Mac, and Linux systems, incorporating changes and improvements made from the console versions. This includes supporting widescreen and higher resolutions that have become the normal since the game's original release, and stripping out the game's [[digital-rights management]] to make it amenable to [[mod (video gaming)|modding]]. These updates will be offered free to users on other digital storefronts like Steam and [[GOG.com]], with the patches to be released around the same time that the Epic Games Store promotion starts in May 2019.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-goo-is-getting-updated-and-going-free-on-the-epic-game-store/ | title = World of Goo is getting updated and going free on the Epic Store | first = Andy | last = Chalk | date = April 18, 2019 | access-date = April 18, 2019 | work = [[PC Gamer]] | archive-date = April 18, 2019 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20190418225522/https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-goo-is-getting-updated-and-going-free-on-the-epic-game-store/ | url-status = live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url = https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2019/04/world-of-goos-devs-answer-questions-about-hd-update-epic-games-store-launch/ | title = World of Goo is Epic Game Store's next freebie—and all PC owners will get HD update | first = Sam | last = Mackovech | date = April 19, 2019 | access-date = April 19, 2019 | work = [[Ars Technica]] | archive-date = April 19, 2019 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20190419190124/https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2019/04/world-of-goos-devs-answer-questions-about-hd-update-epic-games-store-launch/ | url-status = live }}</ref>  [[Netflix]] released a remastered version on May 23, 2023, also available on [[Google Play Store]] and Apple's [[App Store (Apple)|App Store]]. === Soundtrack ===
The ''World of Goo'' soundtrack was created by Kyle Gabler, who also designed, wrote and illustrated the game. It was released as a free download on January 20, 2009.<ref name="soundtrack">{{cite web |title=Music from World of Goo |url=http://kylegabler.com/WorldOfGooSoundtrack/ |publisher=[[Kyle Gabler]] |access-date=January 20, 2009 |archive-date=July 7, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180707225623/http://kylegabler.com/WorldOfGooSoundtrack/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The song "World of Goo Beginning" was created with the intention of resembling [[Libertango]] by [[Ástor Piazzolla]]. "Regurgitation Pumping Station" was originally written for a friend's short film about going on a date with the [[devil]]. "Threadcutter" was originally written for a game called ''Blow'' which Gabler made available on his site. "Rain Rain Windy Windy" was originally written for the soundtrack for a short children's film, commenting that writing children's music is difficult. "Jelly" was originally written for a virtual reality world. "Burning Man" was written for a friend's drama/mystery series. He made it by recording two friends singing single notes, and then using a keyboard to make it sound like a choir. "Cog in the Machine" was originally written for another game of his called ''Robot and the Cities who Built Him''.<ref name="soundtrack"/>
