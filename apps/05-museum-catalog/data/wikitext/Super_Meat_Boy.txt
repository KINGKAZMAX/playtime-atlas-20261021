'''''Super Meat Boy''''' is a 2010 [[Platform game|platformer game]] developed and published by American [[Indie game|indie]] studio Team Meat. It was designed by [[Edmund McMillen]] and [[Tommy Refenes]] as the successor to ''Meat Boy'', a 2008 [[Flash game]] designed by McMillen and Jonathan McEntee. In the game, the player controls Meat Boy, a red, cube-shaped character, as he attempts to rescue his girlfriend, Bandage Girl, from the game's antagonist Dr. Fetus. The gameplay is characterized by fine control and split-second timing, as the player runs and jumps through over 300 hazardous [[Level (video gaming)|levels]] while avoiding obstacles. The game also supports the creation of player-created levels. ''Super Meat Boy'' was first released on the [[Xbox 360]] through [[Xbox Live Arcade]] in October 2010, and was later [[Video game port|ported]] to [[Microsoft Windows]], [[OS X]], [[Linux]], [[PlayStation 4]], [[PlayStation Vita]], [[Wii U]], and the [[Nintendo Switch]]. A [[Wii]] version was in development but was ultimately cancelled.

Development of the game began in early 2009. McMillen worked on [[level design]] and artwork, while Refenes coded it. The game's soundtrack was written by [[Danny Baranowsky]], who had also worked on the original ''Meat Boy''. ''Super Meat Boy'' won several awards and has been cited as one of the [[greatest video games of all time]]. Critics lauded the game's controls, art, soundtrack, and challenging gameplay. The game was also a commercial success, selling over a million copies by January 2012. A sequel, ''[[Super Meat Boy Forever]]'', was released on December 23, 2020; another sequel, ''[[Super Meat Boy 3D]]'', was released on March 31, 2026, both without McMillen's involvement.

==Gameplay==
[[Image:SuperMeatBoy gameplay.png|thumb|left|Players navigate Meat Boy through each level while avoiding traps and hazards, such as saw blades and crumbling blocks.|alt=A two-dimensional screen is filled with a large U-shaped structure covered in saw blades. A small, red figure is seen moving towards a wall and away from a crumbling dirt patch.]]

''Super Meat Boy'' is a [[platform game]] in which players control a small, dark red, cube-shaped character named Meat Boy, who must save his cube-shaped, heavily bandaged girlfriend Bandage Girl from the evil scientist Dr. Fetus. The game is divided into chapters, which together contain over 300 [[Level (video gaming)|levels]].<ref name="IGNhidden"/><ref name="GSsoundint"/> Players attempt to reach the end of each level, represented by Bandage Girl, while avoiding crumbling blocks, saw blades, and various other fatal obstacles. The player can jump and run on platforms, and can jump off or slide down walls. The core gameplay requires fine control and split-second timing, and was compared to, regarding both gameplay and level of difficulty, traditional platform games such as ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' and ''[[Ghosts 'n Goblins]]''.<ref name="GRreview"/>

Levels in each chapter can be played in any order, but a certain number of levels need to be completed to access the [[Boss (video gaming)|boss]] stage, which unlocks the next chapter if cleared.<ref name="GTboss"/> The player has an unlimited number of attempts to complete each level. If Meat Boy is killed, he immediately restarts the level, though the ornamental red meat juice left behind on surfaces that the player has touched remains.<ref name="GTboss"/> A replay function, which may be accessed after a level is completed, simultaneously shows all the player's attempts to complete the level.<ref name="GRreview"/> Completing a level within a certain time earns an "A+" grade, which unlocks a harder alternate version of the level in the "dark world", an optional set of especially difficult levels. Hidden stages called warp zones are accessed by finding portals in specific levels. These warp zones feature bonus levels that have either the art style of older video games and a limit of three lives, or are patterned after another indie video game such as ''[[Castle Crashers]]'' or ''[[Braid (video game)|Braid]]''.<ref name="IGNhidden"/><ref name="GRreview"/> The player may control characters other than Meat Boy, many of whom first appeared in other independent video games.<ref name="GSsoundint"/> Each character has different attributes, such as Commander Video's ability to momentarily float in midair. These characters can be unlocked by collecting [[item (gaming)|bandage items]] placed throughout the game's levels or completing certain warp zones. Some bandages can only be collected by using certain characters.<ref name="Bandage"/> Some levels, such as warp zones and boss levels, can only be played with specific characters.<ref name="IGNreview"/> The available characters vary depending on the version of the game played.<ref name="Ultra"/>

The [[Xbox Live Arcade]] (XBLA) version features an unlockable mode called "Teh Internets", which is updated with new, free, officially curated levels.<ref name="1UPtehint"/> The PC version has a "Super Meat World" section, which allows users to play and rate additional levels that players have created with a level editor. This editor was released in May 2011.<ref name="GIworld"/> Players can also access an unsupported developer mode inside the game to edit their own levels using the "rough" tools that Team Meat used to create the game.<ref name="SMBdevmode"/>

==Plot==
The game's opening describes that Meat Boy (a boy born without skin) and his girlfriend Bandage Girl (a girl whose body is covered in bandages) love one another, though the antagonist Dr. Fetus (an intelligent [[fetus]] in a jar piloting a mechanical body) is unloved: thus, he hates everybody.

One day, Bandage Girl is kidnapped by Dr. Fetus in a fit of jealousy. Meat Boy proceeds to chase Dr. Fetus to rescue her, though the latter causes a forest fire and pursues Meat Boy in a large mech "Lil' Slugger". Lil' Slugger is destroyed after Meat Boy tricks the doctor into piloting it into a variety of hazards. Dr. Fetus then escapes to an abandoned hospital where he unleashes a giant monster made of blood "C.H.A.D". Meat Boy causes it to evaporate upon exposing the creature to sunlight, and kills it offscreen by smothering the creature with a pillow before continuing after Dr. Fetus, who has ventured to an abandoned salt factory. There, Dr. Fetus creates an evil clone of Meat Boy from his own feces "Brownie" who he directs to attack Meat Boy, though the two are interrupted from fighting when salt floods the factory, presumably killing Brownie. Dr. Fetus then ventures to hell, with Meat Boy discovering that, due to the game's difficulty, a variety of Meat Boy corpses are present. A large golem, "Little Horn", is created from these corpses, though it kills itself by repeatedly slamming its own head into the ground, allowing Dr. Fetus to escape whilst triggering a nuclear missile, which causes the [[rapture]] and opens a path to a ruined city. There, Meat Boy fights three large worms, "The Larries", tricking them into jumping into sawblades.

In the final level, Dr. Fetus, frustrated by Meat Boy's ability to respawn, chases him wielding a rocket launcher, though Meat Boy manages to destroy Dr. Fetus' mechanical body by destroying a bridge he stands on during the fight. Dr. Fetus tries to kill Meat Boy and Bandage Girl by triggering a self-destruct sequence, though is unsuccessful when Brownie reappears to save the two, sacrificing himself. As Bandage Girl hugs Meat Boy after escaping, Dr. Fetus suddenly lands on Bandage Girl and tries to pummel her as the game cuts to credits. If Dr. Fetus is beaten in the Dark World, It is shown in an extended ending that Fetus's attacks were ineffective and Bandage Girl proceeds to stomp on him.

A bonus chapter "Cotton Alley" appears after beating the game in which Meat Boy is kidnapped by Dr. Fetus, and Bandage Girl must save him.

==Development and marketing==
The original ''Meat Boy'' is an [[Adobe Flash|Adobe Flash game]] created by [[Edmund McMillen]] and programmed by Jonathan McEntee. The game was developed over a three-week period and was released on [[Newgrounds]] on October 5, 2008.<ref name="WWWint"/><ref name="GSpostmortem1"/> By April 2009, it had garnered over 840,000 views at Newgrounds, and 8 million overall.<ref name="EMpreview"/> A map pack for the Flash version was released on December 8, 2008.<ref name="NGmappack"/> McMillen began development of ''Super Meat Boy'' after [[Nintendo]] and [[Microsoft]] requested that he make a game for their download services, [[WiiWare]] and Xbox Live Arcade, as they were impressed by the success of his Flash games ''[[Aether (video game)|Aether]]'' and ''Meat Boy''.<ref name="GSpostmortem1"/><ref name="EMpreview" /> At the time, he was working with [[Tommy Refenes]] on a Flash game titled ''Grey Matter''. Although McMillen initially pitched the companies a sequel to ''[[Gish (video game)|Gish]]'' or ''Aether'', the pair decided to form Team Meat and work on an expanded version of ''Meat Boy'' instead.<ref name="GSpostmortem1"/><ref name="GREACTint"/> Team Meat also includes soundtrack composer [[Danny Baranowsky]] and sound effects designer Jordan Fehr.<ref name="GSsoundint"/> According to the developers, ''Super Meat Boy'' is "a big throwback to a lot of super hardcore [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] classics like ''[[Ghosts 'n Goblins]]'', ''[[Mega Man (video game)|Mega Man]]'', and the Japanese version of ''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels|Super Mario Bros. 2]]''",<ref name="WWWint"/> with the plot written as "a mash-up of every videogame story from the early 90s".<ref name="EMpreview" /> The game was explicitly designed by the team to be reminiscent of ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'',<ref name="GSpostmortem2"/> and McMillen considered it a tribute to [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], the developer of ''Super Mario Bros.''<ref name="goodtimes"/>

[[File:Team Meat (Edmund McMillen and Tommy Refenes) at GDC 2012 (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|McMillen (''left'') and Refenes at ''[[Indie Game: The Movie]]'' 2012 [[Game Developers Conference|GDC]] panel|alt=Edmund McMillen shakes hands with a person off-frame as Tommy Refenes looks towards him.]]

McMillen worked on level design and artwork, while Refenes coded the game; it was tested primarily by the pair and their families.<ref name="GSpostmortem1"/><ref name="GREACTint"/> McMillen and Refenes lived on opposite sides of the United States, and met only a few times in person while working on the game. They developed the control scheme by iterating through several designs, trying to find one that felt fluid and logical.<ref name="GREACTint"/> Rather than use a pre-built [[game engine]], Refenes programmed an original one. The game was initially set to include around 100 levels, and to have [[Cooperative video game|co-operative]] and competitive multiplayer modes. During development, however, the multiplayer option was dropped and the number of levels was greatly increased.<ref name="EMpreview"/><ref name="GSpostmortem3"/> The pair designed the game to be deliberately "retro", imitating the aesthetics of traditional platform games, but with a modern sensibility regarding difficulty. They wanted the game to be rewarding and challenging, rather than frustrating; to this end they included infinite lives, quick restarts of levels, obvious goals, and short levels. They felt the replay feature transformed death into a form of reward.<ref name="GSpostmortem2"/>

Development of ''Super Meat Boy'' began in January 2009.<ref name="EMpreview"/> Initially announced for WiiWare and PC, the game was set to be released in the first quarter of 2010. The release date was pushed back to the fourth quarter because the developers wanted more time to create extra levels, such as the dark worlds. A picture released on Team Meat's [[Twitter]] page on February 22, 2010, revealed that the game would also be released for XBLA.<ref name="TwitterXBLA"/> The next day, they announced that, while all versions would be released in the same month, the game would be released for XBLA first due to "contractual obligations".<ref name="XBLAreveal"/> In August 2010, the developers were contacted by Microsoft with the prospect of inclusion in Microsoft's 2010 Fall GameFeast XBLA promotion two months later. As they were almost out of money, they did not believe that they could financially support themselves until the Spring event, but felt they had four months' worth of work left to complete on the game.<ref name="GSpostmortem3"/> For the final two months of development they worked daily, slept five hours a night, and frequently forgot to eat—a process that McMillen said he "would never voluntarily go through" again.<ref name="GSgdcint"/> According to McMillen, due to Microsoft's low expectations for the game, ''Super Meat Boy'' was lightly promoted. The level of promotion was not increased during the GameFeast, though the game greatly outsold the rest of the games in the event. The team described the effort required to finish the game for the promotion as "by far the biggest mistake [they] made during SMB's development".<ref name="GSpostmortem4"/> Their development struggle is depicted in the documentary ''[[Indie Game: The Movie]]''. The game was released on XBLA in October 2010 and on PCs via [[Steam (service)|Steam]] and [[Direct2Drive]] a month later. McMillen noted that the PC release was more heavily promoted than the XBLA version.<ref name="GSpostmortem2"/><ref name="GSpostmortem4"/> A version for [[Mac OS X]] was released in November 2011, while another version for [[Linux]] [[operating system]]s was released in December 2011 as part of the [[Humble Indie Bundle#Humble Indie Bundle#4|Humble Indie Bundle #4]] game pack.<ref name="EGMac"/><ref name="WIREDhumble"/>

Due to Sony's initial lack of interest in the game, Team Meat entered into contractual obligations that prohibited ''Super Meat Boy'' from ever being released on the [[PlayStation 3]].<ref name="EGnoPS3"/> Despite this, a version for the [[PlayStation 4]] and [[PlayStation Vita]] was eventually announced.<ref name="ps4_vita_announce" />  The WiiWare version was canceled because the game's file size had been expanded beyond the limits imposed by Nintendo.<ref name="GSpostmortem3"/> Team Meat looked into releasing it as a retail [[Wii]] game, but were told by all third-party publishers they approached that a budget title would not be profitable so late into the Wii's lifecycle.<ref name="KTnowii"/><ref name="WiiWareCancelled" /> Versions of the game were eventually released for Nintendo's follow up systems [[Wii U]]<ref name="NLcitesWiiUrelease" /><ref name="NLcitesWiiUportdate" /> and the [[Nintendo Switch|Switch]].<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2017-08-09-super-meat-boy-is-coming-to-switch | title = Super Meat Boy is leaping onto Switch | first = Jeffrey | last = Matulef | date = August 9, 2017 | access-date = August 9, 2017 | work = [[Eurogamer]] | archive-date = August 10, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170810013847/http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2017-08-09-super-meat-boy-is-coming-to-switch | url-status = live }}</ref> The PlayStation 4, PlayStation Vita, Wii U, and Switch ports were produced by [[BlitWorks]]. The Switch version includes a platform-exclusive "Race Mode", allowing two players on the Switch's split-screen and using separate Joy-Con to race through sets of levels.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.destructoid.com/meat-boy-switch-is-coming-in-a-few-weeks-with-a-kick-ass-race-mode--479412.phtml | title = Meat Boy Switch is coming in a few weeks, with a kick ass 'Race Mode' | first = Chris | last = Carter | date = December 15, 2017 | access-date = October 24, 2018 | work = [[Destructoid]] | archive-date = December 31, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20171231221551/https://www.destructoid.com/meat-boy-switch-is-coming-in-a-few-weeks-with-a-kick-ass-race-mode--479412.phtml | url-status = live }}</ref> This mode was added to the Windows version in October 2018.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.destructoid.com/super-meat-boy-s-switch-exclusive-race-mode-is-coming-to-pc-today-528320.phtml | title = Super Meat Boy's Switch exclusive race mode is coming to PC today | first = Peter | last = Glagowski | date = October 24, 2018 | access-date = October 24, 2018 | work = [[Destructoid]] | archive-date = August 9, 2020 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20200809184813/https://www.destructoid.com/super-meat-boy-s-switch-exclusive-race-mode-is-coming-to-pc-today-528320.phtml | url-status = live }}</ref>

A limited edition retail version of the PC game was released in April 2011. It included bonuses such as behind-the-scenes videos, a sample disc of the game's music, and a ''Super Meat Boy'' [[comic book|comic]].<ref name="limited edition"/> In 2012 Team Meat began prototyping an [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] version of the game. The game was intended to be a different take on the ''Super Meat Boy'' concept that is more adapted to touch-screen controls than a direct port would be.<ref name="EGtouch"/> This project would develop into the new [[endless runner]] game ''[[Super Meat Boy Forever]]'', revealed in 2014 at the video game convention [[PAX (event)|PAX]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Farokhmanesh |first1=Megan |title=Super Meat Boy Forever is an endless platformer, not a little 'throwaway game' |url=https://www.polygon.com/2014/8/29/6084281/super-meat-boy-forever-a-voyeur-for-september |website=Polygon |date=August 29, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200617133133/https://www.polygon.com/2014/8/29/6084281/super-meat-boy-forever-a-voyeur-for-september |archive-date=June 17, 2020 |url-status=live |access-date=January 14, 2022 }}</ref>

Team Meat released several pieces of merchandise related to ''Super Meat Boy''. These include ''Super Meat Boy Handheld'', an [[iOS]] app released on April 3, 2010, and styled on a [[Tiger Electronics|Tiger Electronics handheld]]. It was released as a joke after Refenes' game ''Zits & Giggles'' was removed from the [[iTunes Store]] following a statement by Refenes that likened the [[iPhone]] to a Tiger handheld.<ref name="GRSMBios"/><ref name="KTzits"/> McMillen has released a ''Super Meat Boy'' microgame for ''[[WarioWare D.I.Y.]]''<ref name="GSmicro"/> Team Meat sells charms, plush toys, and posters related to the game,<ref name=SMBsale/> as well as T-shirts, stickers, [[stress ball]]s, and a limited edition ''Super Meat Boy'' comic.<ref name="NGshirt"/><ref name="Etsyshirt"/><ref name="SMBmerch"/> In 2011, Voxelous released a set of four ''Super Meat Boy'' figures of Meat Boy, Bandage Girl, Brownie, and Tofu Boy, later making figures of Commander Video, Jill, Ogmo, and Dr. Fetus.<ref name="KKfigure"/><ref name="Figures2"/>

===Music===
''Super Meat Boy''{{'}}s soundtrack was composed by [[Danny Baranowsky]], who previously composed the soundtracks for the indie video games ''[[Canabalt]]'', ''[[Cortex Command]]'', and ''Gravity Hook''. He also composed the music for the original ''Meat Boy''. McMillen knew of Baranowsky's other work, and approached him late in ''Meat Boy''{{'}}s development, asking him to supply whatever tracks he had on hand. For the soundtrack of ''Super Meat Boy'', Baranowsky incorporated the music he had provided for ''Meat Boy'' into an expanded soundtrack. He tried to ensure that the music would accompany the action on the screen without overpowering the sound effects.<ref name="PCGbarint"/> Baranowsky was given complete freedom for the game's music, and retained all of the rights to it;<ref name="GSpostmortem2"/> McMillen believed that being more invested in the game would let Baranowsky express the part of him that was "manic, obsessive, complex, and full of life". McMillen feels that the soundtrack "gets your heart rate up, complements every aspect of its gameplay, and stays with you for days".<ref name="GSpostmortem2"/>

On October 26, 2010, the game's soundtrack was released as a [[online distribution|download-only]] album via the online [[Bandcamp]] store titled ''Super Meat Boy! Soundtrack''. This release features 34 tracks and a 100-minute runtime, and includes several remixes of tracks from the game.<ref name="Bandcampalbum"/> On January 11, 2011, Baranowsky and Team Meat released a special edition soundtrack on Bandcamp as both a downloadable and physical album. This edition includes a second disc of songs not included in the original release, as well as additional remixes. The album, titled ''Nice to Meat You'', has a total of 73 tracks, is 2 hours 25 minutes long, and features album artwork by McMillen.<ref name="SMBsoundtrack2"/> Three music tracks from the game were released as downloadable content for ''[[Rock Band 3]]'' in June 2011.<ref name="EGrb3"/>

Baranowsky's music was not used for the PlayStation 4, Nintendo Wii U, Nintendo Switch and PlayStation Vita versions, as he "no longer [had] a working relationship with Team Meat" and they could not agree on licensing terms.<ref name="DannyBNo"/> Instead, those versions of the game feature a soundtrack composed by Ridiculon (Matthias Bossi and Jon Evans), David "Scattle" Scatliffe, and [[Laura Shigihara]], who have previously worked on games such as ''[[The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth]]'', ''[[Hotline Miami]]'', and ''[[Plants vs. Zombies]]'' respectively.<ref name="NewSoundtrack"/> A soundtrack album for the new music, titled ''Songs in the Key of Meat: Music from SMB 5th Anniversary'', was released on Bandcamp on October 6, 2015.<ref name="NewSoundtrackAlbum"/>  On April 13, 2017, Tommy Refenes introduced the alternative soundtrack on the PC port.<ref name="NewIntroduction"/>

''Super Meat Boy'' received [[List of video games considered the best|critical acclaim]]. After being showcased at the [[Penny Arcade Expo]] 2010, ''Super Meat Boy'' was declared Game of the Show by ''[[Destructoid]]'' and nominated for the same award by [[Machinima.com]].<ref name="PAX10 winner"/> The game received nominations for the Grand Prize and Excellence in Audio awards at the 2010 [[Independent Games Festival]].<ref name="IGFawards"/> It won the award for Most Challenging Game in ''[[IGN]]''{{'}}s Best of 2010 awards, and received nominations for Best Soundtrack and Best Retro Design.<ref name="IGNawards"/><ref name="IGNawards2"/> It was voted ''[[GameSpot]]''{{'}}s Best Downloadable Console Game of 2010,<ref name="GSaward"/> and won the Best Downloadable Game award from ''[[GameTrailers]]''.<ref name="GTaward"/> Sales were strong, with nearly 140,000 units of the Xbox 360 version sold by the end of 2010.<ref name="GB2010sales"/> The Steam and Xbox 360 versions had sold over 600,000 copies combined by April 2011; 400,000 of these sales were through Steam.<ref name="GSaprilsales"/> On January 3, 2012, Team Meat announced on Twitter that the game had surpassed 1,000,000 sales.<ref name="Jan2012sales"/>

Critics praised ''Super Meat Boy''{{'}}s platforming elements, and often commented on the game's difficulty. ''[[X-Play]]'' reviewer Alexandra Hall said the game had "riveting platforming action", and added that "''Super Meat Boy''{{'}}s designers are masters of their craft."<ref name="XPreview"/> Henry Gilbert of ''[[GamesRadar]]'' felt the platforming was "perfect". He wrote that "while it's always tough and demanding, it never feels cheap, or like the game is cheating you."<ref name="GRreview"/> A reviewer from ''GameTrailers'' stated that "the difficulty rides the perfect line between driving you utterly bonkers when you fail and making you feel like a platform pro when you succeed".<ref name="GTreview"/> ''[[Joystiq]]''{{'}}s Richard Mitchell echoed other reviewers' comments: "''Super Meat Boy'' is tough, as tough as the toughest nails in the toughest universe."<ref name="JSreview"/> Gilbert cited the level of difficulty, which he believed made the game inaccessible to some players, as his reason for not awarding the game a perfect score.<ref name="GRreview"/> Tom McShea of ''GameSpot'' praised the game's "precise control", "excellent level design", and "smooth difficulty curve".<ref name="GSPreview"/> Reviewer Tom Bramwell of ''[[Eurogamer]]'' warned that ''Super Meat Boy'' is "a hard game. It should make you want to throw the pad across the room".<ref name="EGreview"/>

Critics gave high marks to the game's retro art direction and presentation. [[Official Xbox Magazine|''Official Xbox Magazine'' (UK)]]{{'}}s Mike Channel appreciated the variety found in each set of levels.  He stated that "while the graphics may look crude, the presentation is exceptional. Each level has a distinct visual style."<ref name="OXMUKreview"/> Daemon Hatfield, a reviewer for ''IGN'', noted the uniqueness of the game's visual presentation. He commented that the warp zone levels pay tribute to classic [[History of video game consoles (third generation)|8-bit]] games, and lauded the game's soundtrack: "The rocking chiptune soundtrack is the best I've heard since ''Scott Pilgrim vs. The World: The Game''".<ref name="IGNreview"/> Joe Leonard of ''[[1UP.com]]'' noted that the game's humor and over-the-top gameplay help to calm frustrations regarding the difficulty: "''Super Meat Boy''{{'}}s greatest strength has to be how it never takes itself too seriously—as maddening as some of the levels got, I could never stay angry at the game for too long", said Leonard.<ref name="1UPreview"/>'' [[MTV|MTV Multiplayer]]'' reviewer Russ Frushtick praised the game's visual design. He appreciated the game's [[cutscene]]s, which he noted are "hand-drawn animated [shorts] which [bear] more than a passing resemblance to a classic video game intro".<ref name="MTVreview"/>

While the game received high praise overall, certain publications voiced complaints. Hatfield noted that the cutscenes had low production values, stating that "they don't have the polish of the rest of the game".<ref name="IGNreview"/> The reviewer for ''[[PC Gamer]]'' mentioned "a few minor, yet-to-be-patched bugs".<ref name="PCGreview"/> Eduardo Reboucas of ''[[Game Revolution]]'' said that "a lot of the levels in ''Super Meat Boy'' depend a little too much on twitch reflexes and trial-and-error memorization". He also stated that "there are some bits of toilet humor here and there that are duds", and that the game's high level of difficulty "will make most casual players shy away".<ref name="GAMREVreview"/> Mitchell Dyer of ''[[GamePro]]'' agreed, saying that certain "absurdly difficult" levels broke the flow of the game, especially in the boss levels and the later chapters.<ref name="GPROreview"/>

==Legacy==
Meat Boy has made cameo appearances in video games such as ''[[Bit.Trip Runner]]'', ''[[Spelunky]]'', ''[[Dust: An Elysian Tail]]'', ''[[Ori and the Blind Forest]]'', ''[[Retro City Rampage]]'', ''[[ilomilo]]'', and ''[[Indie Pogo]]''.<ref name="GTilomilo"/><ref name="VentureBeat"/> A parody Flash game, ''[[PETA satirical browser games|Super Tofu Boy]]'', was released by [[People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals]] (PETA) on December 1, 2010, to protest the game and promote [[veganism]].<ref name="Petatofu"/> In response, Team Meat added its own interpretation of Tofu Boy to the PC version of the actual ''Super Meat Boy'' on December 2, 2010.<ref name="MTVtofuboy"/> The game's success spurred the development of the 1930s-style animated indie video game ''[[Cuphead]]'', which is also noted for its difficulty.<ref name="Kill Screen: Cuphead"/>

On August 29, 2014, Team Meat announced that a sequel, ''[[Super Meat Boy Forever]]'', was in development for smartphones, tablets, and Steam.<ref name="SMBforever"/> The game would remain in [[development hell|development limbo]] until the project was restarted in 2017, without the assistance of McMillen.<ref name="Forever2017"/><ref name="edtwit"/> In August 2017, the game was confirmed for release for Microsoft Windows, [[Nintendo Switch]], [[PlayStation 4]], [[Xbox One]], [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] systems. The [[Nintendo Switch]] and Windows versions were released in December 2020, with [[PlayStation 4]], [[Xbox One]], [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] versions following in 2021.<ref name="Foreverrelease"/>

Edmund McMillen left Team Meat and released the platforming game [[The End Is Nigh (video game)|The End Is Nigh]] with [[Tyler Glaiel]] in 2017,<ref>{{Cite web |last=rawmeatcowboy |date=2017-09-26 |title=Super Meat Boy Forever - Team Meat on why Edmund McMillen isn't involved |url=https://gonintendo.com/archives/268645-super-meat-boy-forever-team-meat-on-why-edmund-mcmillen-isn-t-involved |access-date=2026-09-30 |website=GoNintendo |language=en}}</ref> which has been described as a [[spiritual successor]] to Super Meat Boy.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rowen |first=Nic |title=Review: The End is Nigh |url=https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-the-end-is-nigh/ |access-date=2026-09-30 |website=Destructoid |language=en-US}}</ref>

A new title, ''[[Super Meat Boy 3D]]'', was announced at the Xbox Games Showcase in 2025.<ref name="SMB3Dannounce"/> The game is co-developed by Team Meat and Sluggerfly and was released on March 31, 2026.<ref name="SMB3Drelease"/>
