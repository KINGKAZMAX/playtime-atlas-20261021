'''''Half-Life: Alyx''''' is a 2020 [[Virtual reality game|virtual reality]] (VR) [[first-person shooter game]] developed and published by [[Valve Corporation|Valve]]. It was released for [[Windows]] on March 23, 2020, and for [[Linux]] on May 15, with support for most PC-compatible [[VR headsets]]. Players control [[Alyx Vance]] on a mission to seize a superweapon belonging to the alien [[Combine (Half-Life)|Combine]] before the events of ''[[Half-Life 2]]'' (2004). Like previous ''[[Half-Life (series)|Half-Life]]'' games, ''Alyx'' incorporates combat, puzzles and exploration. Players use VR to interact with the environment and fight enemies, using "gravity gloves" to snatch objects from a distance, similarly to the [[gravity gun]] from ''Half-Life 2''.

The previous ''Half-Life'' game, ''[[Half-Life 2: Episode Two|Episode Two]]'', was released in 2007 and ended on a [[cliffhanger]]. Valve made [[Unreleased Half-Life games|several attempts to develop further ''Half-Life'' games]], but could not settle on a direction. In the mid-2010s, Valve began experimenting with VR and identified demand for a major VR game. They experimented with prototypes using their various game series, such as ''[[Portal (series)|Portal]]'', and found that ''Half-Life''  best suited VR.

''Alyx'' entered production using Valve's new [[Source 2]] engine in 2016, with the largest team in Valve's history, including members of [[Campo Santo (company)|Campo Santo]], a studio acquired by Valve in 2018. VR affected almost every aspect of the design, including level design, combat, movement and pacing. Valve planned to launch ''Alyx'' alongside its [[Valve Index|Index]] headset in 2019, but delayed it to rewrite the story following internal feedback.

''Alyx'' received acclaim for its graphics, voice acting, narrative and atmosphere, and has been described as the first VR [[killer app]]. It was nominated for numerous awards and won "Best VR/AR" at the [[The Game Awards 2020|2020 Game Awards]]. Valve acknowledged that the audience for VR games was limited, and [[Gabe Newell]], Valve's president, described ''Alyx'' as a long-term investment into new technologies. As of 2024, it had sold more than two million copies. In 2026, Valve [[Porting|ported]] ''Alyx'' to its standalone [[Steam Frame]] headset.

=== Production ===
[[File:Air_Force_officer_using_Valve_Index.jpg|thumb|A [[U.S. Air Force]] member using a [[Valve Index]] VR headset]]Valve developed several VR prototypes, with three projects under development by 2017.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Campbell |first=Colin |date=February 10, 2017 |title=Valve is working on three full VR games |url=https://www.polygon.com/virtual-reality/2017/2/10/14580932/valve-is-working-on-three-full-vr-games |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170302161616/http://www.polygon.com/virtual-reality/2017/2/10/14580932/valve-is-working-on-three-full-vr-games |archive-date=March 2, 2017 |access-date=November 21, 2019 |website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Hollister |first=Sean |title=Valve, of Half-Life and Portal, is making 3 full VR games |url=https://www.cnet.com/news/valve-makers-of-half-life-and-portal-are-working-on-three-full-vr-games/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180310111923/https://www.cnet.com/news/valve-makers-of-half-life-and-portal-are-working-on-three-full-vr-games/ |archive-date=March 10, 2018 |access-date=November 21, 2019 |website=CNET}}</ref> They found the portal systems of their puzzle series ''[[Portal (series)|Portal]]'' were [[Virtual reality sickness|disorienting in VR]], but that the ''Half-Life'' systems were a natural fit.<ref name="The Final Hours Interview" /><ref name="Reeves" /> Walker said that ''Half-Life 3'' had been a "terrifyingly daunting prospect", and the team saw VR as a way to return to the series.<ref name="The Final Hours Interview" /> Additionally, they anticipated that fans would react negatively if ''Half-Life 3'' were a VR-only game and felt a prequel carried less weight.<ref name="Final Hours" />

Valve built prototypes using ''Half-Life 2'' assets and narrowed the gameplay systems to those that best fit VR.<ref name="The Final Hours Interview" /> VR affected almost every aspect of the design, including combat, level design and pacing. For example, shooting in VR, which requires the player to physically position their hand in space, is a different experience from aiming with traditional [[mouse and keyboard|mouse-and-keyboard]] controls.<ref name="Reeves" />

[[Mike Morasky]], the composer for ''[[Portal 2]]'' and ''[[Team Fortress 2]]'', wrote the score in consultation with [[Kelly Bailey (composer)|Kelly Bailey]], the composer for previous ''Half-Life'' games.<ref name="Reddit AMA" /> He cited [[industrial music]] by acts such as [[Nine Inch Nails]], [[the Prodigy]] and [[Skinny Puppy]] as inspiration.<ref name="Final Hours" />

''Half-Life: Alyx'' entered development around February 2016<ref>{{Cite news |last=Wilde |first=Thomas |date=April 4, 2020 |title=Behind the scenes of 'Half Life: Alyx': How Valve revived a classic franchise for the VR era |work=GeekWire |url=https://www.geekwire.com/2020/behind-scenes-half-life-alyx-valve-revived-classic-franchise-vr-era/ |access-date=April 6, 2020}}</ref> and entered full production later that year.<ref name="Verge release date" /> The team, comprising around 80 people,<ref>{{Cite news |last=Baker |first=Harry |date=January 5, 2020 |title=Half-Life Alyx AMA: Everything we learned |work=[[VentureBeat]] |url=https://venturebeat.com/2020/01/25/half-life-alyx-ama-everything-we-learned/ |url-status=live |access-date=March 27, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200207030512/https://venturebeat.com/2020/01/25/half-life-alyx-ama-everything-we-learned/ |archive-date=February 7, 2020}}</ref> was the largest in Valve's history, and included [[Campo Santo (company)|Campo Santo]], a studio Valve acquired in 2018.<ref name="PC Gamer 12" /><ref name="The Final Hours Interview" /> As Valve had repeatedly failed to see projects through, some staff were reluctant to join and many were skeptical that VR was the right direction.<ref name="Final Hours" /> 

Valve did not develop a non-VR version of ''Alyx'' as they were confident that it would only be possible in VR. They anticipated that fans would [[Video game modding|modify]] it to run without VR equipment. Though this bothered some on the team, Walker was not concerned, as he believed it would offer an inferior experience and demonstrate why they had chosen VR.<ref name="Kuchera-2020">{{Cite web |last=Kuchera |first=Ben |date=March 24, 2020 |title=Valve believes Half-Life: Alyx will be modded to play without VR |url=https://www.polygon.com/2020/3/24/21192250/half-life-alyx-valve-mods-vr-standard-display-mouse-keyboard |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200325150509/https://www.polygon.com/2020/3/24/21192250/half-life-alyx-valve-mods-vr-standard-display-mouse-keyboard |archive-date=March 25, 2020 |access-date=March 27, 2020 |website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]}}</ref> In late 2018, Valve held a company-wide [[playtest]] of the entire game. The results convinced them that VR had been the right choice.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The final weeks of development took place remotely due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="Final Hours" />

=== Movement ===
To mitigate the problem of [[motion sickness in VR]], Valve implemented several movement options. They cited inspiration from the 2018 VR game ''[[Budget Cuts]]'', which uses teleporting to move the player between locations. Valve had assumed that teleportation would damage the experience; however, though teleporting appears jarring when watching others use it, they found that players quickly became accustomed to it. According to Walker, "It recedes to the background of your mind, and you become much more focused on what you're doing with it."<ref name="McKeand-2020">{{Cite web|last=McKeand|first=Kirk|date=April 7, 2020|title="The Boneworks thing is weird" – Valve says it took more inspiration from Budget Cuts for Half-Life: Alyx|url=https://www.vg247.com/2020/04/07/budget-cuts-half-life-alyx-boneworks-valve/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200407142731/https://www.vg247.com/2020/04/07/budget-cuts-half-life-alyx-boneworks-valve/|archive-date=April 7, 2020|access-date=April 7, 2020|website=[[VG247]]}}</ref>

To disincentivize players from quickly teleporting through levels, Valve filled areas with elements to capture their attention and slow them down, such as threats, collectables, set pieces, or other points of interest.<ref name="McKeand-2020" /> To solve the problem of taller players having to crouch when moving through some spaces, Valve standardized the player's virtual body size when they teleport, effectively making every player the same height when teleporting.<ref name="Brown-2020">{{Cite magazine|last=Brown|first=Fraser|date=2020-04-07|title=Half-Life: Alyx's movement system was changed because tall testers kept getting stuck|url=https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/half-life-alyxs-movement-system-was-changed-because-tall-testers-kept-getting-stuck/|access-date=2021-03-13|magazine=PC Gamer|language=en-US}}</ref> They found that players did not notice this discrepancy as they were focused on moving to their goal.<ref name="Brown-2020" />

=== Combat ===
Every weapon is used one-handed, as Valve wanted players to have a hand free to interact with the world at all times.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Parlock|first=Joe|title=What we learned about ''Half-Life: Alyx'' from Valve's Reddit AMA|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/joeparlock/2020/01/22/what-we-learned-about-half-life-alyx-from-valves-reddit-ama/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200126073641/https://www.forbes.com/sites/joeparlock/2020/01/22/what-we-learned-about-half-life-alyx-from-valves-reddit-ama/|archive-date=January 26, 2020|access-date=January 26, 2020|website=Forbes}}</ref> The crowbar, a weapon from previous ''Half-Life'' games, was omitted as Valve could not make melee combat work in VR, and because players would accidentally catch it on objects in the game world as they moved, creating confusion. Additionally, players associated the crowbar with [[Gordon Freeman]], the protagonist of previous games. Valve wanted to create a different identity for Alyx, portraying her as a "hacker and tinkerer".<ref>{{Cite web|last=Grayson|first=Nathan|date=March 25, 2020|title=Why You Can't Use A Crowbar In Half-Life: Alyx|url=https://kotaku.com/why-you-cant-use-a-crowbar-in-half-life-alyx-1842491319|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200326000318/https://kotaku.com/why-you-cant-use-a-crowbar-in-half-life-alyx-1842491319|archive-date=March 26, 2020|access-date=March 26, 2020|website=[[Kotaku]]|language=en-us}}</ref> Other discarded weapon concepts include a trip mine, slingshot, shield and rocket launcher.<ref name="Final Hours" />

As players move at more realistic speeds in VR compared to typical FPS games, Valve had to adjust enemies to make combat fair and fun.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Antlions, returning enemies from ''Half-Life 2'', would quickly overwhelm players with their speed. The team slowed the antlions' movement and added the ability to shoot their legs off to slow them down.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Fast zombies and fast headcrabs, also introduced in ''Half-Life 2'', were cut as they were too frightening for some players in VR. According to the designer Dario Casali, "The shock of having [them] come around the corner and latch onto you before you'd even know what was going on was just too much."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rad |first=Lonnie |date=27 March 2020 |title=Half-Life: Alyx Cut Enemies That Were Too Scary In VR |url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-alyx-cut-enemies-that-were-too-scary-in-/1100-6475051/ |access-date=2021-03-20 |website=[[GameSpot]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Jeff ===
Jeff, a blind monster in chapter seven, came from the idea of forcing the player to share space with a frightening entity in VR. The team created several versions, including a Combine robot, before settling on a horror-influenced creature. Valve forced players to do things they did not want but still found enjoyable, such as leading them to the realization that they had to release Jeff after trapping him. They also gave the player more opportunities to escape when Jeff caught them, as near-deaths were exciting and too many deaths proved frustrating. Combat sequences with other enemies, designed to demonstrate Jeff's strength, were removed as they distracted from the tension.<ref name="Marks-2020a">{{Cite web |last=Marks |first=Tom |date=2020-03-26 |title=Valve reveals how ''Half-Life: Alyx'''s most terrifying chapter was made |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/valve-reveals-how-half-life-alyxs-most-terrifying-chapter-was-made-ign-first |access-date=2025-04-07 |website=[[IGN]] |language=en}}</ref>

The chapter contains more physics objects than the entirety of ''Half-Life 2'', and was set in a distillery to explain the abundance of bottles the player can throw to distract Jeff. It includes alien spores that cause Alyx to cough, drawing Jeff's attention. After playtesters instinctively covered their mouths, the team implemented this into the design. This also added strategy, as players have to keep one hand free to carry a bottle. At the end of the chapter, the player traps Jeff in a trash compactor. Most playtesters chose to activate the compactor and crush him.<ref name="Marks-2020a" />

=== Casting ===
[[Merle Dandridge]] reprised her role as Alyx for initial recording sessions in March 2019, but after playtests indicated that Alyx needed a younger voice, [[Ozioma Akagha]] was cast in September 2019.<ref name="Reddit AMA">{{Cite web|date=January 22, 2020|title=We're developers from the Half-Life: Alyx team. Ask us anything!|url=https://www.reddit.com/r/HalfLife/comments/esen9b/were_developers_from_the_halflife_alyx_team_ask/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200122170829/https://www.reddit.com/r/HalfLife/comments/esen9b/were_developers_from_the_halflife_alyx_team_ask/|archive-date=January 22, 2020|access-date=January 22, 2020|publisher=[[Reddit]]}}</ref> Akagha avoided using irritation in her performance, as "you don't want someone in your head that sounds irritated with you".<ref name="Final Hours" /> 

Additional actors include James Moses Black as Eli, replacing [[Robert Guillaume]], who died in 2017,<ref name="O'Connor" /> and [[Rhys Darby]] as Russell, who added comedic elements.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Returning actors include [[Tony Todd]] as the alien [[Vortigaunts]], [[Michael Shapiro (actor)|Mike Shapiro]] as the [[G-Man (Half-Life)|G-Man]], and [[Ellen McLain]] as the voice of the Combine broadcasts.<ref name="O'Connor">{{Cite web|last=O'Connor|first=James|title=Half-Life: Alyx Recasts Alyx, Eli, And Others, And Features Rhys Darby|url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-alyx-recasts-alyx-eli-and-others-and-fea/1100-6471659/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191122175936/https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-alyx-recasts-alyx-eli-and-others-and-fea/1100-6471659/|archive-date=November 22, 2019|access-date=November 22, 2019|website=GameSpot}}</ref> Shapiro recorded his lines in one 20-minute take, with pickups in 2019.<ref name="Olson-2020" /> [[Cissy Jones]] (Olga) and [[Rich Sommer]] (Larry, Russell's drone, and Combine Soldiers) were cast at the suggestion of the writer [[Sean Vanaman]], who had worked with them on Campo Santo's ''[[Firewatch]]'' (2016).<ref name="Olson-2020">{{Cite web|last=Olson|first=Matthew|title=Half-Life: Alyx Behind the Scenes, Part 2: "We're About to Pitch You This Ending, and You're Gonna Hate it"|url=https://www.usgamer.net/articles/half-life-alyx-behind-the-scenes-part-2-feature|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200325220923/https://www.usgamer.net/articles/half-life-alyx-behind-the-scenes-part-2-feature|archive-date=March 25, 2020|access-date=April 18, 2020|website=USGamer|date=March 25, 2020}}</ref>

=== Writing ===

| direction         = vertical

Wolpaw and Pinkerton turned down invitations to return to Valve early in the ''Alyx'' development.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Instead, Valve recruited Rob Yescombe of ''The Invisible Hours'', who worked on ''Alyx'' in 2017 and 2018. Yescombe's narrative was darker than previous ''Half-Life'' games, with scenes of dread, torture and horror. The antagonist was a female Combine officer named Hahn; in one proposed ending, Alyx would kill Hahn in revenge for torturing her father.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Yescombe also proposed an ending in which Alyx and the G-Man would travel back in time to the events of the first ''[[Half-Life (video game)|Half-Life]]'' to prevent Freeman from triggering the alien invasion.<ref name="Final Hours" />

After the company-wide playtest in 2018, feedback was overwhelmingly positive save for the story, which employees scored the lowest of any Valve game.<ref name="Final Hours" /> Morasky described it as "dark, serious and laborious", likening it to a [[Zack Snyder]] superhero film.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The designer Corey Peters said the feedback vindicated their misgivings.<ref name="Final Hours" />

Valve initially planned to launch ''Alyx'' alongside its [[Valve Index|Index]] VR headset in June 2019, but delayed it to address the story. They re-enlisted Wolpaw and Pinkerton to rewrite the plot and dialogue while preserving the gameplay, along with [[Jake Rodkin]] and Sean Vanaman, who had joined when Valve acquired Campo Santo.<ref name="Final Hours" /><ref>{{Cite web|last=Beckhelling|first=Imogen|title=Five cool things we learned from Valve's Half-Life: Alyx - Final Hours documentary|url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2020/07/13/five-cool-things-we-learned-from-valves-half-life-alyx-final-hours-documentary/|access-date=July 15, 2020|website=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]|date=July 13, 2020 }}</ref> Laidlaw, who retired in 2016, denied reports that he had provided consultation, saying he had confidence in Pinkerton and Wolpaw and that he did not want them "second-guessing" him.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Peel |first=Jeremy |date=March 1, 2023 |title='The narrative had to be baked into the corridors': Marc Laidlaw on writing ''Half-Life'' |language=en |website=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]] |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-narrative-had-to-be-baked-into-the-corridors-marc-laidlaw-on-writing-half-life |access-date=March 3, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230302232014/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/the-narrative-had-to-be-baked-into-the-corridors-marc-laidlaw-on-writing-half-life |archive-date=March 2, 2023 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The new writing team identified three problems.<ref name="Final Hours" /> First, the nature of a prequel meant that players knew the characters would survive, reducing suspense. Second, the story did not have an impact on the overall ''Half-Life'' story; the writers did not want ''Alyx'' to feel "just like a hermetically sealed short story in the world of ''Half-Life''".<ref>{{Cite web|last=Kuchera|first=Ben|title=''Half-Life: Alyx'''s wild ending, as explained by Valve's writers|url=https://www.polygon.com/2020/4/10/21212241/half-life-alyx-ending-explained-discussed-g-man-gordon-freeman-half-life-3|access-date=April 18, 2020|website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]|date=April 10, 2020}}</ref> Third, the game had to end with the powerful G-Man giving Alyx something for freeing him.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The team wanted the ending to have a meaningful impact and end the "narrative limbo" fans had been in since ''Episode Two''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=King |first=Jade |date=2021-03-23 |title=Valve Doesn't Want Half-Life Fans To Be Stuck In "Narrative Limbo" Anymore |url=https://www.thegamer.com/half-life-alyx-ending-robin-walker-narrative-limbo/ |access-date=2021-03-25 |website=TheGamer |language=en-US}}</ref>

Having Alyx and the G-Man travel forward in time and rescue Eli at the end of ''Episode Two'' was suggested by the character artist Jim Murray.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The team was reluctant, as this undid the ''Episode Two'' cliffhanger, but were intrigued by the questions it raised about the world and how it pushed the ''Half-Life'' story forward.<ref name="Olson-2020" /> The change required Valve to create new assets, such as the ''Episode Two'' White Forest helicopter hangar and models for Dog, the older Eli, and Gordon Freeman.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The [[red herring]], wherein Alyx believes she is rescuing Gordon Freeman before discovering the G-Man, was conceived by Vanaman late in production. As there was no character model for the Combine scientist Alyx overhears, the scene was animated in [[shadow play]].<ref name="Final Hours" />

While previous Valve games use [[silent protagonist]]s, the writers found that having Alyx speak improved the storytelling.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=MacGregor |first=Jody |date=November 21, 2019 |title=Alyx Vance will be voiced by a new actor in ''Half-Life: Alyx'' |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyx-cast/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191222130142/https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyx-cast/ |archive-date=December 22, 2019 |access-date=November 21, 2019 |magazine=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> They added radio dialog between Alyx and Russell as a simple way to "bring the energy up" whenever needed.<ref name="Final Hours" /> The final script was 280 pages long, compared to 128 pages for ''Half-Life 2'' and 18 for ''Half-Life''.<ref name="Final Hours" />

== Release ==
Valve announced ''Half-Life: Alyx'' in November 2019.<ref name="McWhertor2019" /><ref>{{Citation |last=O'Connor |first=James |title=Half-Life: Alyx Reveal Happening Today |date=November 21, 2019 |url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-alyx-reveal-happening-today/1100-6471545/ |publisher=[[GameSpot]] |access-date=November 21, 2019}}</ref> They waited until it was almost complete before announcing it, aiming to avoid the delays of previous games.<ref name="Final Hours" /> They were conscious that players, having waited years for a new ''Half-Life'' game, might be disappointed by a VR game, and tightly managed the announcement.<ref name="Final Hours" /> To promote ''Alyx'', Valve made the prior ''Half-Life'' games free on Steam from January 2020 until its release.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Paget|first=Mat|date=January 31, 2020|title=Half-Life Series Free To Play Ahead Of Half-Life: Alyx Release|url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-series-free-to-play-ahead-of-half-life-a/1100-6472925/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200202113930/https://www.gamespot.com/articles/half-life-series-free-to-play-ahead-of-half-life-a/1100-6472925/|archive-date=February 2, 2020|access-date=February 2, 2020|website=[[GameSpot]]}}</ref> Valve was due to showcase ''Alyx'' at the [[The Game Awards 2019|2019 Game Awards]] that December, but canceled hours prior, saying they were "hard at work on the game".<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-12-12 |title=Valve cancels Half-Life: Alyx appearance at The Game Awards |url=https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-cancels-half-life-alyx-appearance-at-the-game-awards/ |access-date=2022-06-13 |website=VGC |language=en-GB}}</ref>

''Alyx'' was released on March 23, 2020.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Chalk |first=Andy |date=February 13, 2020 |title=Half-Life: Alyx release date is March 23 |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyx-release-date-is-march-23/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200213201735/https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyx-release-date-is-march-23/ |archive-date=February 13, 2020 |access-date=February 25, 2020 |magazine=PC Gamer}}</ref> It was free to owners of [[Valve Index]] headsets or controllers.<ref name="engadget" /> Valve released a [[Linux]] version on May 15,  along with [[Vulkan]] rendering support for both platforms.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Fenlon |first=Wes |date=May 15, 2020 |title=Half-Life: Alyx now has Steam Workshop support and official mod tools |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyx-now-has-steam-workshop-support-and-official-mod-tools/ |access-date=May 16, 2020 |magazine=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> A pre-release build was mistakenly released on Steam; it included non-VR developer tools, allowing interactions such as picking up objects and firing weapons. However, most basic interactions, such as pressing buttons or filling Alyx's backpack, could not be completed with mouse-and-keyboard controls.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Wales|first=Matt|title=Here's a look at Half-Life: Alyx being played without VR|url=https://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2020-03-27-heres-a-look-at-half-life-alyx-being-played-without-vr|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200328203521/https://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2020-03-27-heres-a-look-at-half-life-alyx-being-played-without-vr|archive-date=March 28, 2020|access-date=March 28, 2020|website=Eurogamer|date=March 27, 2020}}</ref> Asked about plans for future ''Half-Life'' games, the designer David Speyrer said Valve was willing but were waiting for the reaction to ''Alyx''.<ref name="Verge release date" /> Walker said "we absolutely see ''Half-Life: Alyx'' as our return to this world, not the end of it".<ref name="Reeves" />

''Alyx'' is included with all [[Steam Frame]] headsets, a standalone VR headset released by Valve in September 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Judd |first=William |last2=Linneman |first2=John |date=2026-09-14 |title=Porting ''Half-Life: Alyx'' to Steam Frame: how Valve's engineers went from 'there's no way' to shipping standalone |url=https://www.digitalfoundry.net/features/porting-half-life-alyx-to-steam-frame-how-valves-engineers-went-from-theres-no-way-to-shipping-standalone |access-date=2026-09-15 |website=[[Digital Foundry]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Valve [[Porting|ported]] ''Alyx'' to run on the Steam Frame's [[ARM64]] processor and optimized it with [[foveated rendering]], whereby resolution is reduced in areas the player is not looking at.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hollister |first=Sean |date=2026-09-14 |title=Valve has ported Half-Life: Alyx to ARM, and it’s free with Steam Frame |url=https://www.theverge.com/games/994454/valve-half-life-alyx-standalone-arm-port |access-date=2026-09-15 |website=[[The Verge]] |language=en-US}}</ref> The Steam Frame can also stream the PC version using [[Steam Link#Virtual reality|Steam Link]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Orland|first=Kyle|date=2026-09-14|title=Steam Frame: the Ars Technica review|url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/09/steam-frame-the-ars-technica-review/|access-date=2026-09-14|website=[[Ars Technica]]|language=en}}</ref>

=== Mods ===
[[Mod (video games)|Mod]] tools for the Source 2 engine and Steam Workshop support for ''Half-Life: Alyx'' were released on May 15, 2020.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Clayton|first=Natalie|date=May 15, 2020|title=Half-Life: Alyx gets proper mod tools and Steam Workshop support|url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2020/05/16/half-life-alyx-gets-proper-mod-tools-and-steam-workshop-support/|access-date=May 15, 2020|website=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref> Valve announced plans to release a new Hammer level editor for Source 2, and planned to release a partial Source 2 [[software development kit]] for the updated features later, with the focus at launch on shipping and supporting ''Alyx''.<ref name="Reddit AMA" />

In March 2023, fans released the "NoVR" mod, which allows ''Alyx'' to be played without a VR headset. This also allows ''Alyx'' to run on less powerful devices, such as the [[Steam Deck]].<ref name="Orland-2023">{{Cite web |last=Orland |first=Kyle |date=2023-04-11 |title=Half-Life Alyx is now fully playable without VR hardware |url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2023/04/half-life-alyx-is-now-fully-playable-without-vr-hardware/ |access-date=2024-02-26 |website=Ars Technica |language=en-us}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=2023-07-06 |title=Half-Life: Alyx's NoVR mod is looking so good, I might finally play the game |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/half-life-alyxs-novr-mod-is-looking-so-good-i-might-finally-play-the-game/ |access-date=2024-02-26 |work=PC Gamer |language=en}}</ref> Reviewers found the mod removed immediacy and that mouse-and-keyboard controls could not replicate the dexterity of VR controllers.<ref name="Orland-2023" />
