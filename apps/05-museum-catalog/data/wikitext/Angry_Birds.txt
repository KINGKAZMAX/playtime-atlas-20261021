'''''Angry Birds''''' is a  [[video game]] series and [[media franchise]] created by Finnish game designer Jaakko Iisalo and owned by [[Rovio Entertainment]], a subsidiary of [[Sega]]. The franchise primarily follows a flock of [[Anthropomorphism|anthropomorphic]] flightless birds as they defend their nest of eggs from a species of green rotund pigs. A majority of the ''Angry Birds'' video games are [[puzzle video game|puzzle game]]s developed by Rovio Entertainment and released for [[mobile device]]s, while other games and [[Spinoff (media)|spinoffs]] have been developed by other studios and are in other genres such as [[Racing video game|racing]], [[Role-playing video game|role-playing]], and [[Tile-matching video game|tile-matching]]. The franchise also includes other media such as merchandise, theme parks, television, and feature films.

The first game in the series, ''[[Angry Birds (video game)|Angry Birds]]'', was developed and released in 2009. After its success, ''Angry Birds'' became their flagship franchise. Several puzzle successors followed, with the first spinoff, ''[[Bad Piggies]]'', releasing in 2012. The first media adaptation, television series ''[[Angry Birds Toons]]'', premiered in 2013. A theatrical film, ''[[The Angry Birds Movie]]'', was released in 2016, followed by a sequel, ''[[The Angry Birds Movie 2]]'', in 2019. A third film, ''[[The Angry Birds Movie 3]]'', is scheduled for 2026.

''Angry Birds'' is one of the [[List of highest-grossing media franchises|highest-grossing media franchises]] and the most downloaded mobile videogame series.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/105416-most-downloaded-mobile-videogame-series |title=Most downloaded mobile videogame series |access-date=19 May 2026 |newspaper=[[Guinness World Records]]}}</ref> Its first game is [[List of video games considered the best|considered one of the best games of all time]], and its early successors have received positive critical reception. The franchise is frequently referenced in popular culture and has had multiple cross-promotions.

===2009–2012: Conception and initial successors===
The first game in the series, ''[[Angry Birds (video game)|Angry Birds]]'', was conceptualised as a result of [[Rovio Entertainment]] only having the funds to produce one game. The idea originated from a sketch by designer Jaakko Iisalo,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2016/feb/23/how-we-made-angry-birds|title=How we made Angry Birds|first=Keith|last=Stuart|work=The Guardian |date=23 February 2016}}</ref> and its physics-based gameplay was inspired by the [[Adobe Flash]] game ''[[Crush the Castle]]'' (2009).<ref name="BadPiggiesWired">{{cite journal|url=https://www.wired.com/2012/10/bad-piggies-rovio/|title=Angry Birds Creator Has First Original Idea|journal=[[Wired (magazine)#Website|Wired]]|date=3 October 2012|access-date=5 March 2017|archive-date=8 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190708141908/https://www.wired.com/2012/10/bad-piggies-rovio/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Following the game's success, Rovio Entertainment released its first successor, titled ''Angry Birds Halloween'', exclusively for [[iOS]] on 21 October 2010.<ref name="Joystiq">{{cite web|url=http://www.joystiq.com/2010/10/20/angry-birds-halloween-edition-spooks-ipad-iphone/|title=Angry Birds Halloween edition spooks iPad, iPhone|author=Richard Mitchell|work=[[Joystiq]]|date=21 October 2010|accessdate=28 October 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101024223244/http://www.joystiq.com/2010/10/20/angry-birds-halloween-edition-spooks-ipad-iphone/|archive-date=24 October 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> The game was later renamed to ''[[Angry Birds Seasons]]'' and was ported to Android devices in December 2010 alongside a [[Christmas]]-themed [[Patch (computing)#Video games|update]].<ref name="Seasons">{{Cite web |last=McPherson |first=Frank |date=2010-12-02 |title=Angry Bird Seasons For Android |url=https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/angry-bird-seasons-for-android/ |access-date=2025-01-30 |language=en-US |website=[[Adweek]]}}</ref> Multiple other ''Angry Birds'' titles would follow ''Seasons''. In 2011, a [[tie-in]] with the film ''[[Rio (2011 film)|Rio]]'', titled ''[[Angry Birds Rio]]'', was developed by Rovio and released on 22 March. Following its release, ''[[The Independent]]'' reported that ''Angry Birds Rio'' had "the biggest launch ever for Rovio".<ref name="RioIndie">{{cite web |title=Angry Birds find success in 'Rio' |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/angry-birds-find-success-in-rio-2252647.html |website=[[The Independent]] |access-date=8 April 2025 |date=25 March 2011}}</ref> ''Rio'' is the first of numerous ''Angry Birds'' crossovers, being followed by ''[[Angry Birds Star Wars]]'' (2012) and ''[[Angry Birds Transformers]]'' (2014).<ref name="GameRant">{{cite web |last=Warner |first=Eric |title=Angry Birds' Impact on Mobile Gaming is Still Felt 15 Years Later |url=https://gamerant.com/angry-birds-15-year-anniversary-mobile-gaming-impact/ |website=[[Game Rant]] |access-date=8 April 2025 |date=11 December 2024}}</ref> The series' first [[Spinoff (media)|spin-off]], a game titled ''[[Bad Piggies]]'' starring the pigs, was released on 27 September 2012.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Moscaritolo |first1=Angela |title=Angry Birds Creator Launching New Game, Bad Piggies |url=https://uk.pcmag.com/mobile-games/60727/angry-birds-creator-launching-new-game-bad-piggies |website=[[PC Magazine]] |access-date=8 April 2025 |date=4 September 2012}}</ref>

games|update]] them to match modern standards.<ref name="ign-angrybirdsreturn">{{Cite web|title=The Original Angry Birds Games are Returning After Being Removed from Sale|website=[[IGN]]|date=22 June Rovio responded by announcing [[Video game remake|remakes]] of the discontinued titles.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Smith |first1=Olly |title=Rovio is bringing back some of its delisted Angry Birds games 2022.<ref>{{cite web |last=Sheehan |first=Gavin |title=Rovio Relaunches The Original Angry Birds For Mobile |url=https://bleedingcool.com/games/rovio-relaunches-the-original-angry-birds-for-mobile ''[[Angry Birds Toons]]'', a TV series based on the game, made its debut on 16 March 2013. ''Angry Birds Toons'' was released through third-party video distribution platforms, including Comcast's Xfinity On-Demand in the US, Samsung Smart TVs, and Roku set-top boxes. It is also available in a number of countries on traditional television broadcasts. ''Angry Birds Toons'' is still accessible on mobile devices by an additional ''Angry Birds Toons'' channel on all of the ''Angry Birds'' apps homescreens.<ref name="Forbes">{{cite web|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/johngaudiosi/2013/03/11/rovio-execs-explain-what-angry-birds-toons-channel-opens-up-to-its-1-7-billion-gamers/|title=Rovio Execs Explain What Angry Birds Toons Channel Opens Up To Its 1.7 Billion Gamers|first=John|last=Gaudiosi|work=Forbes|date=11 March 2012|access-date=12 March 2012|archive-date=13 March 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130313192720/http://www.forbes.com/sites/johngaudiosi/2013/03/11/rovio-execs-explain-what-angry-birds-toons-channel-opens-up-to-its-1-7-billion-gamers/|url-status=live}}</ref> The DVD versions of the TV series were released by [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]]. The series has a total of 3 seasons.

On 11 April 2014, Rovio released ''[[Piggy Tales]]'', a stop motion animated series. It tells the stories of the Minion Pigs' life.{{citation needed|date=April 2025}}

On 1 November 2014, Rovio released ''[[Angry Birds Stella (TV series)|Angry Birds Stella]]'', an animated series telling the stories of Stella's life and that of her friends on their own island.{{citation needed|date=April 2025}}

On 3 July 2015, Rovio released the ''Rocket Science Show'', an [[educational]] live-action-animated series, the [[NASA]] tell viewers facts about [[planets]] in the [[Solar System]].{{citation needed|date=April 2025}}

On 12 July 2015, ''Real Angry Birds'', inspired by the game ''Angry Birds'', premiered on ''[[Nat Geo Wild]]''.{{citation needed|date=April 2025}}

In 2016, Rovio released ''Fun Game Coding'', another [[educational]] live-action-animated series, containing insightful instructions from tech-savvy Rovio employees.{{citation needed|date=April 2025}}

Directed by the creator of ''[[The Marvelous Misadventures of Flapjack]]'', [[Thurop Van Orman]], the sequel breaks from the series' source material by having the Birds and Pigs ending their rivalry to form an alliance to battle a larger threat,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Aguilar |first1=Carlos |title=Interview: 'Angry Birds 2 Movie' Director And Producer On Defying The Franchise's Video Game Origins |url=https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/interview-angry-birds-2-director-and-producer-on-defying-the-franchises-video-game-origins-178262.html |website=CartoonBrew |access-date=11 September 2019 |date=15 August 2019 |archive-date=16 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190816200414/https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/interview-angry-birds-2-director-and-producer-on-defying-the-franchises-video-game-origins-178262.html |url-status=live }}</ref> as well as becoming one of the most critically well-received video game film adaptations ever made, garnering an unprecedented score of 73% from [[Rotten Tomatoes]].<ref>{{cite web |title=The Angry Birds Movie 2 |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_angry_birds_movie_2 |website=Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=11 September 2019 |archive-date=19 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210319201936/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_angry_birds_movie_2 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Angry Birds Land===
The game's characters have been used, officially or otherwise, in amusement park attractions. In September 2011, the [[Window of the World]] theme park in [[Changsha]], China opened an unlicensed ''Angry Birds'' attraction.<ref name="AB-China">{{cite news|url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/china-steals-angry-birds-for-theme-park/|title=China steals 'Angry Birds' for theme park|author=Charles Cooper|date=16 September 2011|access-date=12 November 2011|work=CBS News|archive-date=10 October 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131010065707/http://www.cbsnews.com/stories/2011/09/16/scitech/main20107294.shtml|url-status=live}}</ref> Visitors to the park use a large slingshot to launch stuffed versions of the bird characters at green balloons that represent the pigs.<ref name="AB-China" />

Upon learning of the attraction, Rovio Entertainment was reported to be considering working with the theme park to officially license it.<ref name="AB-Store" /> In March 2012, Rovio announced plans for an official ''Angry Birds'' Land, that opened on 28 April 2012, at the [[Särkänniemi]] adventure park in Finland.<ref>{{cite web |author=Andrew Wooden |url=http://www.t3.com/news/angry-birds-land-to-hit-theme-park-in-finland |title=Angry Birds Land to hit theme park in Finland |publisher=T3 |date=2 March 2012 |access-date=24 April 2012 |archive-date=9 May 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120509124133/http://www.t3.com/news/angry-birds-land-to-hit-theme-park-in-finland |url-status=live }}</ref> Angry Birds Land opened in May 2014 at Johor Bahru City Centre in Malaysia.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://scandasia.com/angry-birds-malaysia-theme-park-to-open-in-2014/ |title=Angry Birds Malaysia Theme Park To Open In 2014 « Scandasia |publisher=Scandasia.com |date=5 June 2013 |access-date=28 August 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130613085557/http://scandasia.com/angry-birds-malaysia-theme-park-to-open-in-2014/ |archive-date=13 June 2013 }}</ref>

The first Angry Birds themed park in the UK was created in [[Sundown Adventureland]], in the Nottinghamshire countryside.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/travel/shortcuts/2012/aug/22/angry-birds-uk-theme-park|title=Angry Birds gets its own UK theme park|last=Saner|first=Emine|date=22 August 2012|work=The Guardian|access-date=30 August 2017|language=en-GB|issn=0261-3077|archive-date=30 August 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170830154354/https://www.theguardian.com/travel/shortcuts/2012/aug/22/angry-birds-uk-theme-park|url-status=live}}</ref>

It was announced that UK theme park [[Thorpe Park]] would open their own Angry Birds Land themed area within the park, opening in May 2014, with a [[4D cinema]] showing a 10-minute ''Angry Birds'' film with 4D effects, and a slight re-theme of existing drop tower ride [[Detonator (Thorpe Park)|Detonator]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Thorpe Park Mania - Angry Birds Land |url=https://www.thorpeparkmania.co.uk/area/Angry+Birds+Land |access-date=2023-12-04 |website=www.thorpeparkmania.co.uk}}</ref> This Angry Birds Land operated until the end of the 2023 season, being rebranded for the 2024 season.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Chris |date=2023-11-18 |title=Big Easy Boulevard to replace Angry Birds Land at Thorpe Park Resort |url=https://www.attractionsource.com/2023/11/18/big-easy-boulevard-to-replace-angry-birds-land-at-thorpe-park-resort/ |access-date=2023-12-04 |website=Attraction Source |language=en-GB}}</ref>

===Angry Birds Activity Parks===
Rovio opened "Activity Parks" of the game. The first one opened in [[Lightwater Valley]], England, with dance floors, playgrounds that include slides, and touchscreen devices for visitors to play Angry Birds.{{citation needed|date=May 2020}} The attraction also opened in [[Vuokatti]], Finland, with obstacle courses and races,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.angrybirdsvuokatti.fi/en/Activity-Park/Angry-Birds-Arena|title=Angry Birds Arena|publisher=Angry Birds Vuokatti|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140516183258/http://www.angrybirdsvuokatti.fi/en/Activity-Park/Angry-Birds-Arena|archive-date=16 May 2014|url-status=unfit<!--Finnish-->}}</ref> and a separate "Angry Birds Town" with kids' cars.

A park opened in Malaysia, but closed after six years on 5 April 2021 due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Cho|first=Janet|date=2021-03-16|title=Angry Birds Activity Park In JB To Close Its Doors On 5th April|url=https://thesmartlocal.com/malaysia/angry-birds-activity-park-closure/|website=TheSmartLocal Malaysia – Travel, Lifestyle, Culture & Language Guide|language=en-GB|archive-date=10 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210710083023/https://thesmartlocal.com/malaysia/angry-birds-activity-park-closure/|url-status=live}}</ref>

It also opened in the [[Space Center Houston]].{{citation needed|date=November 2025}}

===Education===
Rovio also launched the Angry Birds Playground in partnership with [[University of Helsinki]]. Based on the Finnish national curriculum the program covers maths, science, music, language, arts & crafts, physical education and social interaction. Focused on preschoolers of kindergarten age, the program introduced interactive learning material including books, posters, games and digital content.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/technology/appsblog/2013/sep/16/angry-birds-playground-education-rovio|title=Angry Birds Playground: Rovio catapults gaming into the classroom|last=Dredge|first=Stuart|date=16 September 2013|work=The Guardian|access-date=30 July 2018|archive-date=30 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180730170551/https://www.theguardian.com/technology/appsblog/2013/sep/16/angry-birds-playground-education-rovio|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2015, a spin-off from the Rovio Angry Birds Playground was established as [https://funacademy.fi/ Fun Academy] by co-founder and CEO Sanna Lukander, former Rovio's vice president of learning and book publishing, and co-founder {{interlanguage link|Peter Vesterbacka|fi|Peter_Vesterbacka}}, former Rovio's 'Mighty Eagle'.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20160128005521/en/Rovio-Entertainment-Rovio-Spins-Education-Business-Create|title=Rovio Entertainment: Rovio Spins off Education Business to Create Fun Academy|date=28 January 2016|website=Business Wire|access-date=30 July 2018|archive-date=30 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180730170718/https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20160128005521/en/Rovio-Entertainment-Rovio-Spins-Education-Business-Create|url-status=live}}</ref> Fun Academy is currently{{when|date=December 2022}} present in 9 countries across the world.{{citation needed|date=December 2022}}

==Criticism and controversies==
===Leaking user data===
In January 2014, it was revealed that ''Angry Birds'' was considered a "leaky app" and was used to collect data about its users including their sexual orientation and location by the [[National Security Agency]] (NSA) and [[Government Communications Headquarters|GCHQ]]. In retaliation, anti-NSA hackers defaced Rovio's website. Claims were made by [[Edward Snowden]] that the app was ''leaky'' and that they were capable of [[siphon]]ing data.

According to ''[[The Register]]'', the information was leaked through the in-game advertisement code like that embedded by [[One by AOL|Millennial Media]]: "Millennial's tracking software generates a personal record for each user that can store information from their political affiliation and sexual orientation to whether their marital state was single, married, divorced, engaged or 'swinger'. This information is used to target in-app ads more effectively, and can be collected by UK and US intelligence agencies for analysis."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theregister.co.uk/2014/01/27/leaking_smartphone_apps_nsa_gchq/|title=Fancy a little kinky sex? GCHQ+NSA will know – thanks to ANGRY BIRDS|work=The Register|access-date=24 August 2017|archive-date=29 June 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170629055158/https://www.theregister.co.uk/2014/01/27/leaking_smartphone_apps_nsa_gchq/|url-status=live}}</ref> In a statement to the press, Rovio denied that they were providing any information to the intelligence agencies, but did not exclude the possibility that their advertisers might do so.<ref name="spybirds" />

Following this revelation, on 29 January, the Angry Birds site was defaced by hackers who replaced it with "Spying Birds" via a [[DNS hijacking]] attack. According to Rovio, "The defacement was caught in minutes and corrected immediately".<ref name="spybirds">{{cite web|url=http://www.pcworld.com/article/2092560/hackers-deface-angry-birds-website-following-nsa-spying-claims.html|title=Hackers transform Angry Birds website into 'Spying Birds' following NSA claims|author=Lucian Constantin|date=29 January 2014|work=PC World|access-date=31 January 2014|archive-date=31 January 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140131094319/http://www.pcworld.com/article/2092560/hackers-deface-angry-birds-website-following-nsa-spying-claims.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The [[Syrian Electronic Army]] tweeted after the incident that the attack had been carried out by "a friend" of theirs.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.pcmag.com/article2/0,2817,2430080,00.asp|title=Angry Birds Website Defaced After NSA Spying Allegations|work=PC Magazine|date=29 January 2014 |access-date=24 August 2017|archive-date=17 July 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170717132920/http://www.pcmag.com/article2/0,2817,2430080,00.asp|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Gaza war ads===
During the [[Gaza war]], graphic ads supporting Israel showed up in ''Angry Birds'' and other video games for children. The [[Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Israel)|Israeli Foreign Ministry]] said it had instructed advertisers to block the ads for viewers under the age of 18. Rovio subsequently banned the ads manually.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Satter |first1=Raphael |last2=Paul |first2=Katie |last3=Dang |first3=Sheila |last4=Satter |first4=Raphael |title=Graphic pro-Israel ads make their way into children's video games |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/graphic-pro-israel-ads-make-their-way-into-childrens-video-games-2023-10-30/ |work=Reuters |date=30 October 2023 |language=en}}</ref>
