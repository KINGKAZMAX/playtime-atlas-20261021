'''''Disco Elysium''''' is a 2019 [[role-playing video game]] by {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}. The game was written and designed by a team led by [[Estonia]]n novelist [[Robert Kurvitz]] and executive producer [[Kaur Kender]], featuring a [[painterly]] art style and music by the English band [[Sea Power]]. It was released for [[Windows]] in October 2019 and [[macOS]] in April 2020. An expanded version of the game featuring full voice acting and new content, subtitled ''The Final Cut'', was released for consoles in 2021 alongside a free update for the PC versions. In August 2025, the game was adapted for [[Android (system)|Android]].

''Disco Elysium'' follows a troubled [[detective]] with no memory of his identity or the world around him. As he investigates a murder with a detective from another precinct, the player can piece together the protagonist's identity and discover what led him to his state. ''Disco Elysium'' is a non-traditional role-playing game featuring little combat. Instead, events are resolved through skill checks and dialogue trees using a system of 24 skills representing the protagonist's different aspects and personalities, each of which can speak directly to the player to influence their decisions. The game is based on a [[tabletop role-playing game]] setting that Kurvitz had created before forming {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} in 2016 to adapt it into a video game. This is the second time the ''Elysium'' setting is explored, following the 2013 novel ''Sacred and Terrible Air''.

''Disco Elysium'' received critical acclaim upon its release, winning numerous awards, notably at the [[The Game Awards 2019|Game Awards 2019]] (Best Independent Game, Best Narrative, Best Role Playing Game, Fresh [[Indie game|Indie Game]]). It has sold more than five million copies, and is regarded by some critics as one of the [[greatest video games of all time]],<ref>{{Cite web |author=Slant Staff |date=2020-04-13 |title=The 100 Best Video Games of All Time |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/the-100-best-video-games-of-all-time/ |access-date=2025-04-08 |website=[[Slant Magazine]] |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |author=I. G. N. Staff |date=2021-12-31 |title=The Top 100 Video Games of All Time |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/the-best-100-video-games-of-all-time |access-date=2025-04-08 |website=IGN |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Best Games on PC |url=https://www.metacritic.com/browse/game/pc/ |access-date=2025-04-08 |website=[[Metacritic]] |language=en}}</ref> and a strong example of [[video games as an art form]]. Though a success, conflicts at {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} around 2021 led several of the lead developers and writers, including Kurvitz and Kender, to leave and form their own studios. {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} released ''[[Zero Parades: For Dead Spies]]'' in May 2026, a game in the same style as ''Disco Elysium'', while as of October 2024, at least four different studios employing former {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} staff had announced projects to develop [[spiritual successor]]s to ''Disco Elysium''.

==Gameplay==
[[File:Disco Elysium interface.jpg|thumb|left|alt=The protagonist of Disco Elysium interacts with a man in a run-down area.|Gameplay screenshot showing conversations in white and choices in orange, atop the current scene the character is in]]
''Disco Elysium'' is a [[role-playing video game]] that features an [[open world]] and dialogue-heavy gameplay mechanics.<ref name=PCGUS/><ref name="techspot">{{Cite web |last=Cohen |first=Coberly |date=16 October 2019 |title=Ambitious open-world RPG Disco Elysium lets you take on the role of a mentally unstable detective |url=https://www.techspot.com/news/82354-ambitious-open-world-rpg-disco-elysium-you-take.html |access-date=5 April 2020 |website=Techspot |archive-date=16 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191016095701/https://www.techspot.com/news/82354-ambitious-open-world-rpg-disco-elysium-you-take.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The game is presented in an [[Isometric video game graphics|isometric perspective]] in which the [[player character]] is controlled.<ref name=USgamer/> The player takes the role of a detective, who suffers from alcohol and drug-induced amnesia, on a murder case.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lang |first=Brad |date=17 October 2019 |title=Disco Elysium Review – Stayin' Alive |url=https://www.criticalhit.net/review/disco-elysium-review-stayin-alive/ |access-date=18 October 2019 |website=Critical Hit |archive-date=18 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191018094204/https://www.criticalhit.net/review/disco-elysium-review-stayin-alive/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The player can move the detective about the screen to interact with [[non-player character]]s (NPC) and highlighted objects or move onto other screens. Early in the game, the player gains a partner, [[Kim Kitsuragi]], another detective who acts as the protagonist's voice of professionalism and who offers advice or support in certain dialogue options.

The gameplay features no combat in the traditional sense; instead, combat is handled through [[Skill (role-playing games)|skill]] checks and [[dialogue tree]]s.<ref name="gamecrate">{{Cite web |last=Woo |first=Justin |date=16 October 2019 |title=Disco Elysium: Character generation, skill checks and gameplay strategy |url=https://www.gamecrate.com/disco-elysium-character-generation-skill-checks-and-gameplay-strategy/24424 |access-date=5 April 2020 |website=GameCrate |archive-date=17 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191017134959/https://www.gamecrate.com/disco-elysium-character-generation-skill-checks-and-gameplay-strategy/24424 |url-status=dead }}</ref> There are four primary attributes in the game: [[Intellect]], [[Psyche (psychology)|Psyche]], [[Physical fitness|Physique]], and [[Motor skill|Motorics]]. Each attribute has six distinct secondary skills for a total of 24, which the player can improve through skill points earned from levelling up.<ref name="rockpapershotgunskills">{{Cite news |last=Toms |first=Ollie |date=14 October 2019 |title=Disco Elysium skills & character creation: Intellect, Psyche, Physique, Motorics, and the 24 skills explained |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2019/10/14/disco-elysium-skills-character-creation-intellect-psyche-physique-motorics-and-the-24-skills-explained/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191029031440/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2019/10/14/disco-elysium-skills-character-creation-intellect-psyche-physique-motorics-and-the-24-skills-explained/ |archive-date=29 October 2019 |access-date=5 April 2020 |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref> Upgrading these skills helps the player character pass skill checks, which are based on a random roll of two six-sided dice, but potentially result in negative effects and character quirks, discouraging [[minmaxing]]. For instance, a player character with high Drama may be able to detect and fabricate lies effectively, but may also become prone to hysterics and paranoia. Likewise, high Electrochemistry shields the player character from the negative effects of drugs and provides knowledge on them, but may also lead to substance abuse and other self-destructive, self-gratifying behaviours.<ref name=rockpapershotgunskills/> The choice of clothing that the player equips on the player-character can also impart both positive and negative effects on certain skills.<ref name="gamecrate" />

''Disco Elysium'' features a secondary skill system known as the "Thought Cabinet". Thoughts are unlocked through conversations with other characters and internal dialogues within the protagonist's mind. The player is then able to "internalize" a thought through a certain amount of in-game hours, which, once completed, grants the player character benefits but also occasionally negative effects, a concept that {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} compared to the trait system used in the [[Fallout (franchise)|''Fallout'' series]].<ref name="zaum hungarian interview">{{Cite web |last=Metsniit |first=Mikk |date=31 March 2017 |title=The Hungarian Interview |url=https://zaumstudio.com/2017/03/31/the-hungarian-interview/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201202012348/https://zaumstudio.com/2017/03/31/the-hungarian-interview/ |archive-date=2 December 2020 |access-date=8 April 2020 |website={{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} Studio}}</ref> A limited number of slots are available in the Thought Cabinet at the start, though more can be gained with experience levels. For example, an early possible option for the Thought Cabinet is the "Hobocop" thought, in which the character ponders the option of [[homelessness|living on the streets]] to save money, which reduces the character's composure with other NPCs while the thought is being internalized. When the character has completed the Hobocop thought, it allows the player to receive twice as much money from collecting and recycling trash.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Toms |first=Ollie |date=15 October 2019 |title=Disco Elysium Thought Cabinet: the Thoughts system explained |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]] |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2019/10/15/disco-elysium-thought-cabinet-the-thoughts-system-explained/#what-is-the-thought-cabinet |access-date=5 April 2020 |archive-date=29 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191029031349/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2019/10/15/disco-elysium-thought-cabinet-the-thoughts-system-explained/#what-is-the-thought-cabinet |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="GSpot" /> Later, the player may opt to spend a skill point to "forget" completed thoughts to make space for different ones. This removes it from the Thought Cabinet for the rest of the playthrough, along with any bonuses gained from its completion.

The 24 skills also play into the dialogue trees, creating a situation where the player-character may have internal debates with one or more aspects of either mind or body, creating the idea that the player is communicating with a fragmented persona. These internal conversations may provide suggestions or additional insight that can guide the player into actions or dialogue with the game's non-playable characters,<ref>{{Cite book |title=Critical Hits: Writers Playing Video Games |last=Adjei-Brenyah |first=Nana Kwame |date=2023 |publisher=[[Graywolf Press]] |isbn=978-1-64445-261-5 |location=Minneapolis |pages=23{{ndash}}25 |author-link=Nana Kwame Adjei-Brenyah |editor-last=Lennon |editor-first=J. Robert |editor-link=J. Robert Lennon |chapter=This Kind of Animal |lccn=2022952326 |oclc=1355033155 |editor-last2=Machado |editor-first2=Carmen Maria |editor-link2=Carmen Maria Machado |chapter-url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Dmy8EAAAQBAJ&pg=PT34}}</ref> depending on the skill points invested into the skill. For example, Inland Empire, a subskill of Psyche, is described by {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} as a representation of the intensity of the soul, and may come into situations where the player-character may need to pass themselves off under a fake identity with the conviction behind that stance, should the player accept this suggestion when debating with Inland Empire.<ref>{{Cite web |last=O'Keefe |first=David |date=10 September 2018 |title=Your skills talk to you in Disco Elysium, an inventive RPG that keeps impressing |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/your-skills-talk-to-you-in-disco-elysium-an-inventive-rpg-that-keeps-impressing/ |access-date=7 April 2020 |journal=[[PC Gamer]] |archive-date=13 September 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180913084250/https://www.pcgamer.com/your-skills-talk-to-you-in-disco-elysium-an-inventive-rpg-that-keeps-impressing/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Synopsis==
[[File:Star and Antlers Flag.svg|thumb|The star and antlers [[Communist symbolism|flag of communists]] in the world of ''Disco Elysium'']]

===Setting===
{{Redirect|Revachol|the French anarchist|Ravachol}}
''Disco Elysium'' takes place in the fictional world of Elysium, developed by Kurvitz and his team in the years prior, which includes over six thousand years of history; the genre is described as "fantastic realism".<ref name="zaum hungarian interview" /> The narrative was constructed with attention to [[Karl Marx]]'s [[historical materialism|theory of history]], which posits that the material production of life directly conditions the political, artistic, religious, and philosophical superstructure of society in itself.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Gordon |first=Lewis |date=2021-04-25 |title=The materialism and magic of the surprise RPG hit 'Disco Elysium' |url=https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/the-materialism-magic-of-the-surprise-rpg-hit-disco-elysium |access-date=2024-01-21 |website=[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]] |language=en}}</ref>

The game takes place in the year '51 of the Current Century. Elysium is made of "isolas", masses of land and sea that are separated from each other by the Pale, an inscrutable, mist-like "connective tissue" in which the laws of reality break down. Prolonged exposure to the Pale can cause mental instability and eventually death, and traversing the Pale, which is typically done with [[airship|aerostatics]], is heavily regulated due to the danger.<ref name="gamerant">{{cite web |last=Trahan |first=Philip |date=24 January 2020 |title=The World of Disco Elysium Explained |url=https://gamerant.com/disco-elysium-world-explained/ |access-date=5 April 2020 |website=[[Game Rant]] |archive-date=20 May 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200520001516/https://gamerant.com/disco-elysium-world-explained/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Events in the game take place in the impoverished district of Martinaise within the city of Revachol on the isola of Insulinde, the "New New World".<ref name="PCGUS" /><ref name=gamerant/> Forty-nine years before the events of the game, a wave of communist revolutions swept multiple countries; the Suzerainty of Revachol, a monarchy that up to that point had been Elysium's pre-eminent superpower, was overthrown and replaced by a commune. Six years later, the Commune of Revachol was toppled by an invading alliance of moralist-capitalist nations called "the Coalition". Revachol was designated a Special Administrative Region and remains firmly under Coalition control decades later. One of the few governmental responsibilities that the Coalition concedes to the people of Revachol is policing, which is carried out by the Revachol Citizens Militia (RCM), a voluntary citizens' brigade turned semi-professional police force.

===Plot===
The player character wakes up in a trashed hostel room in Martinaise with a severe [[hangover]] and no memory of his own identity due to an ostensible extreme case of [[drug-induced amnesia]]. He meets Lieutenant [[Kim Kitsuragi]], who informs him that they have been assigned to investigate the death of a hanged man in an empty lot behind the hostel. The victim's identity is unclear and initial analysis of the scene indicates that he was [[lynching|lynched]] by a group of people. The detectives explore the rest of the district, following up on leads while helping residents with a variety of tasks. In the course of the investigation, the player character learns that he is a decorated RCM detective, Lieutenant Harrier "Harry" Du Bois. Harry experienced an event several years ago that began a [[midlife crisis]], and on the night he was assigned to the hanged man case, he snapped and embarked on a self-destructive three-day drinking spree around Martinaise. When the player goes to bed on the first night in-game, Harry has a [[nightmare]] where he discovers himself as the hanged man underneath a [[disco ball]]. When he talks to his own dead body, it tells him that everything is hopeless and he will inevitably fail to solve the case or put his life back together.

Harry and Kim discover the hanged man killing is connected to an ongoing strike by the Martinaise dockworkers' union against the Wild Pines Group, a major logistics corporation. They interview union boss Evrart Claire and Wild Pines negotiator Joyce Messier. Joyce reveals that the hanged man was Colonel Ellis "Lely" Kortenaer, the commander of a squad of [[mercenaries]] sent by Wild Pines to [[Strikebreaker|break the strike]]. She warns that the rest of the mercenaries have gone rogue and will likely seek retribution for Lely's death.

Harry and Kim discover that Lely was killed before the hanging, and the Hardie Boys, a group of dockworker [[vigilante]]s who act as the de facto peacekeepers of Martinaise, claim responsibility for the murder. They assert that Lely raped a hostel guest named Klaasje. When questioned, Klaasje reveals that Lely was shot in the mouth while the two were having consensual sex. Unable to figure out the origin of the bullet and fearful of the authorities due to her past as a [[corporate spy]], Klaasje enlisted the help of a union sympathiser named Ruby, who staged Lely's hanging with the rest of the Hardie Boys. The detectives find Ruby hiding in an abandoned building, where she incapacitates them with a radio wave-based device normally used to aid in traversing the Pale. She claims that the cover-up was Klaasje's idea and has no idea who shot Lely. Harry manages to overcome the Pale device and contemplates arresting Ruby, but she believes Harry to be a corrupt cop and either escapes or kills herself, depending on the player's skills and choices.

Returning to their hostel, the detectives intercede in a standoff between the rogue mercenaries and the Hardie Boys. A firefight breaks out and Harry is wounded, leaving him unconscious for several days. Depending on the player's actions, none, some, or all of the Hardie Boys and mercenaries may die, and Kim may also be hospitalized, in which case street urchin [[Cuno (Disco Elysium)|Cuno]] offers to take his place as Harry's partner. The detectives chase down their remaining leads and determine that the shot that killed Lely came from an old fortress on an islet just off of Martinaise's shoreline.

The detectives explore the ruins and find the shooter, a former [[commissar]] of the Revachol communist army named Iosef Lilianovich Dros who survived the collapse of the Revacholian commune because he deserted his post. Iosef reveals that he shot Lely with his sniper rifle in a fit of anger and jealousy; his motivations were born out of his bitterness towards the capitalist system Lely represented, as well as sexual envy for Klaasje. The detectives arrest him for the murder. At this point, an insectoid [[cryptid]] known as the Insulindian Phasmid appears from the reeds, whose existence the player has the choice to investigate throughout the game. It is implied that the Phasmid indirectly set off the chain of events leading to the murder, as the psychoactive chemicals it exudes inadvertently affected the man's mind for years, stoking his fanaticism and resentment. Harry may have a psychic conversation with the Phasmid, who tells him that it is fearful of the notion of his unstable mind, but awed by his ability to continue existing. It also implies to Harry that the Pale is a consequence of human perception and self-reflection that threatens to consume the world. Before leaving, it comforts Harry, telling him to move on from the wreck of his life.

Harry and his partner are confronted by his old squad upon their return to Martinaise. They reflect on Harry's actions during the game, particularly whether he solved the case and how he handled the mercenaries. Lieutenant Jean Vicquemare, Harry's usual partner, confirms that Harry's emotional breakdown was the result of his fiancée leaving him years ago. In the best possible outcome, the squad expresses hope that Harry's state will improve in the future, and invites him and either Kim or Cuno to join a special RCM unit.

The group then created a collective of artists and musicians, which included oil painter Aleksander Rostov, the lead artist of ''Disco Elysium'', to expand upon the work of that night and developed a Kurvitz eventually managed to overcome his alcoholism and helped Kender in his own recovery. As a sign of gratitude, Kender suggested to Kurvitz that instead of pursuing a novel, he could try capturing his world as a video game to draw a larger interest. Kurvitz had no experience in video games, but once he saw the artwork of the game's setting easily fitting into an [[isometric RPG|isometric format]], as well as Rostov's agreement that they might as well continue taking the risk of failing on a video game together, Kurvitz proceeded with the idea.<ref name="gamesradar conception" /> Kurvitz wrote a concise description of what the game would be: "D&D meets '70s cop-show, in an original 'fantastic realist' setting, with swords, guns and motor-cars. Realised as an isometric CRPG – a modern advancement on the legendary ''[[Planescape: Torment]]'' and ''[[Baldur's Gate]]''. Massive, reactive story. Exploring a vast, poverty-stricken ghetto. Deep, strategic combat."<ref name="gamesradar conception" /> Kender was impressed by the strong statement and invested into the game's development, with additional investment coming from friends and family.<ref name="gamesradar conception" /> The game was announced as an upcoming 2017 game under the title ''No Truce With the Furies'',<ref name="gamesradar conception" /> taken from the poem "Reflections" by [[R.S. Thomas]] and published in Thomas' ''No Truce with the Furies'' in 1995.<ref name="egm title" /> Although the name was changed, the poem would later find its own place in the game as an excerpt of the poem appears in the opening:<blockquote>''"The Furies are at home in the mirror, it is their address. Even the clearest water, if deep enough can drown.''

|access-date=3 July 2021 |website=[[USgamer]] |language=en}}</ref>

Kurvitz said that an aim was to have a full, complex depth of choices and outcomes, limited by the practicalities of game development. Knowing they could not realistically cover all possible choices, Kurvitz and his team instead focused more on what he called "microreactivity", small acts and decisions the player may make such as an embarrassing comment, and how that may propagate throughout events. The dialogue of the player's various skills helped then to provide critique and internalisation of how these small decisions had larger effects on the game world, so that the player would become more aware of such choices in the future.<ref name="wired story">{{Cite magazine |last=Russell |first=Laurence |date=7 June 2021 |title=The Incredible, Absurd World of Disco Elysium: The Final Cut |url=https://www.wired.com/story/disco-elysium-the-final-cut/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211109102433/https://www.wired.com/story/disco-elysium-the-final-cut/ |archive-date=9 November 2021 |magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]] |access-date=7 June 2021}}</ref> An additional factor in writing was the recognition that there was no real solution to the game; while the player may resolve some portions of the story, the primary case is nearly unworkable, similar to the rest of Revachol. They created the companion Kim as a no-nonsense character to help keep the player on track of resolving some part of the game and recognising that there were some story threads they simply could not fix or resolve.<ref name="wired story" />

As originally planned, the game was to focus on action in a single city location to make the 2017 release. However, as {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} had indicated to investors that this was to be a game that spanned a larger world, they found the need to spread beyond that single location, forcing them to delay the game's release, along with the name change to ''Disco Elysium''.<ref name="gamesradar conception" /> This title plays on a few double meanings related to the word "disco"; in one sense, it refers to ideas that briefly gain the spotlight before burning out similar to the fad of [[disco music]], and reflected in the protagonist's clothing style, while in a more literal sense, "disco" is [[Latin]] for "I learn", thus reflecting on the protagonist's overcoming his amnesia to learn about the world of Elysium. Kurvitz had always anticipated the ''No Truce'' title to be more of a working title and wanted to reserve it for when they had bundled ''Disco Elysium'' with a second planned game.<ref name="egm title">{{Cite magazine |last=O'Keefe |first=David |date=20 January 2020 |title=The Invisible Art of Game Titles |url=https://egmnow.com/the-invisible-art-of-game-titles/ |magazine=[[Electronic Gaming Monthly]] |access-date=8 April 2020 |archive-date=11 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200111172653/https://egmnow.com/the-invisible-art-of-game-titles/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Though {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} had initially planned to publish the game through [[Humble Bundle]], they ultimately chose to self-publish it.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Tarason |first=Dominic |date=9 March 2018 |title=No Truce With The Furies gets a mad new title and trailer |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]] |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2018/03/09/no-truce-with-the-furies-gets-a-mad-new-title-and-trailer/ |access-date=16 October 2019 |archive-date=17 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191017031304/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2018/03/09/no-truce-with-the-furies-gets-a-mad-new-title-and-trailer/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

In March 2025, {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} announced an official mobile version of ''Disco Elysium'' for [[Android (operating system)|Android]], aimed at engaging more casual users. The game released on Google Play on August 5, 2025,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Garcia |first=Carley |date=2025-03-13 |title=Disco Elysium is Coming to Another Platform |url=https://gamerant.com/disco-elysium-mobile-release-date/ |access-date=2025-03-14 |website=Game Rant |language=en}}</ref> taking the form of a [[visual novel]].

===Influences and inspirations===
The game's art, drawn mostly in a painterly style, was led by Aleksander Rostov, while the soundtrack was written and recorded by the English band [[Sea Power]] (at the time of the game's publication, known as British Sea Power).<ref name="credits">{{cite video game |title=Disco Elysium |developer={{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} |scene=Ending Credits |date=15 October 2019}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Lipscombe |first=Daniel |date=22 November 2019 |title=Disco Elysium comes to life once the talking stops: {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} details its approach to creating a truly expressive RPG |url=https://www.gamesradar.com/disco-elysium-comes-to-life-once-the-talking-stops-zaum-details-its-approach-to-creating-a-truly-expressive-rpg/ |access-date=9 December 2019 |website=[[GamesRadar+]] |archive-date=23 November 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191123051632/https://www.gamesradar.com/disco-elysium-comes-to-life-once-the-talking-stops-zaum-details-its-approach-to-creating-a-truly-expressive-rpg/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Members of {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} were longtime fans of the band, and approached the group directly to request the use of their music in ''Disco Elysium''.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=2020-01-17 |title=Disco Elysium – The Final Cut – Disco Elysium – FAQ – Inspiration & Recommendations – Steam News |url=https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/632470/view/3334287173823797600 |access-date=2025-04-19 |website=store.steampowered.com |language=en}}</ref> The band agreed to produce [[Disco Elysium (soundtrack)|the soundtrack]], contributing a mix of original compositions and instrumental remixes of previously released songs.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Reynolds |first=Ollie |date=2022-10-12 |title=Meet Sea Power, The Talent Behind Disco Elysium's Exquisite Music |url=https://www.nintendolife.com/features/meet-sea-power-the-talent-behind-disco-elysiums-exquisite-music |access-date=2025-01-08 |website=Nintendo Life |language=en-GB}}</ref> Several tracks from Sea Power's discography were reworked for the game's soundtrack.  "Smallest Church in Sussex", from their 2003 debut ''[[The Decline of British Sea Power]]'', was adapted into "The Smallest Church in Saint-Saëns". "Fire Escape in the Sea" from the 2012 ''EP2'' became "Whirling-In-Rags, 8 AM". "Red Rock Riviera" from the 2012 documentary soundtrack ''[[From the Sea to the Land Beyond]]'' was reworked as "Instrument of Surrender". Two tracks from their 2017 album ''[[Let the Dancers Inherit the Party]]'', "Wants to Be Free" and "Praise for Whatever", were transformed respectively into "Burn, Baby, Burn" and "The Insulindian Miracle". "Cleaning out the Rooms" from the 2011 album ''[[Valhalla Dancehall]]'' was remixed into both "Detective Arriving on the Scene" and "{{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}". The track "Tiger King" from the 2009 ''[[Man of Aran (album)|Man of Aran]]'' soundtrack was also remixed but retained its original title. Additionally, the name of the in-game hostel-cafeteria ''Whirling-in-Rags'' is derived from a lyric in the song "Hail Holy Queen" from the 2013 album ''[[Machineries of Joy]]''.

The voice-acting cast includes metal musicians [[Mikee Goodman]] of [[SikTh]] and Mark Holcomb of [[Periphery (band)|Periphery]].<ref name="gamesradar conception" /><ref name="sikth">{{Cite web |last=Munro |first=Scott |date=22 October 2019 |title=Sikth and Periphery members star in hit video game Disco Elysium |url=https://www.loudersound.com/news/sikth-and-periphery-members-star-in-hit-video-game-disco-elysium |website=[[Metal Hammer Magazine]] |access-date=25 October 2019 |archive-date=25 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191025065652/https://www.loudersound.com/news/sikth-and-periphery-members-star-in-hit-video-game-disco-elysium |url-status=live}}</ref> The original release also had voice-acting by [[Dasha Nekrasova]] of the cultural commentary podcast ''[[Red Scare (podcast)|Red Scare]]''<ref>{{Cite tweet |number=1184524991213309952 |user=discoelysium |title=Good ear! |date=16 October 2019}}</ref> and four of the hosts from the [[Left-wing politics|left-wing]] political satire podcast, ''[[Chapo Trap House]]'';<ref name="credits" /> these would later be replaced in ''The Final Cut''.<ref name="polygon final cut review" />

In a Steam post published in January 2020, {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} cited several works that influenced the writing and style of ''Disco Elysium''.<ref name=":0" /> One major influence is the 1999 video game ''[[Planescape: Torment]]'', which, like ''Disco Elysium'', features an amnesiac player character, heavily emphasises dialogue, and is rendered isometrically.<ref name="thr influence">{{Cite web |last=Beresford |first=Trilby |date=1 April 2020 |title='Disco Elysium' Team Consider Game's Wider Impact: 'Let's Just Hope That It Helps People Through This Time' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/disco-elysium-team-consider-games-wider-impact-1287838 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200401233749/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/disco-elysium-team-consider-games-wider-impact-1287838 |archive-date=1 April 2020 |access-date=8 April 2020 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]}}</ref> The television show ''[[The Wire]]'' was also used as an influence for the game's [[working class]] setting, while [[Émile Zola]]'s writings shared stories on the misery of human life that narrative writer Helen Hindpere said she felt resonated within the game.<ref name="thr influence" /> Other works that influenced ''Disco Elysium'' included the video game ''[[Kentucky Route Zero]]''; television shows ''[[True Detective]]'' and ''[[The Shield]]''; literary references include [[Émile Zola]]'s [[Germinal (novel)|''Germinal'']], [[Dashiell Hammett]]'s ''[[The Glass Key]]'', [[China Miéville]]'s [[The City & the City|''The City & The City'']] (although this influence was denied by Kurvitz in an interview<ref>{{Cite news |last=Writer |first=Matt Cox Former Staff |last2=Cox |first2=Matt |date=2018-05-29 |title="We accommodate for every stupid thing that you wanna do" - how Disco Elysium makes detective games work |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/disco-elysium-rpg-details |access-date=2026-07-06 |work=Rock, Paper, Shotgun |language=en}}</ref>), and the [[Soviet Union|Soviet]] sci-fi authors [[Arkady and Boris Strugatsky]]. For visual references, the developers mentioned painters [[Rembrandt]], [[Ilya Repin]], [[Jenny Saville]], [[Alex Kanevsky]], and [[Wassily Kandinsky]].<ref name=":0" />

Soviet artists [[Vladimir Mayakovsky]] and [[Viktor Tsoi]] as well as German philosophers [[Karl Marx]] and [[Friedrich Engels]] were notable political influences on the developers,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zhang |first=Shuci |title=Disco Elysium investigates political intricacies with haunting art and literary flair |url=https://webbcanyonchronicle.com/14300/showcase/disco-elysium-investigates-political-intricacies-with-haunting-art-and-literary-flair/ |access-date=1 July 2026 |website=Webb Canyon Chronicle|date=10 March 2023|publication-place=[[The Webb Schools]]}}</ref> with Hindpere thanking the four figures in her team's acceptance speech at [[The Game Awards 2019|The Game Awards]].<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DOz4QcRZu_g |title=Disco Elysium Wins the Fresh Indie Game Award Presented by Reggie Fils-Aimé |date=2019-12-12 |last=thegameawards |access-date=2026-07-01 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

[[Émile Zola]] is a significant literary influence on ''Disco Elysium''. Lead writer Helen Hindpere has repeatedly expressed admiration for the French author, noting that both she and several other members of the studio are longtime fans of his work.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gordon |first=Lewis |date=2021-04-25 |title=The materialism and magic of the surprise RPG hit 'Disco Elysium' |url=https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/the-materialism-magic-of-the-surprise-rpg-hit-disco-elysium |access-date=2025-04-19 |website=Input |language=en}}</ref> Studio founder [[Robert Kurvitz]] has similarly acknowledged Zola's impact, stating during a conference in France that he does not consider himself a writer, as he prefers not to draw comparisons with literary figures of Zola's stature: “You are not going to be a better writer than Émile Zola.”<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?si=VN5sRHIPOv2Nr8oh&t=669&v=rqYGh078W0I&feature=youtu.be |title=Importance of Worldbuilding {{!}} Robert Kurvitz {{!}} Game Camp France 2024 |date=2024-08-29 |last=Game Camp |access-date=2025-04-19 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

The creators have also said that their work owes a lot to the Estonian urbanist poet [[Arvi Siig]]. Kurvitz said while accepting the Estonian President's Young Cultural Figure annual award for 2020 "Without his modernism, Elysium – the world the game is placed in – would not be half of what it is." He also said Siig's vision of an international, radical and humanist Estonian culture lives on in ''Disco Elysium''.<ref name="2020-10-30 Eesti Rahvusringhääling">{{cite news |date=2020-10-30 |editor-last1=Kallaste |editor-first1=Kristjan |title='Disco Elysium' creators win President's Young Cultural Figure award |url=https://news.err.ee/1153269/disco-elysium-creators-win-president-s-young-cultural-figure-award |language=en |publisher=[[Eesti Rahvusringhääling]] |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211207154635/https://news.err.ee/1153269/disco-elysium-creators-win-president-s-young-cultural-figure-award |archive-date=2021-12-07 |access-date=2025-05-20 |quote=Robert Kurvitz and Aleksander Rostov, creators of popular role-playing indie videogame "Disco Elysium", were awarded with the annual President's Young Cultural Figure award of €5,000 for 2020.}}</ref>

The main protagonist of ''Disco Elysium'' was directly inspired by producer [[Kaur Kender]]'s father, who served as a detective in the Soviet police. The scene in which the protagonist vomits due to the stench of a decaying corpse is based on a real incident experienced by Kender's father during his career.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?si=v8Xi5jy9SWY7QPbQ&v=SrN0KXeQ0v4&feature=youtu.be |title=People Make Games interviews Argo Tuulik (Full Interview) |date=2023-05-27 |last=People Make Games |access-date=2025-04-20 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

During a livestream, Robert Kurvitz cited ''[[Fallout (franchise)|Fallout]]'' as an influence, describing ''Disco Elysium'' as being “from the ''Fallout'' school of RPG.” He added that the original ''[[Fallout (video game)|Fallout]]'' and ''[[Fallout 2]]'' were among the first games to demonstrate that “video games can be forms of art.”<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/live/qI3UwN_IK0Y?si=MPel532dWK9yVSXH&t=657 |title=Marijam Did & Robert Kurvitz {{!}} Everything to Play For |language=en |access-date=2025-04-19 |via=www.youtube.com|type=Video|publisher=[[Verso Books]]}}</ref>

==Release==
''Disco Elysium'' was first released for [[Windows]] on 15 October 2019.<ref name="destructoid">{{Cite web |last=Marzano |first=Anthony |date=15 October 2019 |title=Police procedural cRPG Disco Elysium is out today |url=https://www.destructoid.com/police-procedural-crpg-disco-elysium-is-out-today-569627.phtml |access-date=16 October 2019 |website=[[Destructoid]] |archive-date=24 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200924222325/https://www.destructoid.com/police-procedural-crpg-disco-elysium-is-out-today-569627.phtml |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[macOS]] version was released on 27 April 2020.<ref>{{Cite news |last=O'Conner |first=Alice |date=28 April 2020 |title=Disco Elysium is now on Mac too |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]] |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2020/04/28/disco-elysium-is-now-on-mac-too/ |access-date=28 April 2020 |archive-date=20 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201020070324/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2020/04/28/disco-elysium-is-now-on-mac-too/ |url-status=live }}</ref> One of the first translations that {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} published was the Chinese version, which was released in March 2020. ''Disco Elysium'' received "universal acclaim" according to [[review aggregator]] website [[Metacritic]], with it being praised for its narrative and conversational systems.<ref name="PCGUS" /><ref name=MC/><ref name="IGN" /> Fellow review aggregator [[OpenCritic]] assessed that the game received "mighty" approval, being recommended by 96% of critics.<ref name="opencritic"/> 

''[[PC Gamer]]'' praised the game for its depth, freedom, customisation, and storytelling and called it one of the best RPGs on the PC.<ref name=PCGUS/> ''[[IGN]]'' praised the game's open world and compared it favourably to ''[[The Witcher 3]]'' and ''[[Red Dead Redemption 2]]'', despite being much smaller.<ref name=IGN/> ''[[The Washington Post]]'' said that the game is "conspicuously well written".<ref name="WashingtonPost">{{Cite news |last=Byrd |first=Christopher |date=17 October 2019 |title='Disco Elysium': Riveting delirium |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/video-games/reviews/disco-elysium-riveting-delirium/ |access-date=18 October 2019 |archive-date=18 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191018101814/https://www.washingtonpost.com/video-games/reviews/disco-elysium-riveting-delirium/ |url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[GameSpot]]'' awarded it a 10 out of 10, their first perfect score since 2017.<ref name=GSpot/><ref>{{Cite web |date=4 November 2019 |title=GameSpot's Full List Of 10/10 Reviews And How Those Scores Are Decided |url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gamespots-full-list-of-1010-reviews-and-how-those-/1100-6422955/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191113001541/https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gamespots-full-list-of-1010-reviews-and-how-those-/1100-6422955/ |archive-date=13 November 2019 |access-date=13 November 2019 |website=[[GameSpot]]}}</ref>

''[[PCGamesN]]'' wrote that the game set new genre standards for exploration and conversation systems.<ref name="PCGN" /> Conversely, ''[[Eurogamer]]'' criticised the game for not offering enough choice in role-playing and for a distinct lack of focus.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hetfeld |first=Malindy |date=18 October 2019 |title=Disco Elysium review - large-scale whodunit with a distinct lack of focus |url=https://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2019-10-18-disco-elysium-review-large-scale-whodunit-with-a-lack-of-focus |access-date=19 October 2019 |website=[[Eurogamer]] |archive-date=18 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191018223416/https://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2019-10-18-disco-elysium-review-large-scale-whodunit-with-a-lack-of-focus |url-status=live}}</ref>

In a review of ''Disco Elysium'' in ''[[Black Gate (magazine)|Black Gate]]'', Joshua Dinges said "The nihilistic world is remarkably well fleshed out, the non-player characters earnestly realized, and the myriad plot threads extremely engaging. You may have no idea what to do fifty percent of the time, but will still find yourself spending every available minute of the day carrying out nebulous tasks and exploring your fascinating surroundings. Surroundings which, I will add, are all gorgeously represented in the style of expressionist watercolors."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.blackgate.com/2019/12/15/a-quirky-and-diabolically-weird-police-procedural-rpg-disco-elysium// |title=A Quirky and Diabolically Weird Police Procedural RPG: Disco Elysium – Black Gate |date=15 December 2019}}</ref>

== Post-release ==
=== Breakup of {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} ===
Following the critical and commercial success of ''Disco Elysium'', work immediately began on a direct sequel to the game as well as several spin-off projects.<ref name="PCGinterview">{{Cite web |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=2024-06-13 |title=A follow-up to the legendary Disco Elysium might have been ready to play within the next year⁠—{{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}'s devs loved it, management canceled it and laid off the team: 'For a while it seemed like miracles were possible, and with them redemption' |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/game-development/for-a-while-it-seemed-like-miracles-were-possible-and-with-them-redemption-a-follow-up-to-the-legendary-disco-elysium-might-have-been-ready-to-play-within-the-next-yearzaums-devs-loved-it-management-canceled-it-and-laid-off-the-team/ |access-date=2024-07-10 |website=PC Gamer}}</ref> The direct sequel, code-named Y12, was led by Kurvitz, Rostov, and Hindpere. Another project, P1, was led by Kender.<ref name="PCGinterview" /> In October 2022, {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} member Martin Luiga announced that he, Kurvitz, Rostov, and Hindpere of {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} had "involuntarily left the company" in late 2021, stating that {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} "no longer represents the ethos it was founded on." Luiga also affirmed that the {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} [[Cultural institution|cultural association]] had also been dissolved.<ref name="pcgamer departures">{{Cite web |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=1 October 2022 |title=Founding member of Disco Elysium developer claims core team members 'involuntarily' left the company |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/founding-member-of-disco-elysium-developer-claims-core-team-members-involuntarily-left-the-company/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221001200459/https://www.pcgamer.com/founding-member-of-disco-elysium-developer-claims-core-team-members-involuntarily-left-the-company/ |archive-date=1 October 2022 |access-date=1 October 2022 |website=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> In an interview, Luiga stated that the other three members had been fired under [[false pretenses]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Laska |first=Karol |date=6 October 2022 |title='Fans Have the Right to Know' - Disco Elysium Co-Creator Talks {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} Losing With the System |url=https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/fans-have-the-right-to-know-disco-elysium-co-creator-talks-zaum-l/z04ad7 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221006143921/https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/fans-have-the-right-to-know-disco-elysium-co-creator-talks-zaum-l/z04ad7 |archive-date=6 October 2022 |access-date=6 October 2022 |website=Gamepressure.com |language=en}}</ref> A spokesperson for {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} denied the allegations.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Batchelor |first=James |date=3 October 2022 |title=Three key members of Disco Elysium studio have left 'involuntarily' |url=https://www.gamesindustry.biz/three-key-members-of-disco-elysium-studio-have-left-involuntarily |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221003112200/https://www.gamesindustry.biz/three-key-members-of-disco-elysium-studio-have-left-involuntarily |archive-date=3 October 2022 |magazine=[[Game Informer]] |access-date=8 October 2022}}</ref>

In early November 2022, conflicting reports of the events were announced. According to Kurvitz, Zaum Studio OÜ, the development studio, was originally owned in majority shares by Margus Linnamäe, and was then acquired by Tütreke OÜ, a holding company owned by studio CEO Ilmar Kompus through a share purchase in 2021. Kurvitz and Rostov claimed that the funds used for that purchase were pulled from the studio itself, making it a fraudulent purchase, upon which they started to challenge the purchase and recover their IP from the studio.<ref name="gr nov2022" /> Among Kurvitz' and Rostov's complaints is that Kompus purchased four sketches from Zaum that were establishing the basis for a ''Disco Elysium'' sequel for {{GBP|1}}, then resold these to Zaum for {{Euro|4.8 million}}, effectively helping Kompus regain part of the money spent to acquire Zaum through Tütreke. Kurvitz and Rostov discovered the change in how the company was organised, including their demotion, and were fired when they began raising questions. Kurvitz and Rostov argued that they still have some control of the ''Disco Elysium'' [[intellectual property]] rights, and thus should have had a say in blocking the sale.<ref name="gr nov2022">{{Cite web |last=Bailey |first=Dustin |date=November 9, 2022 |title=Fired Disco Elysium devs allege fraud, while the studio alleges toxic management Legal proceedings in the matter were started by Kaur Kender, the executive producer of ''Disco Elysium''. Kender had asked similar questions of the change in Zaum's management, leading to his firing.<ref name="pcgamer legal update" /> He filed suit asserting that Kompus owed him {{Euro|1 million}}. Kender further asserted that Kompus was aided by Tõnis Haavel, an Estonian investor and Kompus' brother-in-law<ref name="SITuulik" /> who had been convicted of fraud and was already {{Euro|11.5 million}} in debt.<ref name="pcgamer legal update" /> Haavel has a majority share in Yessirnosir Ltd., a United Kingdom subsidiary of Zaum where the ''Disco Elysium'' rights are held. An initial hearing in Kender's case, which included statements from Kurvitz, was held in October 2022.<ref name="pcgamer legal update" /> By December 2022, Kender had dropped his lawsuit, as Kompus had paid back {{Euro|4.8 million}} from Tütreke back to {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Partis |first=Danielle |date=December 8, 2022 |title=Lawsuit against {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} majority shareholder withdrawn |url=https://www.gamesindustry.biz/lawsuit-against-zaum-majority-shareholder-withdrawn |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221208142756/https://www.gamesindustry.biz/lawsuit-against-zaum-majority-shareholder-withdrawn |archive-date=8 December 2022 |access-date=December 8, 2022 |website=[[GamesIndustry.biz]]}}</ref><ref name="pcgamer dec2022">{{Cite web |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=December 10, 2022 |title=$4.8 million paid to {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} and one lawsuit dropped as the battle over Disco Elysium continues |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/dollar48-million-paid-to-zaum-and-one-lawsuit-dropped-as-the-battle-over-disco-elysium-continues/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221210200805/https://www.pcgamer.com/dollar48-million-paid-to-zaum-and-one-lawsuit-dropped-as-the-battle-over-disco-elysium-continues/ |archive-date=10 December 2022 |access-date=December 10, 2022 |website=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> Kurvitz and Rostov still assert that there were illegal actions to take over the development studio leading to their ouster.<ref name="pcgamer dec2022" />

The studio issued a statement on 14 March 2023 stating that all legal actions from the former members were concluded. Of Kender, he had "divested all his shares in the studio, repaid all his debts to the studio", and paid for the studio's legal fees under court order. Of Kurvitz and Rostov, the studio alleged that their lawsuit was dropped due to a lack of evidence. Further details of these cases remain confidential.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wales |first=Matt |date=March 14, 2023 |title=Disco Elysium studio says lawsuits from former members have been resolved |url=https://www.eurogamer.net/disco-elysium-studio-says-lawsuits-from-former-members-have-been-resolved |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230314232339/https://www.eurogamer.net/disco-elysium-studio-says-lawsuits-from-former-members-have-been-resolved |archive-date=14 March 2023 |access-date=March 14, 2023 |website=[[Eurogamer]]}}</ref> On 17 March 2023, Kurvitz and Rostov responded to this statement by clarifying that the announcement was "wrong and misleading in several respects" and "[sought] to unfairly paint [them] - the remaining minority shareholders in {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} - as mere disgruntled employees." Kurvitz and Rostov also stated that their lawsuit regarding employment claims against the studio was dismissed "as part of a larger campaign against [them]" and that they plan to "pursue legal options accordingly."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=March 18, 2023 |title=Disco Elysium studio declares 'resolution' of legal battle while two of its ousted founders insist the fight continues: 'they will not silence us' |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/disco-elysium-studio-declares-resolution-of-legal-battle-while-two-of-its-ousted-founders-insist-the-fight-continues-they-will-not-silence-us/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230320022925/https://www.pcgamer.com/disco-elysium-studio-declares-resolution-of-legal-battle-while-two-of-its-ousted-founders-insist-the-fight-continues-they-will-not-silence-us/ |archive-date=20 March 2023 |access-date=March 19, 2023 |website=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Rousseau |first=Jeffrey |date=2023-03-14 |title={{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} resolves legal dispute with Disco Elysium producer Kaur Kender |url=https://www.gamesindustry.biz/zaum-resolves-legal-dispute-with-disco-elysium-producer-kaur-kender |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230321211432/https://www.gamesindustry.biz/zaum-resolves-legal-dispute-with-disco-elysium-producer-kaur-kender |archive-date=21 March 2023 |access-date=2023-03-21 |website=[[GamesIndustry.biz]] |language=en}}</ref>

Following the ouster of Kurvitz, Rostov, and Hindpere, work on the direct sequel project Y12 continued for several months under Argo Tuulik, one of the writers on ''Disco Elysium'', and Dora Klindžić until management cancelled it in mid-2022.<ref name="PCGinterview" /> Shortly thereafter, Tuulik and Klindžić were asked to pitch a "standalone expansion" to ''Disco Elysium'', X7, later revealed to be titled ''Locust City: A Disco Elysium Story'', which centered on the characters of Cuno and Cunoesse.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Egan |first=Toussaint |date=2025-04-02 |title=A would-be Disco Elysium expansion has leaked online, with lots of details |url=https://www.polygon.com/gaming/551096/disco-elysium-locust-city-an-elysium-story-zaum-presentation |access-date=2025-04-07 |website=Polygon |language=en-US}}</ref> The game was greenlighted by management without a [[pre-production]] period. Full production began before the understaffed writing duo had a full outline of the game. They gained additional staff when Kender's project P1 was cancelled pursuant to his ouster.<ref name="PCGinterview" /> In May 2023, [[People Make Games]] published the findings of their investigation into {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}'s legal situation, in which Tuulik was featured heavily in interviews.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Litchfield |first=Ted |date=2023-06-02 |title=After watching the explosive documentary on Disco Elysium's legal battle, I can't fathom how Disco Elysium 2 will ever be made |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/after-watching-the-explosive-documentary-on-disco-elysiums-legal-battle-i-cant-fathom-how-disco-elysium-2-will-ever-be-made/ |access-date=2024-07-10 |website=PC Gamer}}</ref> Klindžić alleged that company executives isolated Tuulik in retaliation for unfavorable comments made toward them in the documentary. The team produced a "well-received" internal demo for X7 at the end of 2023.<ref name="PCGinterview" />

In February 2024, ''[[Sports Illustrated]]''{{'}}s gaming publication ''GLHF'' reported that {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} was laying off about 25% of its staff, including Tuulik and Klindžić, cancelling X7, and confirmed the earlier cancellation of Y12.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wutz |first=Marco |date=2024-02-15 |title=Sources: Disco Elysium dev {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} to lay off around a quarter of its staff, cancels new game |url=https://videogames.si.com/news/disco-elysium-dev-zaum-layoffs |access-date=2024-07-10 |website=GLHF}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Wales |first=Matt |date=2024-02-15 |title=Disco Elysium standalone expansion reportedly cancelled and quarter of staff facing redundancy at {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} |url=https://www.eurogamer.net/disco-elysium-standalone-expansion-reportedly-cancelled-and-quarter-of-staff-facing-redundancy-at-zaum |access-date=2024-07-10 |work=Eurogamer.net}}</ref> Tuulik noted that the layoffs primarily targeted women and team members who had raised complaints about working conditions.<ref name="SITuulik">{{Cite web |last=Wutz |first=Marco |date=2024-02-16 |title=Last Disco Elysium writer laid off by {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} speaks out |url=https://videogames.si.com/news/disco-elysium-dev-zaum-layoffs-last-writer-speaks-out |access-date=2024-07-10 |website=GLHF}}</ref> ''[[PC Gamer]]'' reported that the only remaining projects at {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} were M0, a ''Disco Elysium'' [[mobile game]], and C4, an unrelated RPG.<ref name="PCGinterview" />

=== Spiritual sequels and related games ===
{{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}'s C4 project was later revealed to be ''[[Zero Parades: For Dead Spies]]'', an RPG with the same narrative and art style as ''Disco Elysium'', involving a female spy convincing her former collaborators to regroup for a mission.<ref name="nytimes2025" /> Previews for the game identified ''Zero Parades'' as having numerous similar visual elements as ''Disco Elysium'', including its art style and user interface. The game was released in May 2026.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/we-finally-saw-za-ums-zero-parades-in-action-and-even-though-its-extremely-disco-elysium-the-protagonist-couldnt-be-more-different/ |title=We finally saw ZA/UM's Zero Parades in action and even though it's extremely Disco Elysium, the protagonist couldn't be more different |first=Tyler |last=Colp |date=September 24, 2025 |accessdate=September 28, 2025 |work=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> Two months after its release, {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} said ''Zero Parades'' did not sell as well as expected to maintain their current employee count, and were laying off up to 32 staff.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/2-months-after-releasing-zero-parades-studio-za-um-is-laying-off-employees-its-commercial-performance-has-not-enabled-us-to-sustain-a-studio-of-our-current-size/ |  title=  2 months after releasing Zero Parades, Studio ZA/UM is laying off employees: 'Its commercial performance has not enabled us to sustain a studio of our current size' | first = Andy | last = Chalk | date = July 17, 2026 | accessdate = July 17, 2026 | work = [[PC Gamer]] }}</ref>

The conflicts around the {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} studio and breakup of the team led to four different projects from different members each seeking to be a successor to ''Disco Elysium'', in addition to ''Zero Parades''.<ref name="nytimes2025">{{cite web |last1=Faber |first1=Tom |title=A Group of Socialists Created a Hit Game That Tore Them Apart |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/09/17/arts/disco-elysium-zaum-estonia.html |website=[[The New York Times]] |access-date=19 September 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250918104033/https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/09/17/arts/disco-elysium-zaum-estonia.html |archive-date=18 September 2025 |date=17 September 2025}}</ref> After leaving {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}, Kurvitz and Rostov launched Red Info in June 2022, a development studio to work on a [[spiritual successor]] to ''Disco Elysium''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/after-watching-the-explosive-documentary-on-disco-elysiums-legal-battle-i-cant-fathom-how-disco-elysium-2-will-ever-be-made/ |title=After watching the explosive documentary on Disco Elysium's legal battle, I can't fathom how Disco Elysium 2 will ever be made |first=Ted |last=Litchfield |date=June 2, 2023 |accessdate=October 11, 2024 |work=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref> Red Info has been backed by at least $10 million in funding by [[NetEase]] and has brought writer [[Chris Avellone]] to help.<ref name="nytimes2025" />

The other three spiritual successor projects were announced nearly simultaneously in October 2024 by three separate studios, each with some former members of {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}.
* Longdue includes Martin Luiga—formerly of Red Info<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.vg247.com/hopetown-latest-ex-disco-elysium-recruit-reveals-hes-left-another-would-be-successor-studio |title=Hopetown's latest ex-Disco Elysium recruit reveals he's had to leave another of the kinda Disco Elysium successor studios, but says it's on friendly terms |first=Mark |last=Warren |date=March 11, 2025 |access-date=April 24, 2025 |work=[[VG 247]]}}</ref>—plus ''Disco Elysium'' narrator Lenval Brown<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.eurogamer.net/more-disco-elysium-alumni-join-former-staff-at-longdue-working-on-spiritual-successor |title=More Disco Elysium alumni join former staff at Longdue working on spiritual successor |first=Ed |last=Nightingale |date=March 11, 2025 |access-date=April 24, 2025 |work=[[Eurogamer]]}}</ref> and other developers from [[Bungie]] and [[Rockstar Games]]. Longdue announced their game [[Hopetown (video game)|Hopetown]] was funded via a [[Kickstarter]] campaign in April 2025.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/former-disco-elysium-devs-are-working-on-a-spiritual-successor-at-new-studio-longdue-though-robert-kurvitz-and-aleksander-rostov-arent-involved |title=Former Disco Elysium devs are working on a spiritual successor at new studio Longdue, though Robert Kurvitz and Aleksander Rostov aren't involved |first=Nic |last=Reuben |date=October 11, 2024 |access-date=October 11, 2024 |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref>
* Dark Math Games was founded by investor Linnamae, and includes Kender and other {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}} developers. Dark Math is developing ''Tangerine Antarctic'' (previously named ''XXX Nightshift'').<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/a-disco-elysium-successor-studio-has-been-announced-for-the-second-time-today-meaning-there-are-now-4-companies-battling-for-the-title-of-za-ums-true-inheritor/ |title=A Disco Elysium successor studio has been announced for the second time today, meaning there are now 4 companies battling for the title of {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}'s true inheritor |first=Joshua |last=Wolans |date=October 11, 2024 |accessdate=October 11, 2024 |work=[[PC Gamer]]}}</ref><ref name="nytimes2025" /> ''Tangerine Antarctic'' is set in a fictional ski resort located at [[Mount Hope (Ross Dependency)|Mount Hope]] in Antarctica, with "Tangerine Antarctic" the name of the hotel for the resort, and where the characters must solve a murder mystery among the guests that are stuck at the hotel due to a snowstorm. Unlike ''Disco Elysium''{{'}}s isometric view, the game will be played by a third-person camera.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/would-be-disco-elysium-successor-xxx-nightshift-morphs-from-an-isometric-rpg-into-a-third-person-ski-resort-murder-mystery |title=Would-be Disco Elysium successor XXX Nightshift morphs from an isometric RPG into a third-person ski resort murder mystery |first=Mark |last=Warren |date=September 18, 2025 |accessdate=September 20, 2025 |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref>
* [[Summer Eternal]] was founded by writers Argo Tuulik and Dora Klindžić. Both had worked previously with Dark Math and Longdue, but left these studios due to internal conflicts.<ref name="nytimes2025" /><ref>{{cite web |url=https://za.ign.com/disco-elysium-1/203482/news/the-wild-story-behind-disco-elysiums-spiritual-successors-allegations-legal-threats-and-a-usb-stick |title=The Wild Story Behind Disco Elysium's Spiritual Successors: Allegations, Legal Threats, and a USB Stick |first=Wesley|last=Yin-Poole|date=  November 19, 2024 |access-date=April 24, 2025 |work=[[IGN]]}}</ref> Summer Eternal later brought on Olga Moskvina and other senior developers from {{Nowrap|ZA/UM}}.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/sure-why-not-third-group-of-former-disco-elysium-devs-announce-revolutionary-new-rpg-studio |title=Sure, why not – third group of former Disco Elysium devs announce "revolutionary new RPG studio" |first=Edwin |last=Evans-Thirlwell |date=October 11, 2024 |accessdate=October 11, 2024 |work=[[Rock Paper Shotgun]]}}</ref> In October 2026, they announced their Disco-like game in making – [[Red Rooster (video game)|''Red Rooster'']].

''Disco Elysium''{{'s}} non-traditional approach to a role-playing game has inspired at least one developer to follow a similar path for ''[[Esoteric Ebb]]''. The game was inspired by both ''Disco Elysium'' and ''[[Planescape: Torment]]'', taking place in a more traditional fantasy setting, and like ''Disco Elysium'' does not have direct combat but instead skill checks made against the player-character's various internal voices representing different character attributes.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.gamesradar.com/games/rpg/dev-making-planescape-torment-disco-elysium-says-spending-8-years-struggling-to-pay-rent-and-writing-over-1-million-words-for-his-d-and-d-inspired-rpg-was-a-ridiculously-stupid-path/ |title=Dev making "Planescape Torment + Disco Elysium" says spending 8 years struggling to pay rent and writing over 1 million words for his D&D-inspired RPG was "a ridiculously stupid path" |first=Austin |last=Wood |date=February 27, 2026 |accessdate=March 18, 2026 |work=[[GamesRadar]]}}</ref>
