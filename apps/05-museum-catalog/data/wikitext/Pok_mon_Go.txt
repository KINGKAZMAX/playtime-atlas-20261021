'''''Pokémon Go''''' (stylized as '''''Pokémon GO''''') is a 2016 [[augmented reality]] (AR) [[mobile game]] originally developed and published by [[Niantic, Inc.|Niantic]] and since 2025 by [[Scopely|Scopely Explore]], in partnership with [[Nintendo]] and [[the Pokémon Company]], for [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] devices. The game uses [[Global Positioning System|GPS]] to locate, capture, train, and battle [[Pokémon]]. It is [[free-to-play]], featuring a [[freemium]] model that includes [[in-app purchases]] for additional in-game items. ''Pokémon Go'' launched with [[List of generation I Pokémon|approximately 150 Pokémon species]], with new species regularly introduced.

Upon its release, ''Pokémon Go'' received "mixed" or "average" reviews; critics found the experience enjoyable, but noted several technical issues. Despite this, it became one of the most popular and profitable [[mobile app]]s of 2016, amassing over 500 million downloads worldwide. The game is credited with popularizing [[Location-based game|location-based]] and AR technology, encouraging physical activity and social interaction. However, it has also faced criticism for causing accidents and public disturbances. Some governments raised security concerns and imposed regulations on its use. As of May 2018, ''Pokémon Go'' had over 147 million [[Monthly active users|monthly active]] users and had been downloaded more than 1 billion times worldwide by early 2019. By 2020, the game had generated over 6 billion dollars in revenue. It is considered [[List of mobile games listed among the best|among the best mobile games of all time]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Adler |first=Kai |date=2023-09-21 |title=The 16 Best Mobile Games Of All Time |url=https://gamerant.com/best-mobile-games-of-all-time/ |access-date=2025-11-21 |website=Game Rant |language=en |archive-date=November 24, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251124073805/https://gamerant.com/best-mobile-games-of-all-time/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Staff |first=I. G. N. |date=2018-11-22 |title=The Best Mobile Games |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2018/11/22/the-best-mobile-games |access-date=2025-11-21 |website=IGN |language=en}}</ref>

In 2025, [[Scopely]] acquired the game as part of a 3.5 billion dollar purchase of Niantic's gaming division.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Astle |first=Aaron |date=2025-06-02 |title=Pokémon Go under new ownership as Scopely closes $3.5bn Niantic deal |url=https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-under-new-ownership-as-scopely-closes-35bn-niantic-deal/ |access-date=2025-11-03 |website=www.pocketgamer.biz |language=en |archive-date=February 5, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260205220628/https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-under-new-ownership-as-scopely-closes-35bn-niantic-deal/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Players can further customize their avatars by selecting different poses. These poses can be gained for free, purchased, or earned as rewards. Some poses are exclusive to specific events and can only be obtained during their respective event periods. Once the event ends, there is no guarantee that the pose will be available in the game again.

In April 2024, ''Pokémon Go'' released an update<ref>{{cite web |title=Rediscover Pokémon GO 2024 |url=https://pokemongolive.com/rediscovergo?hl=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240817154334/https://pokemongolive.com/rediscovergo?hl=en |archive-date=August 17, 2024 |access-date=April 23, 2024}}</ref> that introduced enhanced customization options for player avatars. The update allowed players to choose from a variety of hairstyles, adjust their avatar's body size, and select from a broader range of skin tones. However, the update received backlash from players who criticized the new avatars for having lower-quality graphics compared to the previous models. Some players were disappointed because the update interfered with certain cosmetic items they had purchased in large quantities before and after the update.<ref>{{cite web |date=April 21, 2024 |title=Pokemon Go's Latest Update is Wrecking Everyone's Player Avatars |url=https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-go-player-avatar-update-bad/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240817164544/https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-go-player-avatar-update-bad/ |archive-date=August 17, 2024 |access-date=April 29, 2024}}</ref>

=== Pokémon collection ===
In ''Pokémon Go'', players do not battle wild [[Pokémon]] to capture them. Instead, they throw a Poké Ball toward the Pokémon by flicking it upward on the screen. Factors affecting the success rate include the Pokémon's catch rate, timing, and the type of Poké Ball used. After capturing a Pokémon, players receive Candy and Stardust as rewards. Candy is used for leveling up or to evolve Pokémon, while Stardust is used to increase a Pokémon's Combat Power (CP). Each Pokémon evolution line has its own type of Candy. The maximum player level was initially 40 but was raised to 50 on November 30, 2020. It was subsequently increased to level 80 following a leveling update on October 15, 2025.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Level 50 is now live in Pokémon Go for everyone |url=https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/technology/level-50-is-now-live-in-pok-c3-a9mon-go-for-everyone/ar-BB1buONN?ocid=uxbndlbing |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210519065205/https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/technology/level-50-is-now-live-in-pok-c3-a9mon-go-for-everyone/ar-BB1buONN?ocid=uxbndlbing |archive-date=May 19, 2021 |access-date=2020-11-30 |website=www.msn.com}}</ref> Players can transfer Pokémon to get more Candy and free up space in their collection. Shiny Pokémon can be found through various methods, such as chance encounters or special events like "Community Day". === Battle system ===
==== Gyms and Raids ====
Players earn [[experience point]]s (XP) by participating in various in-game activities. As they accumulate XP, they advance through levels progressively, unlocking new features. Notably, at level five, players gain access to Pokémon Gyms, where they can engage in battles. They must choose to join one of three color-coded teams: Team Valor (red), Team Mystic (blue), or Team Instinct (yellow). These teams compete for control over Gyms within the ''Pokémon Go'' world.

In June 2017, Niantic announced a revamp of Gym game mechanics to promote teamwork.<ref name="pokemongolive.com">{{cite web |date=June 19, 2017 |title=Raid Battles and New Gym Features are Coming! |url=https://pokemongolive.com/en/post/raids |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170621201806/http://pokemongolive.com/en/post/raids |archive-date=June 21, 2017 |access-date=June 20, 2017 |publisher=Niantic, Inc. Pokémon. Nintendo. Creatures Inc. GAME FREAK Inc. |df=mdy-all}}</ref> Gyms were temporarily disabled on June 19, 2017, and the updated Gyms were introduced shortly afterward through an app update.<ref name="pokemongolive.com" /> The revamped Gyms now include a spinnable component (PokéStop) where players can collect in-game items such as Potions and Pokéballs. Each Gym can hold a maximum of six Pokémon, with the requirement that each Pokémon must be unique within that Gym. The coins earned now depend on how long the defending Pokémon remains in the Gym, replacing the previous 10-coin daily gym defender bonus per Pokémon.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Hernandez |first1=Patricia |date=June 25, 2017 |title=Pokémon Go Players Are Making Themed Gyms with The New Update |url=https://kotaku.com/pokemon-go-players-are-making-themed-gyms-with-the-new-1796402866 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170626165005/http://kotaku.com/pokemon-go-players-are-making-themed-gyms-with-the-new-1796402866 |archive-date=June 26, 2017 |access-date=June 26, 2017 |website=Kotaku |df=mdy-all}}</ref> Legendary, Mythical, and Buddy Pokémon cannot be placed in Gyms.{{citation needed|date=March 2024}}

In July 2017, Raid Battles were introduced, allowing players to team up and battle an over-leveled Pokémon in a Gym. If defeated, players can catch a regular version of that Pokémon. Raid difficulties range from Level 1 to Level 5, with Level 5 being exclusive to Legendary Pokémon. The first Legendary Pokémon, [[Articuno]] and [[Lugia]], were released on July 22, 2017, followed by [[Moltres]] and [[Zapdos]]. From September to November, the Legendary Beasts [[Entei]], [[Raikou]], and [[Suicune]] were available, rotating monthly by region. [[Ho-Oh]] appeared in Raid Battles from November 27 to December 12, 2017. In August 2020, Level 3 Mega Raids were introduced, featuring Mega Evolution, while Level 2 and Level 4 raids were merged into Level 1 and Level 3 raids, respectively.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-01-01 |title=All current Raid Bosses in Pokémon Go January 2021 |url=https://www.imore.com/pokemon-go-current-raid-battles |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240817165102/https://www.imore.com/pokemon-go-current-raid-battles |archive-date=August 17, 2024 |access-date=2021-01-09 |website=iMore}}</ref>

In May 2022, Raid Battles were updated to include Mega Legendary Pokémon, now rated at 6 stars—the highest level in the game. The exclusive Pokémon available in these raids were [[Latias|Mega Latias]] and [[Latios|Mega Latios]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gray |first=Laura |date=2022-05-05 |title=Pokémon GO's New Mega Legendary Raids Are Already Awful |url=https://screenrant.com/pokemon-go-mega-legendary-raid-difficulty-six-stars/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240817154914/https://screenrant.com/pokemon-go-mega-legendary-raid-difficulty-six-stars/ |archive-date=August 17, 2024 |access-date=2022-11-16 |website=ScreenRant |language=en-US}}</ref>

In October 2022, a new type of raid battle called "Elite Raids" was introduced. These raids differ from regular raids in several ways. Elite Raids can only be battled in person, require a full day to begin, and are significantly more challenging, often needing a large group of players to defeat the Pokémon.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jeffery |first=Maxwell |date=2022-11-13 |title=What Are the Red Raid Eggs in Pokemon GO? |url=https://gamingintel.com/red-raid-eggs-pokemon-go/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221116050117/https://gamingintel.com/red-raid-eggs-pokemon-go/ |archive-date=November 16, 2022 |access-date=2022-11-16 |website=Gaming Intel |language=en-US}}</ref>

In May 2023, a new type of raid battle called "Shadow Raids" was introduced. In February 2026, a new type of raid battle called "Super Mega Raids" was introduced. These raids differ from regular mega raids by having a new mechanic where every player needed a mega Pokemon to break the raid Pokemon's shield. These were introduced in 3 megas from "Pokemon Legends Z-A".

In September 2024, ''Pokémon Go'' introduced Dynamax Battles and Dynamax Pokémon, a feature inspired by ''[[Pokémon Sword and Shield]]''. The Pokémon Company. |archive-date=May 19, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250519153936/https://pokemongo.com/post/shadow-raid-max-battle-update |url-status=live }}</ref> Up to four players can team up to battle a Dynamax Pokémon, building up a meter by attacking to Dynamax or Gigantamax their own Pokémon. Each Pokémon's Max Move, which deals increased damage and is exclusive to the Dynamax form, is determined by its regular Fast Attack type (except for G-Max Moves, which are specific to certain species). Players can also use Max Guard to boost defense and Max Spirit to restore health. If all Pokémon are defeated, players can use Max Cheer to boost the Max Meter. Only Dynamax Pokémon, Gigantamax Pokémon, Crowned Sword Zacian, Crowned Shield Zamazenta, and Eternatus are eligible for Dynamax Battles, and specific Pokémon can only be obtained in Dynamax form.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-27 |title=Every Dynamax and Gigantamax Pokemon in Pokemon Go |url=https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/every-dynamax-and-gigantamax-pokemon-in-pokemon-go-so-far-2876339/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241109071819/https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/every-dynamax-and-gigantamax-pokemon-in-pokemon-go-so-far-2876339/ |archive-date=November 9, 2024 |access-date=2024-11-09 |website=Dexerto |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=GO Big with Dynamax and Gigantamax! |url=https://pokemongolive.com/max-pokemon-battle |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241109071822/https://pokemongolive.com/max-pokemon-battle |archive-date=November 9, 2024 |access-date=2024-11-09 |website=Pokémon GO |language=en}}</ref> Gigantamax Battles, introduced in October 2024, function similarly to Dynamax Battles but allow up to 100 players to participate in a single battle, divided into teams of four or fewer. === ''Pokémon Go'' Plus and ''Pokémon Go'' Plus + ===
[[File:Nintendo-Niantic-Pokemon-Go-Plus-wStrap.jpg|thumb|upright|The ''Pokémon Go'' Plus, shown with its wrist strap]]
The ''Pokémon Go'' Plus is a [[Bluetooth Low Energy]] [[wearable device]] developed by Nintendo's [[Platform Technology Development]] division. It enables players to perform in-game actions without looking at their [[smart device]].<ref name="polygonpin">{{cite web |last=Frank |first=Allegra |date=July 11, 2016 |title=Pokémon Go Plus: Everything you need to know |url=http://www.polygon.com/2016/7/11/12153040/pokemon-go-plus-price-info-and-release-date |url-status=live |access-date=July 12, 2016 |website=Polygon |publisher=Vox Media |archive-date=July 13, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160713155211/http://www.polygon.com/2016/7/11/12153040/pokemon-go-plus-price-info-and-release-date }}</ref> When near a Pokémon or PokéStop, the device vibrates, prompting the player to press the button to capture the Pokémon or collect items from the PokéStop.<ref name="polygonpin" /> Players can only see what they have received when the next time they open the app on their mobile device.<ref name="auto" /> The design resembles a Poké Ball and the [[Google Maps pin]].<ref name="polygonpin" /> The decision to create this device, rather than a [[smartwatch]] app, was made to make it more accessible to players who find smartwatches too expensive.<ref>{{cite web |last=Crecente |first=Brian |date=July 12, 2015 |title=Watch Future – Time killers: The strange history of wrist gaming |url=http://www.polygon.com/a/smartwatch-history-guide-evolution/watch-future |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160620154528/http://www.polygon.com/a/smartwatch-history-guide-evolution/watch-future |archive-date=June 20, 2016 |access-date=June 16, 2016 |work=Polygon |df=mdy-all}}</ref> It was launched in the United Kingdom and North America on September 16, 2016.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Pokemon Go Plus. Players in New Zealand and the United States were the first to receive the post-lockdown changes, and gradually, they were rolled out to the rest of the world.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Exploration Bonus Updates Coming to Pokémon GO |url=https://pokemongolive.com/post/hello-world-pokemon-go/ |access-date=2026-03-05 |website=Pokémon GO |language=en}}</ref> The company has since received heavy criticism from the player base for concerns around health and personal safety regulations as well as [[Ableism|negatively impacting]] players with disabilities. This post also announces the creation of "an internal cross-functional team" to address community concerns regarding interaction distance.<ref>{{cite web |date=2021-08-05 |title=A Response To Our Pokémon GO Community |url=https://nianticlabs.com/blog/pgo-exploration-bonus-response/?hl=en |publisher=Niantic}}</ref> The following month, on September 1, 2021, Niantic posts a news update for ''Pokémon Go'' users stating that the 80 meter interaction radius will be returned, and increased communication will be opened with the community with the start of monthly "Developer Diary" announcements.<ref>{{Cite web |title=What we heard -- an update from our task force |url=https://pokemongolive.com/post/sep-taskforce-update/ |access-date=2026-03-05 |website=Pokémon GO |language=en}}</ref>

Gift interactions also remain flexible, with a daily cap of 30 opened gifts instituted in March 2020 still in effect.
