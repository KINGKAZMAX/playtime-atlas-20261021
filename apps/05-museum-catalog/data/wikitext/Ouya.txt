The '''Ouya''' ({{IPAc-en|ˈ|uː|j|ə}} {{respell|OO|yə}}), stylized as '''OUYA''', is<!-- Do NOT change to "was". This violates [[MOS:TENSE]], which specifies that all articles on products are written in present tense, even if discontinued, and even if the rest of the article is written in the past tense.. --> an [[Android (operating system)|Android]]-based [[microconsole]] developed by Ouya Inc. [[Julie Uhrman]] founded the project in 2012,<ref name="momentummediamarketing1">{{cite web |url=http://www.momentummediamarketing.com/how-ouya-created-a-trending-brand-in-less-than-a-day/ |title=How OUYA Created a Trending Brand in Less than a Day |work=Momentum |publisher=Momentum Media Marketing |date=July 25, 2012 |access-date=July 29, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131002233248/http://momentummediamarketing.com/how-ouya-created-a-trending-brand-in-less-than-a-day/ |archive-date=October 2, 2013 |url-status=live }}</ref> bringing in designer [[Yves Béhar]] to collaborate on its design<ref>{{cite web |url=http://fuseproject.com/products-73 |title=OUYA |publisher=fuseproject |access-date=April 4, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130402001808/http://fuseproject.com/products-73 |archive-date=April 2, 2013 |url-status=dead }}</ref> and Muffi Ghadiali as VP of Product Management.<ref>https://www.linkedin.com/in/muffi {{Self-published source|date=June 2022}}</ref> Development was funded via [[Kickstarter]], raising {{USD}}8.5 million, becoming one of the [[Kickstarter#Top projects by funds raised|website's highest-earning projects]] in its history.

Units started to ship to Kickstarter backers in March 2013 and were released to the general public in June 2013. It featured a store for applications and games designed specifically for the Ouya [[Computing platform|platform]], the majority of which were [[casual game]]s. Out of the box, Ouya supports media apps such as [[Twitch (service)|Twitch]] and the [[Kodi (software)|Kodi]] media center.<ref name="kickstarter">{{cite web |author=Launched: July 10, 2012 |url=http://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ouya/ouya-a-new-kind-of-video-game-console |title=OUYA: A New Kind of Video Game Console by OUYA&nbsp;— Kickstarter |publisher=Kickstarter.com |date=July 10, 2012 |access-date=July 29, 2012 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121204152751/http://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ouya/ouya-a-new-kind-of-video-game-console |archive-date=December 4, 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> It runs a modified version of [[Android Jelly Bean]], with [[Rooting (Android OS)|rooting]] being officially encouraged.<ref name="kickstarter" /> The console's hardware design allows it to be easily opened up, requiring only a standard screwdriver for easy [[modding]] and possible hardware add-ons.<ref name="pcworld">{{cite web|last=Wawro|first=Alex|title=Why Ouya Is Making A Killing On Kickstarter|url=http://www.pcworld.com/article/259121/why_ouya_is_making_a_killing_on_kickstarter.html|access-date=July 12, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120714030601/http://www.pcworld.com/article/259121/why_ouya_is_making_a_killing_on_kickstarter.html|archive-date=July 14, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>

All systems can be used as development kits, allowing any Ouya owner to also be a [[Video game developer|developer]], without the need for licensing fees. All games were initially required to have some kind of [[free-to-play]] aspect,<ref name="kickstarter" /> whether they be completely free, have a free trial, or have optional [[Micropayment|purchasable upgrades, levels, or other in-game items]]. This requirement was later removed.<ref>{{cite web|author1=Bob Mills|title=It's about CHOICE|url=https://www.ouya.tv/its-about-choice/|website=Ouya|access-date=November 22, 2014|date=March 19, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141120193550/https://www.ouya.tv/its-about-choice/|archive-date=November 20, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref>

Despite the successful Kickstarter campaign, the Ouya became a [[List of commercial failures in video games|commercial failure]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.xda-developers.com/a-look-back-at-the-ouya-a-tale-of-failure/|title=Ouya: A Look Back|date=May 31, 2015|access-date=December 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160821032555/http://www.xda-developers.com/a-look-back-at-the-ouya-a-tale-of-failure/|archive-date=August 21, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.extremetech.com/gaming/189089-the-stillborn-revolution-ouya-fails-to-sell-developer-seeks-buyout|title=The stillborn revolution: Ouya fails to sell, developer seeks buyout - ExtremeTech|date=September 2, 2014|access-date=December 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161031101808/http://www.extremetech.com/gaming/189089-the-stillborn-revolution-ouya-fails-to-sell-developer-seeks-buyout|archive-date=October 31, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Ouya shutting down">{{Cite web|url=https://www.theverge.com/2019/5/22/18635800/razer-ouya-kickstarter-gaming-service-shutting-down-june-25|title=Ouya will be shut down for good on June 25th|last=Welch|first=Chris|date=2019-05-22|website=The Verge|access-date=2019-06-05}}</ref> Sales were lackluster, game developers failed to embrace the platform, and incentives offered to promote adoption failed,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.extremetech.com/gaming/162077-ouya-game-sales-figures-released-it-doesnt-look-good-for-android-console-gaming|title=Ouya game sales figures released: It doesn't look good for Android console gaming - ExtremeTech|date=July 23, 2013|access-date=December 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170114012424/http://www.extremetech.com/gaming/162077-ouya-game-sales-figures-released-it-doesnt-look-good-for-android-console-gaming|archive-date=January 14, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> causing financial problems for Ouya Inc. and forcing the company to wind down the business. Its software assets were sold to [[Razer Inc.]], who announced the discontinuation of the console in July 2015.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Razer Acquires OUYA Software Assets – Razer Press |date=July 27, 2015 |url=https://press.razer.com/product-news/razer-acquires-ouya-software-assets/ |access-date=2022-10-05 |language=en}}</ref> Razer continued to provide software support for existing Ouya units until June 2019, when it shut down the Ouya storefront, services and accounts, rendering the use of the many applications that required a check-in with the store impossible.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Good |first1=Owen S. |title=Razer closing Ouya store, officially killing the console |url=https://www.polygon.com/2019/5/22/18635860/razer-ouya-store-forge-tv-closing-discontinued-dates |access-date=30 October 2022 |work=Polygon |date=22 May 2019}}</ref>

==History==
Ouya was announced on July 3, 2012, as a new home video game console, led by [[Julie Uhrman]], the [[chief executive officer]] of [[Santa Monica, California]]-based Boxer8, Inc. (later rebranded Ouya, Inc. on August 13, 2012). On July 10, Ouya started a campaign to gauge how many people were interested in the project.<ref name="kickstarter" /> Boxer8 confirmed having a working prototype<ref>{{cite web |url=http://kotaku.com/5927416/the-mastermind-behind-the-ouya-console-is-answering-your-best-questions-right-here |title=The Mastermind Behind the Ouya Console Is Answering Your Best Questions Right Here |publisher=Kotaku.com |date=July 19, 2012 |access-date=July 29, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120722043546/http://kotaku.com/5927416/the-mastermind-behind-the-ouya-console-is-answering-your-best-questions-right-here |archive-date=July 22, 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> with in-progress software and user interface. It features an Nvidia Tegra 3 chip and a price tag of $99 ($95 for 1000 "early birds" backers of the Kickstarter campaign).{{Citation needed|date=June 2023}}

The Kickstarter fundraising goal was $950,000, with a month to reach that goal; within 8 hours it surpassed $1 million.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.badgamehalloffame.com/blog/ouya/|title=OUYA (2013-2015)|last=Cassidy|date=2019-06-26|website=Bad Game Hall of Fame|language=en-US|access-date=2019-09-23}}</ref> Funding continued to increase as more models were made available at various funding levels. According to Kickstarter, in reaching its goal, Ouya holds the record for best first-day performance of any project hosted to date. Within the first 24 hours, the project attracted one backer every 5.59 seconds. Ouya became the eighth project in Kickstarter history to raise more than a million dollars and was the quickest project ever to do so.<ref>{{cite web|last=Strickler|first=Yancey|title=Ouya's Big Day|date=July 11, 2012 |url=http://www.kickstarter.com/blog/ouyas-big-day|access-date=July 11, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120713084853/http://www.kickstarter.com/blog/ouyas-big-day|archive-date=July 13, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Ouya game console kicks off strong|url=http://www.gameverse.com/2012/07/11/ouya-game-console-kicks-off-strong/|access-date=July 11, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120713222957/http://www.gameverse.com/2012/07/11/ouya-game-console-kicks-off-strong/|archive-date=July 13, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref> The Kickstarter campaign finished on August 9 with $8,596,475 at 904% of their goal. This made the Ouya Kickstarter the [[Kickstarter#Top projects by funds raised|fifth-highest earning]] in the website's history at the time.{{Citation needed|date=June 2023}}

Ouya units for Kickstarter funders started to ship on March 28, 2013.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.polygon.com/2013/2/28/4040186/ouya-ship-date-kellee-santiago | title = Ouya ships to backers March 28 | first = Samit | last = Sarkar | date = February 28, 2013 | access-date = February 28, 2013 | publisher = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130302221649/http://www.polygon.com/2013/2/28/4040186/ouya-ship-date-kellee-santiago | archive-date = March 2, 2013 | url-status = live }}</ref> On June 25, 2013, the Ouya was released to the public for $99.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Etherington |first=Darrell |date=2013-06-25 |title=OUYA Gets Its Official Retail Release, But The $99 Game Console Still Hasn't Reached Every Early Backer |url=https://techcrunch.com/2013/06/25/ouya-gets-its-official-retail-release-but-the-99-game-console-still-hasnt-reached-every-early-backer/ |access-date=2023-06-08 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref>

Ouya announced the "Free the Games Fund" in July 2013 with the goal to support developers making games exclusively for their system with Ouya matching a [[Kickstarter]] campaign's pledge dollar-for-dollar if a minimum of $50,000 is raised, but only if the game will be an Ouya exclusive for six months.<ref name="freethegames" />

In October 2013, Uhrman stated that the company planned on releasing a new iteration of the Ouya console sometime in 2014,<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.vg247.com/2013/10/25/ouya-2-0-releasing-sometime-in-2014-improved-controllers-in-the-works/ | title = Ouya 2.0 releasing sometime in 2014, improved controller in the works | access-date = October 25, 2013 | date = October 25, 2013 | publisher = VG247 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20131028014543/http://www.vg247.com/2013/10/25/ouya-2-0-releasing-sometime-in-2014-improved-controllers-in-the-works/ | archive-date = October 28, 2013 | url-status = live }}</ref> with an improved controller, double the storage space, and better Wi-Fi.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.techhive.com/article/2093246/redesigned-16gb-ouya-console-is-the-most-modest-of-upgrades.html|title=Redesigned 16GB Ouya console is the most modest of upgrades|first=Hayden|last=Dingman|work=TechHive |date=January 31, 2014|access-date=December 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924113846/http://www.techhive.com/article/2093246/redesigned-16gb-ouya-console-is-the-most-modest-of-upgrades.html|archive-date=September 24, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref> On November 23, 2013, a limited edition white Ouya with double the storage of the original and a new controller design was available for pre-order at $129.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://shop.ouya.tv/ |title=Official Ouya Store November 2013 |access-date=July 4, 2014 |publisher=Ouya, Inc. |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131123085156/http://shop.ouya.tv/ |archive-date=November 23, 2013 }}</ref>

As of January 1, 2014, the limited edition white Ouya went off sale and cannot be found on the official store, nor from any official resellers. On January 31, 2014, a new black version of the Ouya was released with double storage and new controller design.<ref>{{Cite news | url=http://www.androidshock.com/news/new-black-version-of-ouya-out-now-with-double-storage-and-new-controller-design/ | title=New Black Version of OUYA Out Now with Double Storage and New Controller Design | publisher=AndroidShock | first=Robert | last=Clark | date=January 31, 2014 | access-date=February 11, 2014 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140223184443/http://www.androidshock.com/news/new-black-version-of-ouya-out-now-with-double-storage-and-new-controller-design/ | archive-date=February 23, 2014 | url-status=live }}</ref>

In January 2015, Ouya received an investment of US$10 million from [[Alibaba Group|Alibaba]] with the possibility of incorporating some of Ouya technologies into Alibaba's set-top box.<ref>{{cite news|title=Alibaba Breathes New Life Into Game-Console Maker Ouya|url=https://blogs.wsj.com/digits/2015/01/29/alibaba-breathes-new-life-into-game-console-maker-ouya/|publisher=WSJ|date=January 29, 2015|access-date=October 11, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171202151550/https://blogs.wsj.com/digits/2015/01/29/alibaba-breathes-new-life-into-game-console-maker-ouya/|archive-date=December 2, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref>

In April 2015, it was revealed that Ouya was trying to sell the company because it failed to renegotiate its debt.<ref>{{cite news|title=Exclusive: Gaming startup OUYA needs to find a buyer quickly|url=http://fortune.com/2015/04/28/exclusive-gaming-startup-ouya-needs-to-find-a-buyer-quickly/|publisher=[[Fortune (magazine)|Fortune]]|date=April 28, 2015|access-date=May 10, 2015|quote=Gaming company OUYA is on the auction block after tripping a debt covenant, according to a confidential email sent out earlier this month from CEO Julie Uhrman to company investors and advisors|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150518082005/http://fortune.com/2015/04/28/exclusive-gaming-startup-ouya-needs-to-find-a-buyer-quickly/|archive-date=May 18, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Gaming company Ouya is reportedly putting itself up for sale|url=https://www.theverge.com/2015/4/28/8509005/ouya-android-microconsole-reportedly-seeking-buyer-debt|work=[[The Verge]]|date=April 28, 2015|access-date=May 10, 2015|quote=''Ouya CEO Julie Uhrman sent a memo to investors and advisers earlier this month, saying that the company had failed to satisfy one of its investors' conditions and that renegotiation over the debt had been unsuccessful''|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150511142237/http://www.theverge.com/2015/4/28/8509005/ouya-android-microconsole-reportedly-seeking-buyer-debt|archive-date=May 11, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref> On July 27, 2015, it was announced that [[Razer Inc.]] had acquired Ouya's employees and content library and that Ouya hardware was now discontinued. The deal does not include Ouya's hardware assets. Owners were encouraged to migrate to Razer's own Forge microconsole; Ouya's content library will be integrated into the Forge ecosystem, and "[the] Ouya brand name will live on as a standalone gaming publisher for [[Android TV]] and Android-based TV consoles."<ref name=ars-razeracquired>{{cite web|title=So long, Ouya! Razer acquires microconsole's storefront, technical team|url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2015/07/so-long-ouya-razer-acquires-microconsoles-storefront-technical-team/|website=Ars Technica|date=July 27, 2015|access-date=July 27, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150729010300/http://arstechnica.com/gaming/2015/07/so-long-ouya-razer-acquires-microconsoles-storefront-technical-team/|archive-date=July 29, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=tc-ouyarazer>{{cite web|title=Razer Confirms Acquisition Of Ouya's Software, Tech And Dev Teams In All-Cash Deal|url=https://techcrunch.com/2015/07/27/razer-confirms-ouya-acquisition|website=TechCrunch|date=July 27, 2015 |publisher=AOL|access-date=July 27, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150728135647/http://techcrunch.com/2015/07/27/razer-confirms-ouya-acquisition/|archive-date=July 28, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref> On the same day Uhrman stepped down as Ouya's CEO.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Cooper|first1=Daniel|title=OUYA CEO Julie Uhrman leaves the company she co-founded|url=https://www.engadget.com/2015/07/27/ouya-ceo-julie-uhrman-leaves/|website=Engadget|date=July 27, 2015 |access-date=September 22, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150921123426/http://www.engadget.com/2015/07/27/ouya-ceo-julie-uhrman-leaves/|archive-date=September 21, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref>

The technical team and [[platform evangelism|developer relations]] personnel behind Ouya joined the software team of Razer, which developed its own game platform called the Forge TV. The Forge TV was discontinued in 2016.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.razerzone.com/store/razer-forge-tv|title=Razer Forge TV - Android™ Gaming in Your Living Room|work=Razer|access-date=October 22, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151023101128/http://www.razerzone.com/store/razer-forge-tv|archive-date=October 23, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.androidcentral.com/razer-ceo-talks-ouya-forge-tv-and-upcoming-game-streaming-service-ama|title=Razer CEO talks OUYA, Forge TV and upcoming game streaming service in AMA|work=Android Central|date=July 31, 2015|access-date=October 22, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151016090052/http://www.androidcentral.com/razer-ceo-talks-ouya-forge-tv-and-upcoming-game-streaming-service-ama|archive-date=October 16, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.polygon.com/features/2015/7/27/9046895/razer-ouya-android-why|title=This is why Razer bought Ouya (Hint: China)|author=Brian Crecente|work=Polygon|date=July 27, 2015|access-date=October 22, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151021015700/http://www.polygon.com/features/2015/7/27/9046895/razer-ouya-android-why|archive-date=October 21, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Razer Forge TV {{!}} RZ16-0128x Support |url=https://mysupport.razer.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/3588/~/razer-forge-tv-%7C-rz16-0128x-support |access-date=2022-10-05 |website=mysupport.razer.com}}</ref>

On May 21, 2019, Razer released a statement which announced that online accounts and services would be discontinued on June 25, 2019. According to Razer, most apps would become unusable on the platform, many relying on the user accounts to work. Razer suggests that users may be able to transfer purchases to other storefront platforms like [[Google Play]], if developers and publishers agree to such.<ref name="Ouya shutting down" />

==Hardware==

[[File:Ouya-Controller-FL-Bare.jpg|thumb|right|The controller with the plates removed, revealing fabric pull-tabs to remove the batteries from the inserts]]
The Ouya is a {{convert|75|mm|in|abbr=off|adj=on|2}} cube designed to be used with a [[TV]] as the display via an [[HDMI|HDMI connection]]. It ships with a single wireless controller, but it can also support multiple controllers. Games are available via digital distribution or can be [[Sideloading|side-loaded]].{{Citation needed|date=June 2023}}

===Controller===

The Ouya controller is a typical gamepad with dual analog sticks, a directional pad, 4 face buttons (labeled O, U, Y, and A) and pairs of back bumpers and triggers. It also includes a single-touch touchpad in the center of the controller.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.engadget.com/2013/03/28/ouya-hands-on/ |title=The final OUYA retail console is ready, we go hands-on |date=March 29, 2013 |publisher=engadget.com |access-date=September 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160719110548/https://www.engadget.com/2013/03/28/ouya-hands-on/ |archive-date=July 19, 2016 |url-status=live  }}</ref> The Ouya controller also has magnetically attached faceplates which enclose the 2 AA batteries, one on each side of the removable plates.

Alternate controllers may be used with the console (including those from the [[Xbox 360]], [[PlayStation 3]], [[Wii]] and [[Wii U]]) but only for compatible games.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.pocket-lint.com/news/119164-ouya-works-xbox-360-ps3-controllers-wirelessly |title=OUYA works with Xbox 360 and PS3 controllers wirelessly |date=January 25, 2013 |publisher=pocket-lint.com |access-date=June 5, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130602041314/http://www.pocket-lint.com/news/119164-ouya-works-xbox-360-ps3-controllers-wirelessly |archive-date=June 2, 2013 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://indiegames.com/2013/05/matt_thorsons_towerfall_descen.html |title=Matt Thorson's TowerFall hits OUYA at launch, works with Xbox 360 controllers |date=May 25, 2013 |publisher=Indie Games |access-date=June 5, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130609072255/http://indiegames.com/2013/05/matt_thorsons_towerfall_descen.html |archive-date=June 9, 2013 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Reception==
[[File:OuyaDeveloperConsole.png|thumb|250px|OUYA Developer Console]]

===Pre-release===
While initial reception of the Ouya was positive, raising $3.7 million on Kickstarter in the first two days, there were a number of vocal critics who were skeptical of the ability of the fledgling company to deliver a product at all. On July 12, 2012, ''[[PC Magazine]]''{{'}}s Sascha Segan ran an [[op-ed]] entitled "Why Kickstarter's Ouya Looks Like a Scam"<ref>{{cite news |last=Segan |first=Sascha |url=https://www.pcmag.com/article2/0,2817,2407046,00.asp |title=Why Kickstarter's Ouya Looks Like a Scam |publisher=[[PCMag]] |date=July 12, 2012 |access-date=March 28, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130121213444/http://www.pcmag.com/article2/0,2817,2407046,00.asp |archive-date=January 21, 2013 |url-status=live }}</ref> which was critical not only of the Ouya but of all Kickstarter-funded hardware projects. ''Unreality Magazine'' defended the Ouya, stating "A scam implies some sort of intentionally illegal deceit. [...] Tapping multiple investors from multiple sources isn't a scam, it's not even illegal, it's business."<ref>{{cite web |author=Trackback URI |url=http://unrealitymag.com/index.php/2012/07/19/ouya-innovative-idea-or-sleazy-scam/ |title=Ouya: Innovative Idea or Sleazy Scam? |publisher=Unrealitymag.com |date=July 19, 2012 |access-date=March 28, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130111234540/http://unrealitymag.com/index.php/2012/07/19/ouya-innovative-idea-or-sleazy-scam/ |archive-date=January 11, 2013 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

''[[Engadget]]'' reviewed the Kickstarter pre-release version of the Ouya on April 3, 2013. While praising the low cost and ease of hacking the console, it reported issues with controller buttons becoming stuck beneath the controller plating and the right analog stick snagging on the plating. It also reported a slight lag between the controller and the console and went on to say the controller was "usable, but it's far from great."<ref>{{cite web |last=Stevens |first=Tim |url=https://www.engadget.com/2013/04/03/ouya-review-founding-backer-edition/ |title=OUYA review (founding backer edition) |date=April 3, 2013 |publisher=Engadget.com |access-date=April 4, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130426232645/http://www.engadget.com/2013/04/03/ouya-review-founding-backer-edition/ |archive-date=April 26, 2013 |url-status=live  }}</ref>

''[[The Verge]]'' reported similar issues with the controller and questioned its construction quality. While they praised the hacking and openness of the console, calling it "a device with lots of potential and few true limitations", the review was mostly negative and was critical of the interface and game launch choice and stated that "Ouya isn't a viable gaming platform, or a good console, or even a nice TV interface."<ref>{{cite web |author=David Pierce |url=https://www.theverge.com/2013/4/4/4180242/ouya-review |title=Ouya review: can an indie console take on Sony and Microsoft? |publisher=The Verge |date=February 12, 2013 |access-date=April 4, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130426203018/http://www.theverge.com/2013/4/4/4180242/ouya-review |archive-date=April 26, 2013 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Retail reception===
[[File:Ouya-Controller-FL.jpg|thumb|right|Early reviews criticized the Ouya's controller for lag and build quality issues.]]
''[[Tom's Hardware]]'' summarized early media reviews of the Ouya as "lukewarm".<ref name="toms">{{cite web | url = http://www.tomshardware.com/news/OUYA-gdgt-review-scores-Console-Kickstarter,23340.html | title = OUYA Launches to Lukewarm Early Reviews | publisher = Tom's Hardware | access-date = July 2, 2013 | date = July 2, 2013 | first = Kevin | last = Parrish }}</ref>

''[[Engadget]]'' reviewed the retail version of the Ouya, and noted a largely improved experience as compared to what they found with their pre-release unit. Improvements to the gamepad were "huge", and they found "that the UI has been cleaned up and sped up". Engadget concluded that their "latest experience with the Android-based gaming device [left them] feeling optimistic" and that the company was "taking customer feedback seriously".<ref>{{cite web|last=O'Brien|first=Terrence|title=Hands-on with the Ouya destined for store shelves|date=June 12, 2013 |url=https://www.engadget.com/2013/06/12/hands-on-with-ouya/|publisher=Engadget|access-date=July 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130618082852/http://www.engadget.com/2013/06/12/hands-on-with-ouya/|archive-date=June 18, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref>

''[[Digital Trends]]'' called the final retail console "a device with a lot of potential built with love", and called the design a "sleek and cool-looking cube filled with gamingy goodness". The mostly positive review cited a lot of potential for the future, but was tempered by noting deficiencies in performance ("as powerful as many current smartphones"), and pointing out that the Ouya won't be able to compete with the "big three" console makers on performance, but must rely on carving out a niche in the market.<ref>{{cite web|last=Fleming|first=Ryan|title=Ouya Review|date=June 25, 2013|url=http://www.digitaltrends.com/video-game-system-reviews/ouya-review/|publisher=Digital Trends|access-date=July 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130629150950/http://www.digitaltrends.com/video-game-system-reviews/ouya-review/|archive-date=June 29, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref>

''[[ExtremeTech]]'' found that Ouya "has a number of serious faults". They mentioned the sub-par controller, the connectivity issues, and games which worked flawlessly on smartphones but stuttered on the console. Also, they remarked that "there just aren't enough worthwhile games to play".<ref>{{cite web|title=Ouya to fund exclusive games in attempt to right console's worst wrong|date=July 18, 2013 |url=http://www.extremetech.com/extreme/161677-ouya-to-fund-exclusive-games-in-attempt-to-right-consoles-worst-wrong|publisher='[[ExtremeTech]]|access-date=July 21, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130721091610/http://www.extremetech.com/extreme/161677-ouya-to-fund-exclusive-games-in-attempt-to-right-consoles-worst-wrong|archive-date=July 21, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Free the Games Fund==
In July 2013, Ouya announced the "Free the Games Fund", a scheme to help fund developers, where Ouya would match any Kickstarter campaign if a minimum target of $50,000 was reached, and provided the game remained Ouya exclusive for six months.<ref name="freethegames">{{cite web | url = http://www.ouyaly.com/?p=556 | title = Fund your OUYA Game Development in 5 Steps (#FreetheGames) | publisher = Ouyaly.com | date = August 23, 2013 | access-date = August 23, 2013 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130723134142/http://www.ouyaly.com/?p=556 | archive-date = July 23, 2013 | url-status = live }}</ref> Suspicions were raised concerning the first two games to reach the target. Commentators noticed the small number of backers each pledging a high value amount, the large number of those who had never backed a project before, as well as the use of duplicate names and avatars that included those of celebrities.<ref name="mcvftg">{{cite web|url=http://www.mcvuk.com/news/read/suspicions-surround-kickstarted-ouya-exclusive-gridiron-thunder/0120517|title=UPDATED: Suspicions surround Kickstarted Ouya exclusive Gridiron Thunder|access-date=August 29, 2013|date=August 29, 2013|first=Ben|last=Parfitt|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130831072352/http://www.mcvuk.com/news/read/suspicions-surround-kickstarted-ouya-exclusive-gridiron-thunder/0120517|archive-date=August 31, 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref> This led some to suggest that the projects were artificially inflating their project's backing in order to receive extra money from Ouya. In addition, one project had a backer whose identity appeared to be taken from that of a missing person's case.<ref name="mcvftg" />{{Dubious|date=September 2026}}

Nevertheless, Ouya rejected any suspicion regarding the backing of the projects, and planned to continue with providing funding.<ref name="mcvftg" /><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.gameranx.com/features/id/17077/article/ouya-s-free-the-games-promotion-incites-dubious-kickstarter-behavior|title=Ouya's "Free the Games" Promotion Incites Dubious Kickstarter Behavior|access-date=August 29, 2013|date=August 29, 2013|first=Mitch|last=Bowman|publisher=Gameranx|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131028232537/http://www.gameranx.com/features/id/17077/article/ouya-s-free-the-games-promotion-incites-dubious-kickstarter-behavior/|archive-date=October 28, 2013|url-status=live}}</ref> In September 2013, funding for one of the games that had reached its target (''Elementary, My Dear Holmes'') was later suspended by Kickstarter.<ref name="crowdfundinsider">{{cite web | url = http://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2013/09/22319-controversial-kickstarter-gridiron-thunder-closes-fully-funded | title = Controversial Kickstarter Gridiron Thunder Closes Fully Funded (Updated) | access-date = September 11, 2013 | date = September 11, 2013 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130910132137/http://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2013/09/22319-controversial-kickstarter-gridiron-thunder-closes-fully-funded/ | archive-date = September 10, 2013 | url-status = live }}</ref> The developers of the other funded game, ''Gridiron Thunder'', threatened litigation against a commenter on the Kickstarter page,<ref name="crowdfundinsider" /> and further dismissed concerns that they would have no rights to official NFL branding, a license currently held by [[Electronic Arts]].<ref>{{cite web | url = https://kotaku.com/accused-kickstarter-scammers-we-are-not-scamming-anyb-1224503476 | title = Accused Kickstarter Scammers: "We Are Not Scamming Anybody" | access-date = September 11, 2013 | date = August 29, 2013 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130902215039/http://kotaku.com/accused-kickstarter-scammers-we-are-not-scamming-anyb-1224503476 | archive-date = September 2, 2013 | url-status = live  }}</ref> In the same month, another project, ''Dungeons the Eye of Draconus'', caused controversy by openly stating that a relative of one developer had provided substantial additional backing in order to have the project qualify for money from the Free the Games fund.<ref name="pafgf" /> The project was removed by Ouya from the Free the Games fund, resulting in the developers removing the project from Kickstarter.<ref name="pafgf">{{cite web|url=http://penny-arcade.com/report/article/ouya-shuts-down-yet-another-kickstarter-debacle |title=OUYA shuts down yet another Kickstarter debacle |first=Dabe |last=Alan |access-date=September 19, 2013 |date=September 18, 2013 |publisher=Penny Arcade |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131211193414/http://penny-arcade.com/report/article/ouya-shuts-down-yet-another-kickstarter-debacle |archive-date=December 11, 2013 }}</ref>

Many developers criticized the fund's rules. Sophie Houlden removed her game, ''[[Rose and Time]]'', from the Ouya marketplace in protest.<ref name="naive">{{cite web | url = https://gigaom.com/2013/10/31/ouya-ceo-uhrman-we-were-too-naive-about-its-free-the-games-fund-trouble/ | title = Ouya CEO Uhrman: We were "too naive" about its Free The Games fund trouble | first = Lauren | last = Hockenson | access-date = June 3, 2014 | date = October 21, 2013 | publisher = Gigaom | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140531140145/https://gigaom.com/2013/10/31/ouya-ceo-uhrman-we-were-too-naive-about-its-free-the-games-fund-trouble/ | archive-date = May 31, 2014 | url-status = dead }}</ref> Matt Gilgenbach, who was trying to finance his game ''[[Neverending Nightmares]]'' with help from the fund, said, "It would kill me if due to other projects abusing the Free the Games Fund, people lost confidence in our project and what we are trying to do...While I believe in the idea of the Free the Games Fund, I think it definitely could use some reform in light of the potential avenues for abuse."<ref name="nightmares">{{cite web | url = http://www.joystiq.com/2013/09/13/neverending-nightmares-and-the-dream-of-ouyas-free-the-games/ | title = 'Neverending Nightmares' and the dream of Ouya's Free the Games Fund | first = Mike | last = Suszek | access-date = June 3, 2014 | date = September 13, 2013 | website = Joystiq | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140930202226/http://www.joystiq.com/2013/09/13/neverending-nightmares-and-the-dream-of-ouyas-free-the-games/ | archive-date = September 30, 2014 | url-status = dead }}</ref> A month later, Sophie revealed that she and several other developers had been in talks with Ouya boss Julie Uhrman,<ref name="sophiehoulden.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.sophiehoulden.com/rose-and-time-back-on-ouya//|title=Rose and Time back on Ouya}}</ref> who accepted their criticism, saying: "Developers were telling us over and over, 'You're being too idealistic, and you're being too naive.' That was the part that personally took me a while to understand." Ouya changed the fund rules, including adding a dollar-per-backer limit. Satisfied the matter was resolved, ''[[Rose and Time]]'' was returned to the Ouya marketplace,<ref name="wired.com">{{cite web|url=https://www.wired.co.uk/news/archive/2013-10/24/rose-and-time|title=Developer returns Rose and Time to Ouya store|access-date=May 27, 2021|archive-date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304224236/http://www.wired.co.uk/news/archive/2013-10/24/rose-and-time|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="eurogamer.net">{{cite web|url=http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2013-10-24-rose-and-time-returns-to-ouya-after-dev-says-problems-dont-exist-any-more|title=Rose and Time returns to Ouya after dev accepts Free the Games fund changes|website=[[Eurogamer]]|date=October 24, 2013}}</ref> and ''Neverending Nightmares'' qualified for funding under the new rules.<ref name="naive" />

On September 18, 2013, Ouya modified the exclusivity clause of the fund. Developers would still not be able to release their software on mobile devices, video game consoles, and set-top boxes during the six-month exclusivity period, but they would be allowed to release on other personal computer systems, such as Windows, Mac OS X, and Linux, during that time.<ref name="freethegames-rules">{{cite web |url=http://www.freethegamesfund.com/rules.html |title=OUYA "FREE THE GAMES FUND" OFFICIAL RULES, VERSION 2 |date=September 18, 2013 |website=FreeTheGamesFund.com |access-date=September 19, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130921053734/http://www.freethegamesfund.com/rules.html |archive-date=September 21, 2013 |url-status=live }}</ref>
