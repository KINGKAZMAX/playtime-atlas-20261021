{{Nihongo foot|'''''The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild'''''|ゼルダの伝説 ブレス オブ ザ ワイルド|Zeruda no Densetsu: Buresu obu za Wairudo|lead=yes|group=lower-alpha}} is a 2017 [[action-adventure game]] developed by [[Nintendo EPD]] for the [[Wii U]] and [[Nintendo Switch]]. Set near the end of the [[Zelda timeline|''Zelda'' timeline]], it follows [[Link (The Legend of Zelda)|Link]] as he sets out to save [[Princess Zelda]] and prevent [[Calamity Ganon]] from destroying the world. The player explores the [[open world]] of [[Hyrule]], collects items, and completes objectives such as puzzles and [[side quests]]. ''Breath of the Wild''{{'s}} world is unstructured and encourages exploration and experimentation; the story can be completed in a [[Nonlinear gameplay|nonlinear]] fashion.

The five-year development commenced after the release of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword]]'' (2011). Led by the director [[Hidemaro Fujibayashi]] and the producer [[Eiji Aonuma]], EPD sought to rethink ''[[The Legend of Zelda|Zelda]]''{{'s}} conventions and introduced elements such as detailed chemistry and [[physics engines]]. EPD drew inspiration from ''[[Shadow of the Colossus]]'' (2005) and ''[[The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim]]'' (2011). [[Monolith Soft]], developer of the ''[[Xenoblade]]'' series, assisted in designing landscapes and topography due to their experience in developing open world games.

''Breath of the Wild'' was released on March 3, 2017, as the final [[Nintendo]]-published Wii U game and as a [[launch title|launch]] game for the Switch. It received acclaim, with praise for its gameplay, open-world design, and attention to detail, though some reviewers criticized its technical performance. ''Breath of the Wild'' won numerous year-end accolades, including [[List of Game of the Year awards|Game of the Year]] at the [[The Game Awards 2017|2017 Game Awards]] and the [[21st Annual D.I.C.E. Awards]]. It broke sales records for a Nintendo launch game and sold 35.76 million copies by June 2026, making it the bestselling ''Zelda'' game and one of the [[Best-selling video games of all time|bestselling video games]].

''Breath of the Wild'' is considered one of the [[List of video games listed among the best|greatest video games of all time]]; journalists described it as a landmark in open world design for its emphasis on experimentation, physics-based [[sandbox game|sandbox]], and [[emergent gameplay]]. Numerous developers cited ''Breath of the Wild'' as inspiration, and it is a popular point of comparison among open-world games. A [[Spinoff (media)|spinoff]], ''[[Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity]]'', was released in 2020, and a sequel, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom]]'', was released in 2023. An enhanced version for the [[Nintendo Switch 2]] was released on June 5, 2025.

==Gameplay==

As Link, players can perform actions such as running, climbing, swimming, and gliding with a [[paraglider]], although Link is limited by his [[Endurance|stamina]].<ref name="GSpot"/> Link can procure items from the environment, including weapons, food, and other resources. Unlike in previous ''Zelda'' games, weapons and shields degrade through use, eventually shattering.<ref name="GSpot"/> Many items have multiple uses; for example, wooden weapons can light fires, wooden shields can collect incoming enemy arrows, and certain shields can be used as makeshift [[snowboard]]s.<ref name="GSpot"/> Players can obtain food and materials for [[elixir]]s from hunting animals, gathering wild fruit, or collecting parts of defeated enemies.<ref name="IGN"/> By cooking combinations of food or materials, the player can create meals and elixirs that can replenish Link's health and stamina, or provide temporary status bonuses such as increased strength or resistance to heat or cold.<ref name="IGN"/>

An important tool in Link's arsenal is the "Sheikah Slate", a magical stone tablet which can be used to mark waypoints on the in-game map and take pictures of materials, creatures, and enemies. These pictures are stored in the "Hyrule Compendium", an in-game photo album that can be used to locate valuable items. ==Plot==
{{Further|The Legend of Zelda#Fictional chronology|label1=Fictional chronology of The Legend of Zelda}}
''Breath of the Wild'' takes place at the end of the [[The Legend of Zelda#Fictional chronology|''Zelda'' timeline]] in the kingdom of [[Hyrule]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ign.com/articles/2018/08/06/the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-now-has-an-official-place-in-the-series-timeline-sort-of|title=The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild Now Has an Official Place in the Series' Timeline... Sort Of|last1=Arif|first1=Shabana|website=IGN|date=August 6, 2018|access-date=August 6, 2018|archive-date=August 28, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190828110555/https://www.ign.com/articles/2018/08/06/the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-now-has-an-official-place-in-the-series-timeline-sort-of|url-status=live}}</ref> An [[Amnesia|amnesiac]] warrior named Link awakens in a chamber called the Shrine of Resurrection, located on the [[Great Plateau]] overlooking Hyrule. He encounters an old man who eventually reveals himself as the lingering spirit of [[Universe of The Legend of Zelda#King of Hyrule|Rhoam]], the last king of Hyrule. He explains that Link has been slumbering in the Shrine of Resurrection for a century, and narrates the history of the kingdom and Link's own past. 

In ancient times, the Sheikah race had developed Hyrule into an advanced civilization, protected by four enormous animalistic machines called the Divine Beasts and an army of autonomous weapons called Guardians. When an ancient evil known as [[Calamity Ganon]] appeared and threatened Hyrule, four great warriors were given the title of Champion, and each piloted one of the Divine Beasts to weaken Ganon. Meanwhile, the princess of Hyrule, who possessed the blood of the Goddess [[Hylia (The Legend of Zelda)|Hylia]], sealed Ganon away while being protected by her appointed knight.

Ten millennia later, the kingdom of Hyrule had regressed to a [[Medieval technology|medieval state]].<ref name="CreatingAChampion">{{cite book |author1=Nintendo |last2=Naoyukikayama |last3=Kikai |last4=Akinorisao |last5=Mikame |first5=Chasito |last6=Thorpe |first6=Patrick |date=November 2018 |title=The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild - Creating a Champion |publisher=Dark Horse Books |page=341 |quote=Hylian Building Ruins [[Romanesque architecture|Romanesque]] style (thick walls, small windows). |isbn=978-1-5067-1010-5}}</ref> Reading their ancestors' prophecies, Hyrule's peoples recognized the signs of Ganon's return and excavated surrounding areas to uncover the Divine Beasts and Guardians. During this time, [[Princess Zelda]] trained vigorously to awaken the sealing magic needed to defeat Ganon while trying to maintain her personal research into the Divine Beasts. A knight was appointed to protect her: Link, who was chosen due to his ability to wield the [[Master Sword]]. After being sought out by Zelda, the Champions—[[Mipha]], princess of the aquatic [[Zoras|Zora]]; [[Revali]], archer of the birdlike [[Rito (The Legend of Zelda)|Rito]]; [[Daruk]], warrior of the mountainous [[Goron]]s; and [[Urbosa]], chief of the desert-dwelling [[Gerudo]]—assembled to pilot the Divine Beasts while Zelda and Link would face Ganon directly.

When Ganon appeared on Zelda's seventeenth birthday, he possessed the Guardians and Divine Beasts, turning them against Hyrule. King Rhoam and the Champions were killed, and Link was gravely wounded protecting Zelda.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Nintendo |title=[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild – Creating a Champion]] |publisher=[[Dark Horse Comics]] |year=2018 |pages=65 |language=en |isbn=978-1-5067-1010-5 |quote=When Calamity strikes, Link drives back the Guardians while protecting Princess Zelda, but his strength gives out before they reach Fort Hateno.}}</ref> Zelda unlocked her sealing powers, using them to save Link. Zelda had Link taken to the Shrine of Resurrection for him to heal, hid the Master Sword in Korok Forest under the protection of the [[Great Deku Tree]], and used her light magic to seal herself and Ganon in Hyrule Castle. Rhoam finishes his story by explaining that Ganon has grown stronger while sealed in Hyrule Castle, and pleads for Link to defeat Ganon before he regains his strength and destroys the world.<ref name="IGN always wanted2">{{Cite web |url=http://www.ign.com/articles/2016/06/14/e3-2016-the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-hands-on-preview |title=E3 2016: The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild Might Be The Open World Zelda We Always Wanted |last=Otero |first=Jose |date=June 14, 2016 |publisher=[[Ziff Davis]] |work=[[IGN]] |access-date=June 14, 2016 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160614182547/http://www.ign.com/articles/2016/06/14/e3-2016-the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-hands-on-preview |archive-date=June 14, 2016}}</ref>

Link leaves the plateau and reunites with [[Impa]], a Sheikah elder who previously served the royal family, and she instructs him to free the Divine Beasts. Link travels across Hyrule, returning to locations from his past and regaining his memories. At the behest of Hyrule's peoples, he boards the four Divine Beasts and severs them from Ganon's control, freeing the spirits of Hyrule's fallen Champions and allowing them to pilot the Divine Beasts. In this endeavor, Link is assisted by [[Prince Sidon|Sidon]], the Zora prince and Mipha's younger brother; [[Yunobo]], Daruk's cowardly descendant; [[Characters of The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom#Teba|Teba]], a Rito warrior; and [[Riju]], the young chief of the Gerudo. After obtaining the Master Sword from Korok Forest, Link enters Hyrule Castle and confronts Ganon. The Divine Beasts weaken him, and after Link defeats him, he transforms into Dark Beast Ganon. Zelda gives Link the Bow of Light, which allows him to weaken Ganon enough for Zelda to seal him away, restoring peace and allowing the spirits of King Rhoam and the Champions to depart to the afterlife. If the player completes an optional quest that involves Link regaining all of his memories, they unlock a [[Post-credits scene|post-credits cutscene]] in which Zelda declares that Hyrule must be rebuilt and that she and Link must begin the process by themselves. As Link and Zelda survey Hyrule, the princess confides in him that although her powers may have dwindled over time, she has come to terms with it.

Before full development, the developers designed a playable 2D prototype similar to the original ''Zelda'' to experiment with physics-based puzzles. It was removed after the developers found it gave the player too much mobility and broke the climbing mechanic.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Goldfarb |first=Andrew |date=December 9, 2017 |title=Zelda: Breath of the Wild's Cut Dual Hookshots Had Link Swinging 'Like Spider-Man', Says Director |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2017/12/09/zelda-breath-of-the-wilds-cut-dual-hookshots-had-link-swinging-like-spider-man-says-director |access-date=June 18, 2022 |website=IGN |language=en |archive-date=June 18, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220618071058/https://www.ign.com/articles/2017/12/09/zelda-breath-of-the-wilds-cut-dual-hookshots-had-link-swinging-like-spider-man-says-director |url-status=live}}</ref> As previous ''Zelda'' games increased the amount of actions Link could perform, the development team realized that having too many actions would complicate the controls, so instead of adding more actions, they increased the number of events the player could interact with in the world.<ref>Dark Horse Books (2018), p. 423</ref>

=== Downloadable content ===
Throughout 2017, Nintendo released two packs of [[downloadable content]] (DLC) via an "expansion pass": ''The Master Trials'' and ''The Champions' Ballad''.<ref name="DLC">{{cite web |url=https://www.polygon.com/e3/2017/6/13/15791860/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-e3-expansion |title=See Zelda: Breath of the Wild's two expansions in action |first=Ben |last=Kuchera |date=June 13, 2017 |website=Polygon |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180114224648/https://www.polygon.com/e3/2017/6/13/15791860/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-e3-expansion |archive-date=January 14, 2018}}</ref>{{r|Verge: $20}} ''The Master Trials'', released in June, adds gameplay modes, features, and items. In the "Trial of the Sword" challenge, the player faces three trials, consisting of twelve, sixteen, and twenty-three rooms respectively. Each room is full of enemies which the player must defeat before proceeding. Every room must be completed before the player can claim their reward.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.polygon.com/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-guide-walkthrough/2017/7/3/15910780/trial-of-the-sword-tips-strategy-help-weapons-items-food-armor-stealth-bombs-stasis-magnesis |title=Zelda Breath of the Wild guide: 17 tips for winning Trial of the Sword |first=Jeffrey |last=Parkin |date=July 3, 2017 |website=Polygon |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170703210147/https://www.polygon.com/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-guide-walkthrough/2017/7/3/15910780/trial-of-the-sword-tips-strategy-help-weapons-items-food-armor-stealth-bombs-stasis-magnesis |archive-date=July 3, 2017}}</ref> The player begins with no equipment and will lose all progress if they receive a game over. At the end of each trial the player's Master Sword has its attack power upgraded by ten points and the blue sheen of its blade grows slightly. By the end of the third trial, the Master Sword will have doubled in attack power and will glow blue. The pack also adds an optional higher [[difficulty level]], Master Mode, which adds faster rank leveling and raises the ranks of enemies by 1.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://kotaku.com/in-master-mode-breath-of-the-wild-s-starting-area-is-t-1797019585 |title=In Master Mode, Breath Of The Wild's Starting Area Is The Game At Its Best |website=[[Kotaku]] |first=Kirk |last=Hamilton |date=July 18, 2017 |access-date=November 22, 2019 |archive-date=August 9, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200809031224/https://kotaku.com/in-master-mode-breath-of-the-wild-s-starting-area-is-t-1797019585 |url-status=live}}</ref> The enemies are more perceptive when Link sneaks near them and slowly regenerate health in battle. New floating platforms throughout the land offer enemies to battle and treasure as a reward. The Hero's Path feature draws the player's path on the map, designed to help players determine places they have not visited. The player can also find the hidden Travel Medallion to save Link's current position as a single waypoint to which the player can transport Link at any time. New items include the Korok Mask, which alerts the player when a Korok is nearby, and other themed cosmetics related to previous ''Zelda'' games.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://time.com/4763060/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-master-trials/ |title=Not Finishing 'Breath of the Wild' Just Got Easier |first=Matt |last=Peckham |magazine=Time |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170607001232/http://time.com/4763060/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-master-trials/ |archive-date=June 7, 2017}}</ref>

''Breath of the Wild'' received "universal acclaim", according to the [[review aggregator]] website [[Metacritic]].<ref name="MCNS"/><ref name="MCWIIU"/> It is the [[2017 in video gaming#Critically acclaimed games|highest-rated game of 2017]] on Metacritic,<ref name="Metacritic Best">{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/browse/games/score/metascore/year/all/filtered?sort=desc&year_selected=2017 |title=Best Video Games for 2017 |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=August 29, 2017 |archive-date=August 20, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170820041442/http://www.metacritic.com/browse/games/score/metascore/year/all/filtered?year_selected=2017&sort=desc |url-status=live}}</ref> and holds the largest number of perfect reviews of any game from any year.<ref name="VG247: perfect" /><ref name="Polygon: all time" /> Fellow review aggregator [[OpenCritic]] assessed that the game received "mighty" approval, being recommended by 96% of critics,<ref name="opencritic"/> making it the highest-rated Wii U game on the site,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://opencritic.com/browse/wii-u|title=Best Wii U Video Games of All Time|publisher=[[OpenCritic]]|access-date=March 19, 2026}}</ref> as well as the second-highest-rated Nintendo Switch game, surpassed only by ''[[Super Mario Odyssey]]'' (2017).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://opencritic.com/browse/switch|title=Best Nintendo Switch Video Games of All Time|publisher=[[OpenCritic]]|access-date=March 19, 2026}}</ref> Several critics called it a masterpiece.{{efn|name=masterpiece|Reviewers who called the game a "masterpiece" include those of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'',<ref name="Time review"/> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'',<ref name="EW review"/> ''[[IGN]]'',<ref name="IGN review p2"/> ''[[Eurogamer]]'',<ref name="Eurogamer: portable"/> and ''[[The A.V. Club]]''.<ref name="AV Club: all time"/>}}

The open-world gameplay received praise. Jose Otero of ''[[IGN]]'' described it as "a masterclass in open-world design" and "a wonderful sandbox full of mystery, dangling dozens upon dozens of tantalizing things in front of you that just beg to be explored".<ref name="IGN"/> ''[[GameSpot]]'' called it the most impressive game Nintendo had made, writing that it "takes designs and mechanics perfected in other games and reworks them for its own purposes to create something wholly new, but also something that still feels quintessentially like a ''Zelda'' game ... It's both a return to form and a leap into uncharted territory, and it exceeds expectations on both fronts".<ref name="GSpot"/> ''[[Edge (magazine)|Edge]]'' wrote that the world was "an absolute, and unremitting, pleasure to get lost in" and that "the magic of being given all the tools in the opening hour is the knowledge that the solution to any problem is already at your disposal, and you can always change tack".<ref name="Edge"/> According to ''[[Digital Trends]]'', the ability to climb any surface and glide across the map gave players freedom of movement that "broke the genre open, pushing developers to rethink how players interact with the world" and putting extra thought into vertical design. In contrast to "[[Ubisoft]]-style" games that filled the map with points of interest and objectives, ''Breath of the Wild''{{'}}s free-form exploration encourages players to set their own objectives, marking the map with discoveries that serve as a record of what players have accomplished.<ref name=":0"/>

According to Kyle Orland of ''[[Ars Technica]]'', the open-world design reinvented ''Zelda'' formula, in comparison to other entries of the franchise.<ref name="Ars Technica review">{{cite web|url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2017/03/refreshing-new-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-is-an-instant-classic/|title=Breath of the Wild may be the best Zelda game ever|last=Orland|first=Kyle|date=March 3, 2017|work=[[Ars Technica]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170330145059/https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2017/03/refreshing-new-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-is-an-instant-classic/|archive-date=March 30, 2017|url-status=live|access-date=March 31, 2017}}</ref> Journalists commented on unexpected interactions between game elements,<ref name="USgamer: coolest" /><ref name="Polygon: naked" /><ref name="Polygon: dogs" /> with serendipitous moments proving popular on [[social media]].<ref name="The Verge: future" /> Chris Plante of ''[[The Verge]]'' predicted that whereas prior open-world games tended to feature prescribed challenges, ''Zelda'' would influence a new generation of games with open-ended problem-solving.<ref name="The Verge: future"/> ''Digital Trends'' wrote that the level of experimentation allowed players to interact with and exploit the environment in creative ways, resulting in various "tricks" still discovered years after release.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|date=December 23, 2021|title=What Makes an Open World Game Breath of the Wild-like?|url=https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/breath-of-the-wild-likes/|access-date=January 5, 2022|website=Digital Trends|language=en|archive-date=January 5, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220105044519/https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/breath-of-the-wild-likes/|url-status=live}}</ref>
