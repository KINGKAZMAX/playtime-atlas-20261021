'''''Candy Crush Saga''''' is a free-to-play [[tile-matching video game]] released by [[King (company)|King]] on April 12, 2012, originally for [[Facebook]]; other versions for [[iOS]], [[Android (operating system)|Android]], [[Windows Phone]], and [[Windows 10]] followed. It is a variation of their [[browser game]] ''Candy Crush''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.games.com/game/king/candy-crush |title=Candy Crush at Games.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150906021332/http://www.games.com/game/king/candy-crush |archive-date=September 6, 2015 }}</ref>

In the game, players complete levels by swapping colored pieces of candy on a game board to make a match of three or more of the same color, eliminating those candies from the board and replacing them with new ones, which could potentially create further matches. Matches of four or more candies create unique candies that act as power-ups with larger board-clearing abilities. Boards have various goals that must be completed within a fixed number of moves, such as collecting a specific number of a type of candy.

The game uses a [[freemium]] model; while it can be played completely through without spending money, players can buy special actions to help clear more difficult boards, from which King makes its revenues—at its peak, the company was reportedly earning almost $1 million per day.<ref name="whatappealcandycrush-bbc">{{Cite news |date=2013-12-18 |title=What is the appeal of Candy Crush Saga? |language=en-GB |work=BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-25334716 |access-date=2023-12-22 |archive-date=August 23, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240823203211/https://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-25334716 |url-status=live }}</ref> Around 2014, over 93 million people were playing ''Candy Crush Saga'', while revenue over a three-month period as reported by King was over $493 million.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web|url=http://www.theguardian.com/technology/2014/mar/26/candy-crush-saga-king-why-popular|title=Why is Candy Crush Saga so popular?|date=March 26, 2014|website=the Guardian}}</ref> Five years after its release on mobile, the ''Candy Crush Saga'' series has received over 2.7 billion downloads, and the game has been one of the highest-grossing and most-played mobile apps in that time frame. As of September 2023, it had reached over $20 billion in lifetime revenue, making it one of the [[List of highest-grossing media franchises|highest-grossing media franchises]] of all time.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.gamesindustry.biz/kings-candy-crush-saga-hits-20bn-in-lifetime-revenue | title=King's Candy Crush Saga hits $20bn in lifetime revenue | date=September 27, 2023 | access-date=April 24, 2025 | archive-date=September 27, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230927183216/https://www.gamesindustry.biz/kings-candy-crush-saga-hits-20bn-in-lifetime-revenue | url-status=live }}</ref>

King has since released three related titles—''Candy Crush Soda Saga'', ''Candy Crush Jelly Saga'' and ''Candy Crush Friends Saga''—and most of the company's other mobile titles follow the same ''Saga'' freemium format.

==Gameplay==
[[File:Candy Crush Saga game setup example.jpg|thumb|left|Gameplay on iOS, with candy, striped candies, jelly, licorice lock, and chocolate]]

''Candy Crush Saga'' is a [[tile-matching video game|"match three" game]], where the core gameplay is based on swapping two adjacent candies among several on the gameboard to make a row or column of at least three matching-colored candies. On this match, the matched candies are removed from the board, and candies above them fall into the empty spaces, with new candies appearing from the top of the board. This may create a new matched set of candies, which is automatically cleared in the same manner. The player scores points for these matches and gains progressively more points for chain reactions (called cascades).<ref name="cnet review">{{cite web | url = https://www.cnet.com/products/candy-crush-saga-android/review/ | title = Candy Crush Saga for Android review: Great alternative to Bejeweled | first = Jaymar | last = Cabebe | date = April 3, 2013 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[CNet]] | archive-date = August 30, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170830235351/https://www.cnet.com/products/candy-crush-saga-android/review/ | url-status = live }}</ref> Additionally, creating matches of four or more candies will create a special candy that, when matched, can clear a row, column, or other section of the board.<ref name="cnet review"/>

The game is split into many levels, which must be completed in sequence. Each level poses a different challenge to the user, such as removing jelly on tiles or clearing candies in a fixed number of moves to bring special ingredients to the bottom of the board. Boards have a number of different configurations and may include special spaces that have their own unique rules, such as spaces covered with jelly that must be cleared by making a match on that space.<ref name="cnet review"/> If the player meets the level's goal, they will be given stars based on their score and can proceed onto the next level. Otherwise, they will lose one life and must try again. If the player runs out of lives, they have to wait for some period of real-world time while their lives regenerate before attempting the level again.<ref name="cnet review"/> Completed levels can be replayed if desired.

The game has been expanded with a number of episodes, adding 15 new levels per episode as well as new gameplay mechanics. Each episode has 15 levels of gameplay. In the game's first major expansion, the game added a separate set of levels in the Dreamworld. While levels had the same goals, the players had to balance matches of candies of two randomly selected colors to prevent Odus the Owl from waking up; if they failed, the level had to be repeated. If they collected enough matched candies to fill a meter, the game would automatically activate the Moon Struck power: the board was cleared of all candies of those two colors, and the player gained a few turns of additional matches without needing to balance colors. After this, Odus returned to sleeping and two new colors were randomly selected for the balance. This continued until the player completed the level or ran out of turns as in the main game. Dreamworld levels used a different set of lives from the main game, allowing the player to switch back and forth between these modes. The Dreamworld is no longer accessible.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://kotaku.com/the-first-candy-crush-saga-expansion-changes-the-way-th-1481974117 | title = The First Candy Crush Saga Expansion Changes The Way The Game Is Played | first = Mike | last = Fahey | date = December 12, 2013 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Kotaku]] | archive-date = December 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161220104113/http://kotaku.com/the-first-candy-crush-saga-expansion-changes-the-way-th-1481974117 | url-status = live }}</ref>

===In-app purchases===
The game is primarily monetized through [[Microtransaction|in-app purchase]]s (through either a [[credit card]], [[iTunes]] credits or [[Google Play]] credits); players begin with five "lives", lost whenever a level is failed. This applies to all of King's games. When lives are exhausted, users can either send requests to their Facebook friends for more lives, wait for them to replenish themselves (a life is restored every half-hour), or purchase them. Initially, when a player makes a purchase for new lives, they receive five new lives for a specific dollar amount. In a recent update, when a player makes a purchase for new lives, the player receives gold bars, the quantity depending on how much money they spend. Gold bars can be used for new lives, extra moves, boosters or to unlock a new episode. At certain points, primarily at the start of new "episodes", users must also either purchase or receive a request from at least three friends before they may access the next set of levels. An update meant players waited for only three days to unlock the next episode.

Boosters, to make the levels easier, can be bought using in-app purchases.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sugar Coma|url=http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/technology/2013/07/candy_crush_saga_the_most_addictive_game_since_angry_birds.html|work=Slate|date=July 2013|access-date=January 24, 2014|archive-date=January 31, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140131110956/http://www.slate.com/articles/technology/technology/2013/07/candy_crush_saga_the_most_addictive_game_since_angry_birds.html|url-status=live}}</ref> While the game includes [[freemium]] content, 97.7% of those playing the game do so for free, while only 2.3% pay.<ref name="auto">[https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2015/feb/13/candy-crush-saga-players-855m-2014 Candy Crush Saga players spent £865m on the game in 2014 alone] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170131045834/https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2015/feb/13/candy-crush-saga-players-855m-2014 |date=January 31, 2017 }}, theguardian.com, February 13, 2015.</ref>

==Characters==
Throughout the game, the player solves puzzles so Tiffi (short for Toffette) can solve problems plaguing the residents of the Candy Kingdom. These include tutorial guide Mr. Toffee, whose voice was changed from an over-the-top French accent in the original version of the game into a more modest deep male voice;<ref name="whatappealcandycrush-bbc" /> the [[Easter Bunny]]; the shop owner Mr. Yeti; Odus the owl from Dreamworld levels; the villainous Bubblegum Troll; and many others.

In ''Candy Crush Jelly Saga'', the two main characters that the player plays against are the Jelly Queen and Cupcake Carl.{{citation needed|date=November 2020}}

==Development==
Prior to the release of ''Candy Crush Saga'', most of King's games were [[browser game]]s offered through their website or partner portals such as [[Yahoo!]]. This included ''Candy Crush'', a straightforward tile-matching game released in 2011 which King's chief creative officer and co-founder Sebastian Knutsson said came after a few hundred other games they had designed for the portal.<ref name="apple">{{cite web | url = https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1462263204 | title = Behind the Scenes: The Magical Making of Candy Crush | access-date = August 24, 2020 | publisher = [[Apple, Inc.]] | archive-date = December 12, 2023 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20231212143405/https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1462263204 | url-status = live }}</ref> ''Candy Crush''{{'}}s concept had been based on an early game King made, ''Miner Speed'', that borrowed gameplay elements from ''[[Bejeweled (video game)|Bejeweled]]''.<ref name="vb miner speed"/> ''Candy Crush'' added new animations for the candies, and expressive [[paper doll]]-like characters that helped to make the game one of five most popular ones on the site by 2012.<ref name="vb aug14"/> At that point, the game was a basic [[score attack]] game. Knutsson said, "the first version was three minutes of great gaming, but that three minutes didn't really evolve."<ref name="apple"/> ''Candy Crush'', as with several of King's other portal games, featured tournament-style gameplay, where players could spend money to enter competitive tourneys for in-game boosts, which served as one of the main forms of revenue for the company in addition to in-game item sale [[microtransactions]] and advertisements.<ref name="latimes 2012">{{cite web | url = http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/entertainmentnewsbuzz/2012/04/kingcom-topples-ea-wooga-on-facebook-for-now.html | title = Games publisher King.com topples EA, Wooga on Facebook, for now | first = Alex | last = Pham | date = April 17, 2012 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Los Angeles Times]] | archive-date = October 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161020104330/http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/entertainmentnewsbuzz/2012/04/kingcom-topples-ea-wooga-on-facebook-for-now.html | url-status = live }}</ref>

Around 2009, [[Facebook]] began to pull in developers, in particular [[Zynga]], to offer [[social network game]]s that could be built on its fundamental services; for King, this resulted in a large drop in players at their game portals within a year. At this point, King started to determine how it could enter the Facebook and the associated [[mobile gaming|mobile game]] markets, breaking up its web development department to work on Facebook and mobile games in 2010, including bringing several of their existing browser games to those platforms.<ref name="informilo">{{cite web | url = http://www.informilo.com/2014/06/king-saga-story-behind-maker-candy-crush-saga/ | title = King Saga: The Story Behind The Maker Of Candy Crush Saga | first = Ben | last = Rooney | date = June 19, 2014 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Informilo]] | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170207063515/http://www.informilo.com/2014/06/king-saga-story-behind-maker-candy-crush-saga/ | archive-date = February 7, 2017 | url-status = dead }}</ref> Most of these existing games were introduced as beta versions to Facebook users, and the company used player counts and feedback to determine which of these titles had the most prospect for moving forward, allowing them to focus more intensive development on those titles while dropping the rest, in the style of a [[rapid prototyping]] approach.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.siliconbeat.com/2012/11/15/if-social-games-on-facebook-are-dying-why-is-king-com-booming/ | title = If social games on Facebook are dying, why is King.com booming? | first = Chris | last = O'Brien | date = November 15, 2012 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[San Jose Mercury News]] | archive-date = October 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161020040613/http://www.siliconbeat.com/2012/11/15/if-social-games-on-facebook-are-dying-why-is-king-com-booming/ | url-status = dead }}</ref> The Facebook platform allowed them to explore expansion of their existing tournament-style games and the ability to include microtransactions within the game.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2011/02/18/king-com-takes-tournament-games-into-facebook/ | title = King.com takes tournament games into Facebook | first = Dean | last = Takahashi | date = February 18, 2011 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = October 29, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161029011452/http://venturebeat.com/2011/02/18/king-com-takes-tournament-games-into-facebook/ | url-status = live }}</ref>

In April 2011, King released its existing portal game ''Miner Speed'' as its first cross-platform (Facebook and mobile) game to figure out the transition between Facebook and mobile games for this new direction.<ref name="vb miner speed">{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2011/04/28/king-com-launches-its-first-cross-platform-mobile-game-exclusive/ | title = King.com launches its first cross-platform mobile game | first = Dean | last = Takahashi | date = April 28, 2011 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = September 13, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20160913220328/http://venturebeat.com/2011/04/28/king-com-launches-its-first-cross-platform-mobile-game-exclusive/ | url-status = live }}</ref><ref name="informilo"/> King's first major success in this area followed with ''Bubble Witch Saga'', released in October 2011; by January 2012 it has attracted over 10 million players and was one of fastest rising Facebook games at that time.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.gamasutra.com/view/news/128892/Fastestgrowing_Facebook_games_From_Tetris_Battle_to_Words_With_Friends.php | title = Fastest-growing Facebook games: From Tetris Battle to Words With Friends | first = Eric | last = Caoili | date = January 10, 2012 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Gamasutra]] | archive-date = October 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161020051521/http://www.gamasutra.com/view/news/128892/Fastestgrowing_Facebook_games_From_Tetris_Battle_to_Words_With_Friends.php | url-status = dead }}</ref> ''Bubble Witch Saga'' introduced the "saga" approach in contrast to typical tile-matching games, where instead of having the game continue through a fixed amount of time or until the player reached an unplayable state, the game was divided into discrete levels that required the player to complete certain goals within a fixed set of moves, and where the next level could only be reached after completing the previous level. These saga elements allowed for the basics of social gameplay, but did not require the time investment that then-popular titles like Zynga's ''[[FarmVille]]'' required; players could play just for a few minutes each day through the saga model.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2012/04/12/at-2-5b-games-played-a-month-king-com-reaps-benefits-from-its-casual-saga-titles-on-facebook/ | title = At 2.5B games played a month, King.com reaps benefits from its casual Saga titles on Facebook | first = Dean | last = Takhashi | date = April 12, 2012 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = October 29, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161029011449/http://venturebeat.com/2012/04/12/at-2-5b-games-played-a-month-king-com-reaps-benefits-from-its-casual-saga-titles-on-facebook/ | url-status = live }}</ref> The success of ''Bubble Witch Saga'' establishing King as a viable developer in this arena, becoming the second-largest developer by daily player count on the Facebook platform by April 2012, trailing only Zynga.<ref name="informilo"/><ref name="latimes 2012"/>

''Candy Crush Saga'' was selected as King's next Facebook game based on the popularity of the portal version of ''Candy Crush''.<ref name="vb aug14">{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2014/08/18/lessons-from-a-game-guru-candy-crush-saga-creator-once-survived-six-months-without-pay/view-all/ | title = Lessons from a game guru: Candy Crush Saga creator once survived six months without pay | first = Dean | last = Takihashi | date = August 18, 2014 | access-date = October 20, 2016 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = October 29, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161029011503/http://venturebeat.com/2014/08/18/lessons-from-a-game-guru-candy-crush-saga-creator-once-survived-six-months-without-pay/view-all/ | url-status = live }}</ref> The basic cross-platform framework from ''Miner Speed'' were used to craft the foundation of ''Candy Crush Saga'', adding the "saga" elements from ''Bubble Witch Saga''.  Initial ideas to expand ''Candy Crush'' into ''Candy Crush Saga'' were proposed by Knutsson, around 2011, including making the saga map visually look like a [[board game]].<ref name="informilo"/><ref name="apple"/> The game was first released for Facebook in April 2012, at the time featuring only 65 levels.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.gamesindustry.biz/articles/2013-07-18-working-at-the-candy-store | title = Working at the Candy Store | first = Rachel | last = Weber | date = July 18, 2013 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[GamesIndustry.biz]] | archive-date = October 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161020045657/http://www.gamesindustry.biz/articles/2013-07-18-working-at-the-candy-store | url-status = live }}</ref> The game quickly gained popularity, gaining more than 4 million players within a few weeks of release.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.gamasutra.com/view/news/169403/Candy_Crush_Saga_highlighted_in_this_weeks_fastestgrowing_Facebook_games.php | title = Candy Crush Saga highlighted in this week's fastest-growing Facebook games | first = Eric | last = Caoili | date = May 1, 2012 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Gamasutra]] | archive-date = October 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161020051259/http://www.gamasutra.com/view/news/169403/Candy_Crush_Saga_highlighted_in_this_weeks_fastestgrowing_Facebook_games.php | url-status = dead }}</ref>

King later released mobile versions for [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] that same year, adding a feature that allowed mobile users to synchronize their progress with the Facebook version. Knutsson stated that at that time, with ''Candy Crush Saga'' as popular as it was on Facebook, they knew that they "had to get it right" in the transition process.<ref name="informilo"/> King had previously discussed the nature of games that kept their state between a PC and mobile version with Fabrication Games, believing this was a necessary trend in the future of gaming, Both recognized several of the difficulties that would have to be addressed to provide both the progress synchronization and gameplay interface between mouse-driven PC computers and touch-driven mobile devices.<ref name="vb aug14"/> King found that one issue with transiting ''Bubble Witch Saga'' to mobile was that the gameplay elements were too small for mobile devices, and aimed to correct that for ''Candy Crush Saga'' on mobile. The mobile release delay for ''Candy Crush Saga'' was in part due to adding the ability to play the mobile version in an offline mode that would still synchronize once the player returned online.<ref name="vb aug14"/>

The mobile version helped to boost the popularity of the game, attributed to the nature of the game being able to be played in a pick-up-and-go manner ideally suited for mobile devices. Tommy Palm, one of the four developers for ''Candy Crush Saga'', stated that the first weekend numbers after the game's mobile release were over ten times greater than the estimates they expected.<ref name="vb aug14"/> By January 2013, ''Candy Crush Saga'' overtook Zynga's ''[[FarmVille 2]]'' as the top-played game on the Facebook platform.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.polygon.com/2013/1/17/3885960/king-coms-candy-crush-saga-takes-the-top-spot-on-facebook-but-zynga | title = King.com's Candy Crush Saga takes the top spot on Facebook, but Zynga still dominates | first = Tracey | last = Lien | date = January 17, 2013 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] | archive-date = October 8, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20171008080256/https://www.polygon.com/2013/1/17/3885960/king-coms-candy-crush-saga-takes-the-top-spot-on-facebook-but-zynga | url-status = live }}</ref>

King had not planned for ''Candy Crush Saga'' to be as popular as it was, expecting the game to have only a six-month window after which players would move on to a different game, and thus had committed only minimal resources to its ongoing support at launch. Instead, with the game's popularity still high by the end of 2012, King became more serious about supporting the game for the long term, looking into deeper game mechanics, adding more levels, and other methods to extend the game.<ref name="apple"/> Since its release, ''Candy Crush Saga'' was expanded over the years by adding new episodes, each containing a number of new levels. This enabled King to also introduce new gameplay features alongside other game improvements.<ref name="informilo"/> New features were first tested on King's own portal to see how players there responded and allowed them to tweak these as needed, then push them into the episodes on the Facebook/mobile version.<ref name="nytimes">{{cite web | url = http://bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2013/07/02/candy-crush-saga-is-this-summers-sweet-treat/? | title = Candy Crush Saga Is This Summer's Sweet Treat | first = Jenna | last = Wortham | date = July 2, 2013 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[The New York Times]] | archive-date = October 14, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20171014183804/https://bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2013/07/02/candy-crush-saga-is-this-summers-sweet-treat/ | url-status = live }}</ref> By September 2016, King released its 2000th level for the game to celebrate the milestone of over 1 trillion ''Candy Crush Saga'' games having been played.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-37484114 | title = Candy Crush Saga: Life beyond level 2,000 | first = Leo | last = Kelion | date = September 28, 2016 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[BBC]] }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2016/09/28/candy-crush-saga-gets-its-2000th-level/ | title = Candy Crush Saga adds 2,000th level to celebrate 1,000,000,000,000 games played | first = Mike | last = Miniotti | date = September 28, 2016 | access-date = October 19, 2016 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = October 14, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161014212629/http://venturebeat.com/2016/09/28/candy-crush-saga-gets-its-2000th-level/ | url-status = live }}</ref> Starting around that time, with the game offered as a free-to-play model, King seeks to provide new content on a weekly or biweekly basis, including time-limited content. [[Riccardo Zacconi|Zacconi]] saw this approach as a means to keep players, who otherwise have not purchased anything, to keep coming back and playing the game.<ref name="vb nov2017"/>

In March 2025, ''Candy Crush Soda Saga'' was included in the 14.1 beta of [[Android Auto]], as part of the Android Auto testing program.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Holt |first=Kris |date=2025-03-28 |title=Beta test brings Candy Crush Soda Saga and Angry Birds 2 to Android Auto |url=https://www.engadget.com/transportation/beta-test-brings-candy-crush-soda-saga-and-angry-birds-2-to-android-auto-143021332.html |access-date=2025-04-01 |website=Engadget |language=en-US |archive-date=April 8, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250408171720/https://www.engadget.com/transportation/beta-test-brings-candy-crush-soda-saga-and-angry-birds-2-to-android-auto-143021332.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The game was already available on cars with [[Android Automotive]], and could be played on the 14.1 beta when the car was in park.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Schoon |first=Ben |date=2025-03-27 |title=Android Auto 14.1 starts rolling out in beta with support for Android games [Video] |url=https://9to5google.com/2025/03/27/android-auto-games-rolling-out-beta/ |access-date=2025-04-01 |website=9to5Google |language=en-US |archive-date=April 7, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250407134312/https://9to5google.com/2025/03/27/android-auto-games-rolling-out-beta/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Commercial===
By 2013, ''Candy Crush Saga'' had been downloaded more than 500 million times across Facebook, iOS, and Android devices. It was considered the most downloaded app from the Apple App Store,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theverge.com/2013/11/15/5107794/candy-crush-saga-500-million-downloads|title=Half a billion people have installed 'Candy Crush Saga'|last=Webster|first=Andrew|date=November 15, 2013|work=[[The Verge]]|publisher=[[Vox Media]]|access-date=November 18, 2013|archive-date=November 18, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131118060011/http://www.theverge.com/2013/11/15/5107794/candy-crush-saga-500-million-downloads|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.cnet.com/news/candy-crush-saga-tops-itunes-app-download-list-for-2013/ | title = Candy Crush Saga tops iTunes app download list for 2013 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | date = December 16, 2013 | work = [[CNet]] | first = Steven | last = Musil | archive-date = December 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161220191637/https://www.cnet.com/news/candy-crush-saga-tops-itunes-app-download-list-for-2013/ | url-status = live }}</ref> and had at least 6.7 million active users on a daily basis; the game had a daily revenue of $633,000 from the United States section of the [[App Store (iOS)|iOS App Store]] alone.<ref>{{cite web|author-first1=Joe |author-last1=White|url=http://appadvice.com/appnn/2013/07/freemium-app-candy-crush-saga-earns-a-record-breaking-633000-each-day|title=Freemium App Candy Crush Saga Earns A Record-Breaking $500 Each Day|publisher=AppAdvice|date=July 9, 2013|access-date=September 30, 2013|archive-date=September 25, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130925080900/http://appadvice.com/appnn/2013/07/freemium-app-candy-crush-saga-earns-a-record-breaking-633000-each-day|url-status=live}}</ref> By 2014, the game had over 245 million active players each month but has since dropped off, with that count falling to around 166 million by 2016.<ref name="gibiz">{{cite web | url = http://www.gamesindustry.biz/articles/2016-10-24-welcome-to-the-new-era-games-as-media | title = Welcome to the New Era: Games as Media | first = Joost | last = van Dreunen | date = October 24, 2016 | access-date = October 31, 2016 | work = [[GamesIndustry.biz]] | archive-date = November 1, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161101042028/http://www.gamesindustry.biz/articles/2016-10-24-welcome-to-the-new-era-games-as-media | url-status = live }}</ref> It again rose back to 293 million active monthly players by November 2017.<ref name=":0" />

Though initially released with advertising to help with revenue, King removed the advertising in 2013 and solely has earned money from the game in the form of in-app purchases.<ref name="gibiz"/> Only a small percentage of the player base has purchased in-game items, up to around 4%, but this has led to millions of dollars in monthly revenue for King.<ref name="gibiz"/> In 2014, ''Candy Crush Saga'' players spent over $1.33 billion on in-app purchases. This is a decline from the previous year since in the second half of 2013 players spent over $1.04 billion.<ref name="auto"/> By 2015, the monthly revenue was estimated at $120 million, but with declining players purchasing in-game items, down to 2% by 2016, that revenue has dropped to just over $53 million per month.<ref name="gibiz"/> Again, by the end of October 2018, the monthly revenue rose to $128 million.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/candy-crush-facts-statistics/|title=20 Amazing Candy Crush Facts and Statistics|date=2017-11-05|website=DMR|language=en-US|access-date=2019-01-03|archive-date=April 30, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190430044009/https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/candy-crush-facts-statistics/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Five years after its release on mobile, the ''Candy Crush Saga'' series has received over 2.73 billion downloads.<ref>{{cite news |title=King's Candy Crush franchise has cleared 2.73 billion downloads five years since launching on mobile |url=https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/66991/candy-crush-saga-fifth-anniversary/ |work=[[Pocket Gamer]] |date=November 14, 2017 |archive-date=September 6, 2022 |access-date=April 24, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220906003451/https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/66991/candy-crush-saga-fifth-anniversary/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Its revenue for the quarter ending September 2017 was $250 million, having gained significant improvement in year-to-year revenues from 2016.<ref name="vb nov2017">{{cite web | url = https://venturebeat.com/2017/11/17/candy-crush-saga-2-73-billion-downloads-in-five-years-and-still-counting/ | title = Candy Crush Saga: 2.73 billion downloads in five years and still counting | first = Dean | last = Takahashi | date = November 17, 2017 | access-date = November 17, 2017 | work = [[Venture Beat]] | archive-date = November 18, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20171118224614/https://venturebeat.com/2017/11/17/candy-crush-saga | url-status = live }}</ref> It remains one of the top gross-revenue earnings app for mobile in the four years leading up to 2017.<ref name="vb nov2017"/> By the end of July 2018, the total revenue earned by this game stood at $3.91 billion.<ref name=":0" />

''Candy Crush'' received particular mention in [[Hong Kong]] media, with reports that one in seven Hong Kong citizens plays the game.<ref>{{cite news|last=Buffa|first=Chris|title=Candy Crush Saga: Played By Every Seventh Person In Hong Kong Daily|url=http://www.modojo.com/features/2013-03-05-candy-crush-saga-played-by-every-seventh-person-in-hong-kong-daily|newspaper=Modojo|access-date=April 27, 2013|archive-date=July 6, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150706132251/http://www.modojo.com/features/2013-03-05-candy-crush-saga-played-by-every-seventh-person-in-hong-kong-daily|url-status=dead}}</ref>  In December 2013, King entered the Japanese market with a series of television commercials in [[Japan]], and by December 4 it had become the 23rd most downloaded game in Japan on Android devices and number 1 most downloaded from the App Store.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://venturebeat.com/2013/12/06/king-is-running-commercials-for-candy-crush-in-japan-and-theyre-working|title=King is running TV commercials for Candy Crush in Japan, and they're working|last=Grubb|first=Jeffrey|date=December 6, 2013|work=GamesBea|publisher=[[VentureBeat]]|access-date=December 7, 2013|archive-date=December 7, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131207131546/http://venturebeat.com/2013/12/06/king-is-running-commercials-for-candy-crush-in-japan-and-theyre-working/|url-status=live}}</ref> It is also the most popular game in the UK of all time.<ref name=":0" />

Starting in 2020, King has hosted an online and in-person tournament, called ''Candy Crush All Stars'', with a $1 million prize pool, of which $500,000 goes to the tournament winner.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jerez |first=Raissa |date=2025-03-24 |title=Candy Crush All Stars tournament is back with $1M prize pool up for grabs |url=https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/candy-crush-all-stars-tournament-is-back-with-1m-prize-pool-up-for-grabs-3168687/ |access-date=2025-04-01 |website=Dexerto |language=en |archive-date=March 24, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250324181848/https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/candy-crush-all-stars-tournament-is-back-with-1m-prize-pool-up-for-grabs-3168687/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2024, 15 million players competed, with in-person finals held in [[Los Angeles]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Takahashi |first=Dean |date=2025-03-20 |title=Global Candy Crush All Stars tournament returns with $1M prize pool |url=https://venturebeat.com/games/global-candy-crush-all-stars-tournament-returns-with-1m-prize-pool/ |access-date=2025-04-01 |website=VentureBeat |language=en-US |archive-date=April 7, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250407174038/https://venturebeat.com/games/global-candy-crush-all-stars-tournament-returns-with-1m-prize-pool/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Controversy==
King filed for applications for trademarks on the word "candy" in January 2013 with the [[United States Patent and Trademark Office]] (USPTO), which became publicly known in January 2014.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.polygon.com/2014/1/21/5329806/candy-crush-saga-developer-trademarks-candy | title = Candy Crush Saga developer trademarks 'candy' | first = Jenna | last = Pitcher | date = January 21, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] }}</ref> News of these pending trademarks raised concerns from other developers, who feared that King would use their trademark to intimidate smaller developers.<ref>{{Cite web | url = http://www.polygon.com/2014/1/21/5332560/what-kings-candy-trademark-really-means | title = What King's 'candy' trademark really means | first = Tracey | last = Lien | date = January 21, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] | archive-date = December 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161220173655/http://www.polygon.com/2014/1/21/5332560/what-kings-candy-trademark-really-means | url-status = live }}</ref> In response to this criticism, King decided not to pursue the trademark.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.gamespot.com/articles/candy-crush-dev-abandons-candy-trademark-in-us-after-controversy/1100-6417960/ | title = Candy Crush dev abandons "Candy" trademark in US after controversy | first = Eddie | last = Makuch | date = February 25, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[GameSpot]] | archive-date = July 3, 2015 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20150703063445/http://www.gamespot.com/articles/candy-crush-dev-abandons-candy-trademark-in-us-after-controversy/1100-6417960/ | url-status = live }}</ref>

The mobile game known as ''CandySwipe'' created in 2010, two years prior to the release of ''Candy Crush Saga'', had many similarities that its independent developer Albert Ransom noticed as ''Candy Crush Saga'' became more successful. Following news of the "candy" trademark, Ransom issued a statement in February 2014, claiming that King had intentionally copied elements from his own game including the app icon, the art for the candy pieces, and sound effects like the level-completion "Sweet!".<ref>{{cite web | url = https://kotaku.com/candy-crush-saga-is-ripping-me-off-says-indie-develope-1521728191 | title = Indie Creator of CandySwipe Fumes Over Candy Crush Saga Trademark Move | first = Owen | last = Good | date = February 12, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Kotaku]] | archive-date = December 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161220104115/https://kotaku.com/candy-crush-saga-is-ripping-me-off-says-indie-develope-1521728191 | url-status = live }}</ref> While details were not given, Ransom stated that he had "amicably" resolved the matter with King by April of that year.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.gamespot.com/articles/candy-crush-saga-settles-dispute-with-candyswipe-amicably/1100-6419093/ | title = Candy Crush Saga settles dispute with CandySwipe "amicably" | first = Emanual | last = Maiberg | date = April 19, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[GameSpot]] | archive-date = November 11, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161111165710/http://www.gamespot.com/articles/candy-crush-saga-settles-dispute-with-candyswipe-amicably/1100-6419093/ | url-status = live }}</ref>

During this same period, it was discovered that King had applied for a trademark on the word "saga", and they had taken action against Stoic, the developers of ''[[The Banner Saga]]''; King had attempted to block Stoic's registration of "The Banner Saga", and after the game released in January 2014, it issued a [[cease and desist]] letter to the developers (King's trademark application was still pending approval at this time).<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2014-06-25-king-vs-the-banner-saga | title = King vs The Banner Saga | first = Welsey | last = Yin-Poole | date = June 26, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Eurogamer]] | archive-date = December 20, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20161220114353/http://www.eurogamer.net/articles/2014-06-25-king-vs-the-banner-saga | url-status = live }}</ref> Similar to the ''CandySwipe'' situation, Stoic announced in April 2014 that the situation with King had been resolved with both sides coming to an agreement allowing Stoic to continue to use their name.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.polygon.com/2014/4/17/5624980/king-settles-trademark-disputes-with-the-banner-saga-developer | title = Candy Crush maker King settles trademark disputes with The Banner Saga developer | first = Tracey | last = Lien | date = April 17, 2014 | access-date = December 12, 2016 | work = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] | archive-date = April 19, 2014 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140419044111/http://www.polygon.com/2014/4/17/5624980/king-settles-trademark-disputes-with-the-banner-saga-developer | url-status = live }}</ref>

''Candy Crush Saga'' also received criticism when it struck a deal with [[Microsoft]] to automatically install/reinstall the game on all new computers with then latest [[Microsoft Windows|Windows operating system]] and already sold devices that had been upgraded to [[Windows 10 Home]],<ref>{{cite news|last=Walton|first=Mark|title=Humanity weeps as ''Candy Crush Saga'' comes preinstalled with Windows 10|url=https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2015/05/humanity-weeps-as-candy-crush-saga-comes-pre-installed-with-windows-10/|access-date=September 27, 2015|website=Ars Technica|agency=Condé Nast|date=May 15, 2015|archive-date=December 2, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171202064054/https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2015/05/humanity-weeps-as-candy-crush-saga-comes-pre-installed-with-windows-10/|url-status=live}}</ref> even before [[Acquisition of Activision Blizzard by Microsoft|Microsoft acquired King's parent company Activision Blizzard]] in October 2023.

''Candy Crush Saga'' is considered to be an [[video game addiction|addictive game]] as it uses a [[compulsion loop]] that provides pleasurable reinforcement the more one plays.<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2014/may/21/candy-crush-angry-birds-psychology-compulsive-casual-games-mobile-flappy-birds | title = My favourite waste of time: why Candy Crush and Angry Birds are so compulsive | first=  Keith | last =Stuary | date = May 21, 2014 | access-date = September 10, 2018 | work =[[The Guardian]] }}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine | url = https://business.time.com/2013/11/15/candy-crush-saga-the-science-behind-our-addiction/ | title = Candy Crush Saga: The Science Behind Our Addiction | first = Eliana | last = Dockterman | date = November 15, 2013 | access-date = September 10, 2018 | magazine = [[Time (magazine)|Time]] | archive-date = August 31, 2018 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20180831063712/http://business.time.com/2013/11/15/candy-crush-saga-the-science-behind-our-addiction/ | url-status = live }}</ref> The game was investigated by the [[United Kingdom|UK]] [[Office of Fair Trading]] concerning exploitative [[game mechanics]] with regards to younger users.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/anthonykosner/2014/01/20/candy-crush-saga-has-trademarked-candy-and-apples-app-store-is-helping-enforce-it/|title=Candy Crush Saga Has Trademarked Candy And Apple's App Store Is Helping Enforce It|work=[[Forbes]]|access-date=April 24, 2025|archive-date=January 19, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250119024203/https://www.forbes.com/sites/anthonykosner/2014/01/20/candy-crush-saga-has-trademarked-candy-and-apples-app-store-is-helping-enforce-it/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In September 2015, another sequel named ''Candy Crush Jelly Saga'' was soft-launched in various countries for the Android platform, followed by a worldwide release in January 2016. The game introduces jelly-filled squares. Matches made with candies occupying jelly-filled squares will generally cause all squares that were part of the match to become jelly-filled, with the goal of making all squares on the gameboard filled with jelly to complete the puzzle.
