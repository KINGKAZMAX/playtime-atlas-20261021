'''''Celeste''''' is a 2018 [[platform video game]] developed and published by [[Indie game|indie]] studio [[Maddy Makes Games]]. The player controls the player character [[Madeline (Celeste)|Madeline]], a young woman with [[anxiety]] and [[Depression (mood)|depression]], who endeavors to climb Celeste Mountain, a fictional version of [[Mount Celeste]]. During her climb, she encounters several characters, including a personification of her self-doubt nicknamed "Badeline", who attempts to stop her from reaching the mountain's summit.

Development of ''Celeste'' began in August 2015, when game developers [[Maddy Thorson]] and Noel Berry created a version of ''Celeste'' for the [[PICO-8]] during the course of a weekend; Thorson, who served as producer, and Berry, who served as the lead programmer, wanted to expand the PICO-8 version into a full game. Inspired in part by ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' (1988) and Thorson's own ''[[TowerFall]]'' (2013), the gameplay was designed to be minimal and to mirror the feeling of [[bouldering]]. ''Celeste'' was designed to be accessible, featuring [[game mechanics]] and an "Assist Mode" that make the game more forgiving and less challenging. To create a more "introspective" game, the developers added themes of [[self-forgiveness]] into the narrative, which grew to become intertwined with the gameplay. ''Celeste''{{'}}s [[Video game soundtrack|soundtrack]] was composed by [[Lena Raine]].

''Celeste'' released on January 25, 2018, for [[Linux]], [[macOS]], [[Nintendo Switch]], [[PlayStation 4]], and [[Windows]], releasing for [[Xbox One]] the following day. It was also released for [[Google Stadia]] in July 2020. Upon release, the game received widespread acclaim from critics. Reviewers praised the interplay of its narrative and gameplay, and lauded its player movement and controls. Critics also appreciated its approach to difficulty and accessibility, and its depictions of depression and anxiety. ''Celeste''{{'}}s music and its [[pixel art]] style was also praised.

Since its release, ''Celeste'' has been considered by some [[Video game journalism|video game journalists]]{{By whom|date=October 2026}} to be among the [[greatest video games of all time]], and it has garnered a dedicated [[fandom]] and active [[speedrunning]] and [[Video game modding|modding]] communities. On September 9, 2019, a free [[downloadable content]] (DLC) [[Expansion pack|expansion]] named ''Farewell'' was released, introducing a new [[Level (video games)|chapter]] to the game. The game's developers have rejected the possibility of a sequel to ''Celeste'', though they have created small-scale [[Spinoff (media)|spin-offs]]: ''Celeste 2: Lani's Trek'', a sequel to the original PICO-8 version, was released for ''Celeste''{{'}}s third anniversary in 2021; ''[[Celeste 64: Fragments of the Mountain]]'', a short sequel with [[3D platformer|three-dimensional gameplay]], was released for its sixth anniversary in 2024.

Madeline then enters the Celestial Resort, an abandoned hotel, and meets Oshiro. Oshiro, a ghost who owns the abandoned resort, struggles to leave his past behind and lives in denial.<ref name="EuroG" /><ref name="Grayson 2018">{{Cite web |last=Grayson |first=Nathan |date=April 16, 2018 |title=Celeste Taught Fans And Its Own Creator To Take Better Care Of Themselves |url=https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221117050311/https://kotaku.com/celeste-taught-fans-and-its-own-creator-to-take-better-1825305692 |archive-date=November 17, 2022 |access-date=December 6, 2022 |website=[[Kotaku]] |publisher=[[G/O Media]] |language=en}}</ref> He tries to convince Madeline to stay and improve the resort's poor condition. After she agrees, Badeline appears and taunts Oshiro, saying that Madeline only helped him to fuel her own ego. Believing Badeline, he becomes enraged and chases Madeline out of the resort. After navigating a windy ridge, Madeline reaches Theo at a gondola lift. Badeline halts the gondola's motion, causing Madeline to have a [[panic attack]]; Theo helps to calm her down. The two arrive at an ancient temple, where Theo gets trapped inside a crystal until Madeline frees him. After speaking to Theo, Madeline gains the confidence to confront Badeline and says she wants to abandon her. In retaliation, Badeline throws her down the mountain. Madeline then finds Granny, who suggests that she seek resolution instead of abandonment. Madeline then searches for Badeline, and after a chase, Madeline apologizes to her. The two reconcile and vow to climb together. After reaching the summit, they enjoy a strawberry pie with Granny, Theo, and Oshiro.

About a year later, Madeline meets with Granny to explore the mountain's core. The two go on to develop a connection to each other before Granny dies. ==Development==
===Conception===
[[File:CelesteClassic.png|thumb|The [[PICO-8]] prototype of ''Celeste'']]

The original version of ''Celeste'' was developed by [[Maddy Thorson]] and Noel Berry in August 2015 for the [[PICO-8]], a [[fantasy video game console]], in three days.<ref name=GSPico82/> Due in part to time limitations, Thorson and Berry wanted core gameplay to be minimal with additional mechanics to add complexity, for which they felt the idea of a character struggling to climb a mountain was fitting.<ref name="Berry 2018" /> The initial prototype was titled "''Everest''".{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=34:32–34:41}} They decided that the project would be a [[Single-player video game|single-player]] adventure from its initial conception, with lessons from the development of Thorson's previous game, ''[[TowerFall]]'' (2013), carried over into the game.<ref name="Berry 2018" /> After the development of the PICO-8 version, retroactively titled ''Celeste Classic'',<ref name="Robinson 2016" /> the team wanted to expand the game with additional development time and fewer restrictions.<ref name="Berry 2018" /> ''Celeste Classic'' was added to ''Celeste'' as a hidden [[minigame]].<ref name="GSPico82" /> ''Celeste'' was produced by Thorson, and Berry served as the lead programmer.<ref name="Paste" /> The development team underwent [[Crunch (video games)|crunch]] during the final months of development.{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=35:21–36:29}}

===Gameplay and accessibility===
[[File:Noel Berry and Maddy Thorson at ACMI, Melbourne (retouched) (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|Noel Berry (''left'') and [[Maddy Thorson]] (''right''), ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s lead [[video game programmer|programmer]] and [[video game producer|director]], respectively]]
Thorson and Berry cited multiple games as influencing ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s gameplay, including ''[[Metroid (video game)|Metroid]]'' (1986), ''[[Donkey Kong Country 2]]'' (1995), and ''[[Kero Blaster]]'' (2014).<ref name="Berry 2018" /> Thorson said ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' (1988) was the game that most inspired ''Celeste'', particularly for its approach to problem-solving.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|p=|loc=14:08–14:44}} ''[[Shacknews]]'' and ''[[Kill Screen]]'' said ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s level design was reminiscent of levels created by Thorson in ''[[Super Mario Maker]]'' (2015).<ref name="SN" /><ref name="Hudgins 2016" />  

Thorson wanted Madeline's [[Glossary of video game terms#moveset|moveset]] to be minimal while emulating the feeling of [[bouldering]], which inspired Madeline's limited stamina when climbing walls;{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=35:21–36:29}} the stamina system was further shaped by the importance of vertical space given by the mountain-climbing theme.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=2:44–3:12; 3:49–4:09; 1:47–1:58}} Due to the gameplay requiring more precise control, the developers chose not to use a pre-existing [[physics engine]] so they could predetermine outcomes for certain situations.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=9:58–10:57; 11:36–12:09}} The precise gameplay also influenced the art style, as the team felt that [[pixel art]] could more clearly convey information to the player, in addition to the team's familiarity with the style.{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=36:31–37:21}} The team frequently adjusted Madeline's attributes and redesigned levels.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=12:35–13:56}}

During the design phase, the team wanted ''Celeste'' to feel difficult yet fair to the player, and modified or removed levels or mechanics they deemed unfairly difficult.<ref name="Berry 2018" /> The team prioritized matching the player's intent over requiring near-perfect execution, though challenges later in the game require more precision.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=4:26–5:01}} Several mechanics contribute to this, including "[[coyote time]]", which allows players to jump for a small moment after leaving the ground, named after ''[[Looney Tunes]]'' character [[Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner|Wile E. Coyote]]'s ability to become [[Cartoon physics#Midair suspension|suspended in midair]] and only fall once made aware of the situation.<ref name="Clayton 2020" /><ref name="Berry 2018" /> Additional mechanics include the ability for a jump to be input slightly before touching the ground, a mechanic that simulates rounded corners to prevent the player from colliding with a corner,<ref name="O'Connor 2020" /> and one that allows players to jump off a wall while slightly in front of it rather than requiring direct contact.<ref name="Clayton 2020" /> The mechanics were designed to allow ''Celeste'' to be either hardcore or forgiving, depending on the player's preference.{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=6:19–6:57}} The game was further designed to make more difficult challenges have a more clear solution.<ref name="Kim 2018" />
 
Systems like strawberries, B-sides, and Assist Mode were designed to allow the player to customize the difficulty of ''Celeste''.<ref name="ViceAssist" /> Thorson said that the ability of Assist Mode to make the game less challenging served as a counterbalance to the optional content such as strawberries, which make it more challenging.{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=41:35–42:26}} The implementation of an accessibility mode was decided on after observing public discourse surrounding ''[[Cuphead]]'' (2017) that originated after many players felt that the game was too difficult or unforgiving. This mode was initially titled "Cheat Mode", but Thorson felt that the name was too judgmental and decided on "Assist Mode" as an alternative.<ref name="ViceAssist" /> She said the mode was added late into development,{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=41:35–42:26}} and Berry added that it took only "a couple of days' work" to develop,<ref name="Frank 2018" /> though balancing the game as a whole required extensive [[playtest]]ing.<ref name="Berry 2018" />

===Story and themes===
Thorson wrote ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s story.<ref name="Signor 2021" /> At the start of development, the team had little direction for the story,{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=39:00–39:21}} but wanted it to be more [[Introspection|introspective]] than ''TowerFall{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=39:00–39:21}}'' and were inspired by [[Studio Ghibli]] films such as ''[[Spirited Away]]'' (2001).{{Sfn|Brown|2019|loc=14:56–15:32}} The inspiration for ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s story came during development, when Thorson reached a "breaking point" where her depression and anxiety became too severe to be ignored. She wanted to explore those ideas in the game, but creating a narrative around her experiences was difficult because her struggles persisted during development.<ref name="Grayson 2018" /> When writing the story, the team did not consult mental health professionals and chose to write based only on their own experiences, making the story personal and relatable instead of representative of mental illness as a whole.<ref name="Grayson 2018" /> The developers were surprised by the large role played by the story in ''Celeste'', and their decision to delay the game was made to "do the story justice", properly connect it to the gameplay, and make Madeline's experiences in the narrative reflect the player's experiences in the gameplay.{{Sfn|Slate|2018|loc=39:31–40:21}}

The ending was undecided for most of development. Thorson initially knew she wanted Madeline to fall down the mountain before ultimately reaching the summit, but did not know how it would be executed or framed within the story as a whole. She later decided to show the player that Madeline's struggles were not "magically better" after she reached the summit, and that Madeline's attempt to rid herself of anxiety by forcing her 'other self' into submission was incorrect and that she instead must learn to exist alongside it.<ref name="Grayson 2018" /> After development, Thorson said that because she was "in the process of discovering her [[queer]]ness" during development, ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s story inadvertently had queer undertones.<ref name="Signor 2021" /> Thorson said that Madeline was a [[Trans woman|transgender woman]] in 2020 after the character appeared with a [[Transgender flag|transgender pride flag]] during the ending of ''Farewell'',<ref name="Jones 2019" /><ref name="Farewell-Flag" /> which she said was obvious in retrospect because Madeline's [[gender identity]] inadvertently reflected her own.<ref name="Transgender-confirmed=1" /><ref name="Transgender-confirmed-2" /> Thorson wrote that she likely would have written Madeline's story differently after she knew they were both trans, but that a lot of the story of ''Celeste'' reflected Thorson's journey through the struggle of understanding her own gender identity.<ref name="Transgender-confirmed-2" />

Specific sounds in the music of ''Celeste'' were designed to match certain themes. According to Raine, "In The Mirror" was one of the strangest tracks she has composed. Its [[synthesizer]]-heavy sequences, noted by Raine to be reminiscent of [[Vangelis]] and ''[[Blade Runner]]'' (1982), were designed to match the "cosmic horrors" experienced by Madeline. In "Resurrections", she created an "othering" sound that transitioned from the more simple melodic progression of "First Steps", and an "ethereal sound evocative of a space that didn't seem quite real", as the song would play during a [[lucid dream]] sequence.<ref name="OSV" /> "In the Mirror" includes vocals performed by Raine as Madeline's [[Intrapersonal communication|internal dialogue]], reversed due to the theming of mirrors.<ref name="OSV" /> When developing the song, she wanted there to be a sound nearly unintelligible and spoken in the background. She decided to record herself in a dark closet, attempting to vocalize what she related to in Madeline's struggles.<ref name="Goldner 2019" /> According to Raine, she tries to "inject some part" of herself into her musical projects.<ref name="OSV" />

Each character in ''Celeste'' is represented by their own instrument. Madeline is represented by a piano, Badeline is represented by a synthesizer, Theo is represented by guitar, and Oshiro is represented by a "[[theremin]]-like" synthesizer. Granny is an exception, as, according to Raine, "she embodies the power of the mountain and is a fairly [[Omnipresence|omnipresent]] figure". Badeline's theme, a [[Motif (music)|motif]] that first appears in "Resurrections" and later reappears in several other tracks, is written entirely in [[Key (music)|minor key]], in contrast to Madeline's, which is "primarily cheery" and only occasionally transitions into a minor key. In the song "Anxiety", Raine attempted to capture the feeling of a panic attack and made the piano theme representative of Madeline be "engulfed" by the synthesizer representative of Badeline.<ref name="OSV" />

===Fan community===
After release, ''Celeste'' developed a dedicated [[Fandom|fanbase]], including an active [[speedrunning]] community.<ref name="Klepek 2021">{{Cite web |last=Klepek |first=Patrick |date=February 18, 2021 |title=How 'Celeste' Found Success in Embracing Speedrunners Who Broke The Game |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-celeste-found-success-in-embracing-speedrunners-who-broke-the-game/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221117050313/https://www.vice.com/en/article/88a755/how-celeste-found-success-in-embracing-speedrunners-who-broke-the-game |archive-date=November 17, 2022 |access-date=December 15, 2022 |website=[[Vice (magazine)|Vice]] |language=en}}</ref> Certain ''Celeste'' players have created custom levels for the game using [[Video game modding|mods]].<ref name="Signor 2021" /><ref name="SJ" /> According to ''USgamer'', the community was "a huge part of ''Celeste''{{'}}s success".<ref name="McCarthy 2019b" /> ''Kotaku'' wrote that the game "attracted an audience that's undeniably queer" and that many queer members of the ''Celeste'' community felt "empowered" by the game because Madeline's [[character arc]] felt evocative of the struggles faced by the [[LGBTQ community]].<ref name="Signor 2021" />

Speedrunning was actively considered by the game's designers.<ref name="Klepek 2021" /> Himself a speedrunner, sound designer Kevin Regamey contributed to the game's accommodation of speedrunning, and noted that the development team wanted to make speedrunning more accessible to casual players.<ref name="McCarthy 2019b" /><ref name="Signor 2021" /> Several techniques discovered by speedrunners were formally implemented into the game in [[Patch (computing)|updates]]. According to Thorson, the team wanted to allow the best players to continually discover new tricks that can be used by less skilled players. Mechanics are adjusted to accommodate players who wish to perform advanced techniques, though it is often done subtly to preserve the feeling of "breaking" the game.<ref name="Klepek 2021" /> The development team conversed with members of the community to better accommodate speedrunning in certain updates.<ref name="Signor 2021" /> Members of ''Celeste''<nowiki/>'s speedrunning community generally appreciate the developers' involvement, and have praised how changes to the game avoid removing techniques used in speedruns.<ref name="Klepek 2021" /> Others feel that the act of speedrunning ''Celeste'' ties into the game's themes of perseverance.<ref name="Signor 2021" />

After the release of the ''Farewell'' expansion in 2019, queer symbols caused a debate over Madeline's gender identity among the ''Celeste'' fandom.<ref name="Jones 2019" /><ref name="Dale 2019" /> The epilogue shows a [[pride flag]] and transgender pride flag on Madeline's desk, and some fans argued that these signs, as well as plot points throughout the game, implied that Madeline was a trans woman.<ref name="Jones 2019" /><ref name="Farewell-Flag" /> Other fans disagreed, and one critic noted that the game's ambiguous presentation of Madeline's queerness enabled this debate.<ref name="Jones 2019" /> Thorson wrote a year later that Madeline was definitely trans, and apologized to fans who had felt hurt by the silence of the game's creators on the subject. Thorson said that realizing Madeline's identity had taken some time, especially as Thorson was figuring out her own gender identity, and the development team had debated how to represent this in the art. The team eventually realized that without explicitly stating that Madeline was trans, some fans would always continue to deny it.<ref name="Transgender-confirmed-2" />

==Legacy==
Before release, Berry said that a potential sequel to ''Celeste'' was not currently planned, and that the team had "told the story [they] wanted to tell".<ref name="Berry 2018" /> Ahead of ''Farewell''<nowiki/>'s release, Thorson said the team "really [does not] want to make a sequel" to ''Celeste'',<ref name="Marks 2019" /> adding that the team is unaware how they would be able to "do a sequel justice", and that they are "way more interested in making something new" than revisiting an old creation.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Blake |first=Vikki |date=September 7, 2019 |title=No, there are no plans to make a Celeste sequel |url=https://www.gamesradar.com/no-there-are-no-plans-to-make-a-celeste-sequel/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221117071103/https://www.gamesradar.com/no-there-are-no-plans-to-make-a-celeste-sequel/ |archive-date=November 17, 2022 |access-date=December 16, 2022 |website=[[GamesRadar+]] |publisher=[[Future plc]] |language=en}}</ref> In a [[Blog|blog post]] commemorating the game's fourth anniversary in 2022, Thorson said, "I do not want to make ''Celeste'' again and I do not want to be who I was when we made ''Celeste'', again", and that she would like to move forward with future projects.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Nightingale |first=Ed |date=January 26, 2022 |title=Celeste creator self-reflects on game's fourth anniversary |language=en-gb |work=[[Eurogamer]] |publisher=[[Gamer Network]] |url=https://www.eurogamer.net/celeste-creator-self-reflects-on-games-fourth-anniversary |url-status=live |access-date=December 16, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221215010219/https://www.eurogamer.net/celeste-creator-self-reflects-on-games-fourth-anniversary |archive-date=December 15, 2022}}</ref>
